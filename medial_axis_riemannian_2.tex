\begin{abstract}
We generalise Theorem~6 of Bia\l{}o\.zyt \cite{Bia} --- the
characterisation of the points reconstructible from the medial axis of a
closed set --- from Euclidean space to real hyperbolic space
$\mathbb{H}^{n}$, and set up the structural framework for general
Hadamard manifolds. For a graph-type closed set
$\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ we prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:main})
\[
  \Rx=(\Xhat\setminus\X)\ \cup\
  \bigcup\bigl\{\Hb{}\ \text{maximal free, horospherically defective}\bigr\},
\]
where a maximal free horoball is \emph{horospherically defective} when
its contact set is not convex inside the intrinsically flat horosphere
$\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$. The proof rests on two pillars. A curvature-free
\emph{reconstruction engine} --- a rigidity statement, a drift theorem
through the horofunction boundary, and an unconditional structure
trichotomy --- reduces reconstruction outside the hull to drift into a
maximal free horoball. A \emph{horospherical dictionary} special to
$\mathbb{H}^{n}$, whose horospheres are flat, then identifies the feet of
high centres with Euclidean nearest contacts, so that Motzkin's theorem
(a closed Euclidean set is convex iff Chebyshev) converts reconstruction
into non-convexity of the contact set. Sufficiency follows because a
non-convex contact set furnishes medial centres reconstructing the whole
horoball; necessity, for compact $\X$, from a deepest-direction shadow
lemma, and for non-compact $\X$ from an ascent-and-no-collapse analysis
of a \emph{moving} contact diagram whose asymptotic contacts are read at
finite height. Part~II records the genesis of the statement --- the
naive hull-based notion of \cite{R1} fails already for a unit circle in
$\mathbb{H}^{3}$ --- together with the worked examples and the
obstructions to leaving $\mathbb{H}^{n}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Bia\l{}o\.zyt's Theorem~6 \cite{Bia} describes, for a closed set
$\X\subseteq\R^{n}$, exactly which points can be recovered as centres of
maximal balls touching $\X$ --- the \emph{reconstructible set} $\Rx$ ---
in terms of the convex hull of $\X$ and the \emph{defective} supporting
half-spaces, those whose contact slice is non-convex. The companion note
\cite{R1} lifted the sufficiency half to Hadamard manifolds, replacing
half-spaces by maximal free horoballs and the convex hull by the horoball
hull $\Xhat$. Its Conjecture~5.1 proposed that, conversely, every
reconstructible point outside $\Xhat$ lies in a \emph{defective} horoball,
defectiveness being measured against $\Xhat$. This note settles the
necessity direction in $\mathbb{H}^{n}$, and in doing so corrects the
notion of defect.

\paragraph{The main theorem.} Throughout, a closed set is
\emph{graph-type} if, near every part of it that approaches a horosphere,
it is the graph of a locally semiconvex height deficit (the zero set of
the deficit being the contacts). All sets arising in practice --- smooth
or piecewise-smooth hypersurfaces, finite point sets, and their unions
--- are graph-type.

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction in $\mathbb{H}^{n}$]\label{thm:main}
Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ be closed and graph-type. Then
\[
  \Rx \;=\; (\Xhat\setminus\X)\ \cup\
  \bigcup\bigl\{\Hb{}\ \text{maximal free, horospherically defective}\bigr\},
\]
where a maximal free horoball $\Hb{}$ is \emph{horospherically defective}
if its contact set is not convex inside the flat horosphere
$\Sb{}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$ --- the asymptotic contacts being read
through the finite-height moving contact set of
\S\ref{sec:nec-noncompact}. For compact $\X$ this is
Corollary~\ref{cor:compactclosed}; the inclusion $\supseteq$ is
\S\ref{sec:suff-main} and $\subseteq$ is \S\ref{sec:nec-main}.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Architecture of the proof.} Two layers do the work, and
separating them is the organising idea of the note.

\emph{The reconstruction engine} (\S\ref{sec:inflation}) is purely
metric and curvature-free. Its centrepiece is a rigidity statement
(Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity}): one cannot inflate a medial ball along
a foot-geodesic, because a second foot cuts the inflation instantly. So
multiple contacts on a limit horosphere are never produced by a single
inflation --- they are produced by \emph{drift}, medial balls of
unbounded radius whose centres run off to the ideal boundary. A drift
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}), proved through the horofunction
boundary, shows such a drift converges to a maximal free horoball
carrying the limiting feet as contacts; and an unconditional structure
trichotomy (Theorem~\ref{thm:structure}) shows every point outside
$\Xhat$ either drifts or escapes along a single-contact ray. This reduces
the whole question to the geometry of a maximal free horoball and its
contacts.

\emph{The horospherical dictionary} (\S\ref{sec:dictionary}) is special
to $\mathbb{H}^{n}$, whose horospheres are intrinsically flat. Normalising
a maximal free horoball to $\{z>1\}$ in the upper half-space model, the
distance from a high centre $(\alpha,t)$ to a contact is monotone in the
\emph{Euclidean} distance from $\alpha$ to the contact's horospherical
position. Feet are therefore Euclidean nearest contacts, medial centres
are non-Chebyshev points, and Motzkin's theorem --- a closed Euclidean
set is convex iff every point has a unique nearest --- turns the entire
problem into the convexity of the contact set. Sufficiency
(\S\ref{sec:suff-main}) and necessity (\S\ref{sec:nec-main}) are then two
faces of this duality.

\paragraph{What is new, and the correction.} The hull-based defect of
\cite{R1} is inadequate: the horoball hull can collapse to $\X$ (already
for two points in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$), and in $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ a unit
circle on a horosphere reconstructs an entire region while every maximal
free horoball meets it in a connected --- hence non-split, non-defective
--- set (\S\ref{sec:dim3}). The operative condition is not a gap in the
contact set but its failure to be convex inside the flat horosphere; in
dimension two these coincide, which is why the phenomenon is invisible
there. For non-compact $\X$ even the convex hull of the attained contacts
is not enough: a single asymptotic ridge and two parallel ones share
every directional invariant yet reconstruct oppositely
(\S\ref{sec:genesis-offset}). The right reading is through a
\emph{moving} contact diagram, in which an asymptotic ray presents at
height $t$ as a contact at a moving Euclidean site; the diagram is
non-convex exactly when reconstruction occurs.

\paragraph{Organisation.} Part~I proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: the
engine (\S\ref{sec:inflation}), the dictionary (\S\ref{sec:dictionary}),
sufficiency (\S\ref{sec:suff-main}), necessity (\S\ref{sec:nec-main}),
and the assembly (\S\ref{sec:assembly}). Part~II is the record behind the
statement: the genesis through bitangency, splitness and the
dimension-three counterexample (\S\ref{sec:taxonomy}); the further results
and development notes, including the earlier necessity routes and the
boundary-envelope analysis (\S\ref{sec:devnotes}); the move toward general
Hadamard manifolds (\S\ref{sec:nonhyp}); and a status table
(\S\ref{sec:summary}). All numerical claims were checked directly; all
arguments were re-derived by hand.

We use one external input, as in \cite{R1}.

\begin{lemma}[Cut-locus density; Wolter, Albano \cite{Wol,Alb}]
\label{lem:cut}
Let $M$ be complete and $\X$ closed. The cut locus $\Cut(\X)$ --- the
set of endpoints of maximal minimizing segments of normal geodesics
from $\X$ --- satisfies $\Cut(\X)\subseteq\overline{\Mx}$.
\end{lemma}

\section*{Part I. The reconstruction theorem in $\mathbb{H}^{n}$}

\section{Setting and definitions}\label{sec:setting}

Throughout, $(M^{n},g)$ is a \emph{Hadamard manifold}: complete,
simply connected, of nonpositive sectional curvature. We write
$d(y,z)$ for the Riemannian distance. Every geodesic is globally
minimizing, extends to a complete geodesic line, and is determined by
a point and a velocity. $\X\subsetneq M$ is a fixed nonempty closed
set, $d(y):=\dist(y,\X)$, and
\[
m(y):=\{x\in\X \mid d(y,x)=d(y)\}
\]
is the nonempty compact set of \emph{feet} of $y$ (Hopf--Rinow). All
balls $\B(y,r)$ are open. A ball (or any set) is \emph{free} if it is
disjoint from $\X$; thus $\B(y,r)$ is free iff $r\le d(y)$.

\begin{definition}[Medial axis, reconstructible set]\label{def:medial}
The \emph{medial axis} is
$\Mx:=\{a\in\X^{c} : \#m(a)\ge 2\}$; on a Hadamard manifold this
coincides with the set of points admitting at least two distinct
minimizing geodesics to $\X$ (no branching, cf.\
\cite[Rem.~2.2]{R1}). The \emph{reconstructible set} is
\[
\Rx:=\bigcup_{a\in\Mx}\B\bigl(a,d(a)\bigr).
\]
For $p\in\Rx$ and $a\in\Mx$ with $p\in\B(a,d(a))$ we call
$\mu(a,p):=d(a)-d(p,a)>0$ the \emph{margin} of $p$ in the medial ball
$\B(a,d(a))$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Busemann function, maximal free horoball]
\label{def:busemann}
For a unit-speed geodesic ray $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M$ the
\emph{Busemann function} is
$b_{\gamma}(y):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\bigl(d(y,\gamma(t))-t\bigr)$; the
limit exists since the expression is non-increasing in $t$ and bounded
below by $-d(y,\gamma(0))$. It is $1$-Lipschitz, convex, and
$b_{\gamma}(\gamma(s))=-s$. Set $c_{\gamma}:=\inf_{\X}b_{\gamma}$.
When $c_{\gamma}$ is finite, the \emph{maximal free horoball} and its
\emph{horosphere} are
\[
\Hb{\gamma}:=\{b_{\gamma}<c_{\gamma}\},\qquad
\Sb{\gamma}:=\{b_{\gamma}=c_{\gamma}\}.
\]
$\Hb{\gamma}$ is open, convex, free, and maximal among free
sub-level sets of $b_{\gamma}$. The \emph{depth} of a point
$p\in\Hb{\gamma}$ is $c_{\gamma}-b_{\gamma}(p)>0$. The
\emph{horoball hull} is
$\Xhat:=M\setminus\bigcup\{\text{free open horoballs}\}
=\{y : b_{\gamma}(y)\ge c_{\gamma}\ \text{for every ray}\
\gamma\}$, a closed set containing $\X$.
\end{definition}

The correct notion of defect --- forced by the
dimension-three counterexample of \S\ref{sec:dim3} and motivated there
--- is intrinsic to the horosphere. Since a horosphere of a Hadamard
manifold is intrinsically flat, $\Sb{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$, its
contact set has an unambiguous flat convex hull.

\begin{definition}[Horospherical defect]\label{def:hdefect}
For a maximal free horoball $\Hb{\gamma}$ let $K_{\gamma}:=
\Sb{\gamma}\cap\X$ be its contact set and let
$\operatorname{hconv}_{\gamma}(K_{\gamma})$ be the closed convex hull
of $K_{\gamma}$ taken in the intrinsic flat metric of the horosphere
$\Sb{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$. Call $\Hb{\gamma}$
\emph{horospherically defective} if
$\operatorname{hconv}_{\gamma}(K_{\gamma})\neq K_{\gamma}$.
\end{definition}

\section{The reconstruction engine}\label{sec:inflation}

The first layer is metric and curvature-free: it holds
on any Hadamard manifold and reduces reconstruction outside the hull to
the geometry of a single maximal free horoball. We record the inflation
construction of \cite[Thm.~3.1]{R1} in the quantitative form we need,
then prove the rigidity that channels all multiplicity into drift, the
drift theorem, and the structure trichotomy.

We first record the inflation construction of \cite[Thm.~3.1]{R1} in
the quantitative form we need. Fix $a\in\X^{c}$, a foot $q\in m(a)$,
and let $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to M$ be the unit-speed ray with
$\gamma(0)=q$ and $\gamma(\rho)=a$, where $\rho:=d(a)$ (the geodesic
from $q$ through $a$, extended; on a Hadamard manifold the extension
is a globally minimizing ray). Define the set of \emph{free times}
$T:=\{t>0 : \B(\gamma(t),t)\cap\X=\emptyset\}$ and the
\emph{escape time}
\[
r(a,q):=\sup T\;\in\;[\rho,\infty].
\]

\begin{lemma}[Monotone free inflation]\label{lem:monotone}
$T$ is an interval containing $(0,\rho]$, and for $t'<t$ with
$t\in T$ one has $\B(\gamma(t'),t')\subseteq\B(\gamma(t),t)$.
Moreover for every $t\in T$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $d(\gamma(t))=t$, with $q\in m(\gamma(t))$;
\item if $p\in\B(a,\rho)$ with margin $\mu=\rho-d(p,a)$, then
$d(p,\gamma(t))\le t-\mu$ for all $t\ge\rho$, $t \in T$;
\item every $q'\in m(a)$ satisfies $d(q',\gamma(t))=t$; in
particular all feet of $a$ lie on $\partial\B(\gamma(t),t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $d(\gamma(t'),\gamma(t))=t-t'$ (geodesics minimize), for
$y\in\B(\gamma(t'),t')$ we get
$d(y,\gamma(t))\le d(y,\gamma(t'))+(t-t')<t$, proving the nesting and
that $T$ is an interval; $\rho\in T$ because $\B(a,\rho)$ is free.
(i): freeness gives $d(\gamma(t))\ge t$, while
$d(\gamma(t),q)=t$ gives $\le$ and exhibits $q$ as a foot. (ii):
$d(p,\gamma(t))\le d(p,a)+d(a,\gamma(t))=(\rho-\mu)+(t-\rho)$. (iii):
$d(q',\gamma(t))\le d(q',a)+(t-\rho)=\rho+(t-\rho)=t$ by the triangle
inequality, and $\ge t$ by freeness, since $q'\in\X$.
\end{proof}

Part~(iii) of Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone} looks like an
engine for producing horoballs with several contacts; in fact it is
self-limiting. Boundary-riding is an \emph{equality} in the triangle
inequality, and equalities in uniquely geodesic spaces force collinearity,
which destroys freeness at once. This is the rigidity that any necessity
mechanism must respect.

\begin{lemma}[Cut branch]\label{lem:cutbranch}
Suppose $r:=r(a,q)<\infty$. Then $c:=\gamma(r)$ satisfies $d(c)=r$,
the ball $\B(c,r)$ is free and contains $\B(a,\rho)$, and
$c\in\Cut(\X)\subseteq\overline{\Mx}$. Consequently, if
$p\in\B(a,\rho)$ has margin $\mu$, then for every
$\varepsilon\in(0,\mu/2)$ there exists $a'\in\Mx$ with
\[
d(a')\ge r-\varepsilon,\qquad
p\in\B\bigl(a',d(a')\bigr),\qquad
\mu(a',p)\ge\mu-2\varepsilon .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$d(\gamma(t))=t$ on $T$ and continuity of $d$ give $d(c)=r$. If
$y\in\B(c,r)$, pick $\eta>0$ with $d(y,c)<r-\eta$; for $t<r$ with
$r-t<\eta/2$,
$d(y,\gamma(t))\le d(y,c)+(r-t)<r-\eta+\eta/2<t$, so
$y\in\B(\gamma(t),t)$, which is free; hence $\B(c,r)$ is free, and it
contains each $\B(\gamma(t),t)\supseteq\B(a,\rho)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}. The segment $\gamma|_{[0,t]}$ minimizes
$d(\cdot,\X)$ for every $t\le r$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}(i), while
for $t>r$ freeness fails, so $d(\gamma(t))<t$ and the segment no
longer minimizes: $c$ is the cut point of $\X$ along $\gamma$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:cut} applies. Finally take $a'\in\Mx$ with
$d(a',c)<\varepsilon$: then $d(a')\ge d(c)-\varepsilon=r-\varepsilon$
and
$d(p,a')\le d(p,c)+\varepsilon\le(r-\mu)+\varepsilon$ using
Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}(ii) at $t\to r$; hence
$\mu(a',p)=d(a')-d(p,a')\ge\mu-2\varepsilon>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Instant cutting]\label{prop:rigidity}
Let $M$ be Hadamard and $a\in\Mx$. Then $r(a,q)=d(a)$ for every foot
$q\in m(a)$: the inflation of a medial ball along any of its
foot-geodesics is cut instantly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\rho:=d(a)$, pick a second foot $q'\neq q$, and suppose
$t\in T$ with $t>\rho$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}(iii),
$d(q',\gamma(t))=t=d(q',a)+d(a,\gamma(t))$: equality in the triangle
inequality, so the concatenation of the geodesic segments $[q',a]$
and $[a,\gamma(t)]$ has length $d(q',\gamma(t))$ and is therefore the
(unique) geodesic from $q'$ to $\gamma(t)$. In particular it passes
through $a$ with velocity $\dot\gamma(\rho)$, so its initial segment
$[q',a]$, reversed, is part of $\gamma$:
$q'=\gamma(\rho-d(q',a))=\gamma(0)=q$, contradicting $q'\neq q$.
Hence $T\subseteq(0,\rho]$ and $r(a,q)=\rho$.
\end{proof}

What survives of the escape mechanism is its
single-contact version, which by Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity} can only
fire at uniquely-footed centres; it supplies the third branch of the
structure theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Single-foot escape]\label{thm:branch-one}
Let $M$ be Hadamard, $\X$ closed, $a\in\X^{c}$, $q\in m(a)$, and let
$p\in\B(a,d(a))$ have margin $\mu>0$. If $r(a,q)=\infty$ --- which
forces $m(a)=\{q\}$ --- then with $\gamma$ the ray of
\S\ref{sec:inflation}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $c_{\gamma}=0$, attained at $q$: $\Hb{\gamma}=\{b_{\gamma}<0\}$
is a maximal free horoball with attained contact
$q\in \Sb{\gamma}\cap\X$;
\item $b_{\gamma}(p)\le-\mu$, so $p\in\Hb{\gamma}$ at depth at least
$\mu$;
\item $\Hb{\gamma}=\bigcup_{t>0}\B(\gamma(t),t)$, an increasing union
of free balls each containing $\B(a,d(a))$ for $t\ge d(a)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All balls $\B(\gamma(t),t)$, $t>0$, are free. For $x\in\X$ this
means $d(x,\gamma(t))\ge t$, hence $b_{\gamma}(x)\ge0$ and
$c_{\gamma}\ge0$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}(i),
$d(q,\gamma(t))=t$ for all $t$, so $b_{\gamma}(q)=0$; with $q\in\X$
this forces $c_{\gamma}=0$, attained at $q$, proving (a). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}(ii), $d(p,\gamma(t))\le t-\mu$ for all
$t\ge d(a)$, whence $b_{\gamma}(p)\le-\mu<0=c_{\gamma}$, proving (b).
For (c), the inclusion $\subseteq$ of the union follows from
$b_{\gamma}(y)\le d(y,\gamma(t))-t<0$ for $y\in\B(\gamma(t),t)$;
conversely if $b_{\gamma}(y)<0$ then $d(y,\gamma(t))<t$ for all large
$t$. The containment statement is Lemma~\ref{lem:monotone}.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity}, escaping inflations
cannot start from medial centres; the mechanism by which the medial axis
actually produces multi-contact horoballs is \emph{drift} --- medial
balls containing $p$ with radii tending to infinity and uniform margin,
as in the bisector family of a two-point set in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$. For
this branch we pass to the horofunction description of the boundary at
infinity. For $\delta>0$ set
\[
T_{\delta}(p):=\sup\bigl\{\,d(a) : a\in\Mx,\ \mu(a,p)\ge\delta\,\bigr\}
\in[0,\infty],\qquad \sup\emptyset:=0 .
\]

\begin{lemma}[Horofunction convergence; {\cite[II.8.13]{BH}}]
\label{lem:horofn}
Let $M$ be Hadamard, $o\in M$, and let $(a_{k})\subset M$ with
$d(o,a_{k})\to\infty$ and $a_{k}\to\xi\in\bdry M$ in the cone
topology. Then
$h_{k}(\cdot):=d(\cdot,a_{k})-d(o,a_{k})\longrightarrow
b_{\xi}(\cdot)$ uniformly on compact sets, where $b_{\xi}$ is the
Busemann function of the ray from $o$ to $\xi$, normalized by
$b_{\xi}(o)=0$. Moreover $\overline{M}=M\cup\bdry M$ is compact, so
every unbounded sequence subconverges to some $\xi\in\bdry M$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Limit horoball from unbounded medial radii]
\label{thm:branch-two}
Let $M$ be Hadamard, $\X$ closed, $p\in\X^{c}$, and suppose
$T_{\delta}(p)=\infty$ for some $\delta>0$. Then there exist
$\xi\in\bdry M$, $s_{\infty}\in[\delta,\,d(p)]$, medial centres
$a_{k}\in\Mx$ with $r_{k}:=d(a_{k})\to\infty$ and
$\mu(a_{k},p)\ge\delta$, such that, with $b_{\xi}$ normalized at
$o=p$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\X\subseteq\{b_{\xi}\ge s_{\infty}\}$ and
$b_{\xi}(p)=0\le s_{\infty}-\delta$; hence $c_{\xi}\ge
s_{\infty}>0=b_{\xi}(p)$ and $p$ lies in the maximal free horoball
$\Hb{\xi}=\{b_{\xi}<c_{\xi}\}$ at depth at least $\delta$;
\item the free balls exhaust the sub-horoball: every compact
$K\subseteq\{b_{\xi}<s_{\infty}\}$ satisfies
$K\subseteq\B(a_{k},r_{k})$ for all large $k$;
\item \emph{(contact capture)} if feet $q_{k}\in m(a_{k})$ have a
bounded subsequence, then along it $q_{k}\to q_{*}\in\X$ with
$b_{\xi}(q_{*})=s_{\infty}$; in that case $c_{\xi}=s_{\infty}$, the
infimum is attained at $q_{*}$, and $q_{*}\in \Sb{\xi}\cap\X$.
If two sequences of feet $q_{k}\neq q_{k}'$ of $a_{k}$ subconverge to
\emph{distinct} limits, $\Hb{\xi}$ is bitangent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose $a_{k}\in\Mx$ with $\mu(a_{k},p)\ge\delta$ and
$r_{k}=d(a_{k})\to\infty$. Set $t_{k}:=d(p,a_{k})\le r_{k}-\delta$
and $s_{k}:=r_{k}-t_{k}\ge\delta$. For any $x_{0}\in\X$, freeness of
$\B(a_{k},r_{k})$ gives $d(x_{0},a_{k})\ge r_{k}$, so
$d(x_{0},p)\ge d(x_{0},a_{k})-t_{k}\ge s_{k}$; hence
$s_{k}\le d(p)$, and after passing to a subsequence
$s_{k}\to s_{\infty}\in[\delta,d(p)]$. Also
$t_{k}\ge r_{k}-d(p)\to\infty$, so $(a_{k})$ is unbounded and, after a
further subsequence, $a_{k}\to\xi\in\bdry M$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:horofn}).

(a) For $x\in\X$: $h_{k}(x)=d(x,a_{k})-t_{k}\ge r_{k}-t_{k}=s_{k}$,
and letting $k\to\infty$, $b_{\xi}(x)\ge s_{\infty}$. Trivially
$b_{\xi}(p)=0$. Hence $c_{\xi}=\inf_{\X}b_{\xi}\ge
s_{\infty}\ge\delta$, while $c_{\xi}\le b_{\xi}(x_{0})<\infty$; the
maximal free horoball $\Hb{\xi}$ exists and contains $p$ at depth
$c_{\xi}-b_{\xi}(p)\ge\delta$.

(b) If $b_{\xi}(y)<s_{\infty}$ then, by locally uniform convergence
on a compact neighbourhood of $K$,
$h_{k}(y)<s_{k}$ for large $k$ uniformly on $K$, i.e.\
$d(y,a_{k})<t_{k}+s_{k}=r_{k}$.

(c) Along the bounded subsequence,
$h_{k}(q_{k})=d(q_{k},a_{k})-t_{k}=r_{k}-t_{k}=s_{k}$, and locally
uniform convergence plus $q_{k}\to q_{*}$ gives
$b_{\xi}(q_{*})=s_{\infty}$. Since $q_{*}\in\X$ (closedness) we get
$c_{\xi}\le s_{\infty}$, so $c_{\xi}=s_{\infty}$ is attained at
$q_{*}$. Two distinct such limits give two points of
$\Sb{\xi}\cap\X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:escape-feet}
Without the boundedness hypothesis in (c) the feet may escape to
infinity and the contact be only asymptotic
($c_{\xi}=s_{\infty}$ not attained, or attained at fewer points than
the medial multiplicity suggests). This is a genuine phenomenon
already in $\R^{2}$: for
$\X=\operatorname{graph}(e^{x})\cup\{(5,0)\}$ the medial centres
$(\bar x_{T},-R_{T})$ with feet $(5,0)$ and
$(-T,e^{-T})$ have $R_{T}\to\infty$, one foot converging to the
attained contact $(5,0)$ and the other escaping along the asymptote;
the limit half-plane $\{y<0\}$ is maximal free with a single attained
contact. See \S\ref{sec:taxonomy}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The structure trichotomy}\label{sec:structure}

The two branches above, with the hull, exhaust the
points outside $\X$ unconditionally.

\begin{theorem}[Structure trichotomy --- no residual case]
\label{thm:structure}
Let $M$ be Hadamard, $\X$ closed, and $p\in\X^{c}$ (reconstructible
or not). Then at least one of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
\item \textbf{(Hull)} $p\in\Xhat\setminus\X$.
\item \textbf{(Drift)} $T_{\delta}(p)=\infty$ for some $\delta>0$;
then $p$ lies, at depth $\ge\delta$, in a maximal free horoball
exhausted (below level $s_{\infty}$) by medial balls, whose attained
contacts are the subsequential limits of their feet
(Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}).
\item \textbf{(Ray escape)} There is $\delta'>0$ such that for all
sufficiently large $t$, $p$ lies at depth $\ge\delta'$ in a maximal
free horoball with an attained contact, produced by an escaping
single-foot inflation (Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-one}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume (I) fails; since $p\notin\X$, this means $p\notin\Xhat$.
Choose a ray $\gamma$ with $b_{\gamma}(p)<c_{\gamma}=:c$ (finite by
this very inequality) and set $\delta^{*}:=c-b_{\gamma}(p)>0$. For
$x\in\X$ the function $s\mapsto d(x,\gamma(s))-s$ is non-increasing
with limit $b_{\gamma}(x)\ge c$, so $d(x,\gamma(t))\ge t+c$ for every
$t$: the balls $\B(\gamma(t),t+c)$ are free, and
$d(\gamma(t))\ge t+c$. Since $d(p,\gamma(t))-t\downarrow
b_{\gamma}(p)=c-\delta^{*}$, there is $t_{0}$ with
$d(p,\gamma(t))\le t+c-\delta^{*}/2$ for $t\ge t_{0}$: $p$ lies in
the maximal free ball $\B(\gamma(t),d(\gamma(t)))$ with margin
$\mu_{t}\ge\delta^{*}/2$. For $t\ge t_{0}$ pick a foot
$q_{t}\in m(\gamma(t))$. If $r(\gamma(t),q_{t})<\infty$ for an
unbounded set of $t$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cutbranch} with
$\varepsilon=\delta^{*}/8$ supplies $a'_{t}\in\Mx$ with
$d(a'_{t})\ge d(\gamma(t))-\delta^{*}/8\ge t+c-\delta^{*}/8$ and
$\mu(a'_{t},p)\ge\delta^{*}/4$, so $T_{\delta^{*}/4}(p)=\infty$:
case (II). Otherwise $r(\gamma(t),q_{t})=\infty$ for all large $t$,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-one} (applied to $a=\gamma(t)$, margin
$\delta^{*}/2$) gives case (III) with $\delta'=\delta^{*}/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The trichotomy is unconditional and does not require $p\in\Rx$:
reconstructibility enters only when the horoballs delivered by drift or
ray-escape are upgraded to defective ones, which is the content of the
horospherical dictionary below. It removes the ``confinement'' case
conjectured in an earlier draft.
\end{remark}

In Euclidean space the drift branch always carries reconstruction outside the hull, by lifting the defect witness of \cite{Notes}.

\begin{proposition}[In $\R^{n}$, reconstruction outside the hull
forces drift]\label{prop:euclidean}
For $M=\R^{n}$ and $p\in\Rx\setminus\cconv\X$, case (II) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:structure} holds: $T_{\delta}(p)=\infty$ for some
$\delta>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the repaired necessity direction of Theorem~6 \cite[\S4.6]{Notes}
there are a unit vector $v$, $c=\sup_{\X}\sca{v}{\cdot}$, and a
defect witness
$w\in(\cconv\X\cap\{\sca{v}{\cdot}=c\})\setminus\X$ with
$h:=\sca{v}{p}-c>0$. Lift the \emph{witness}: $w_{t}:=w+tv$. Then
$d(w_{t})\ge\sca{v}{w_{t}}-c=t$ since
$\X\subseteq\{\sca{v}{\cdot}\le c\}$, while
$|p-w_{t}|^{2}=t^{2}-2th+|p-w|^{2}$ gives
$|p-w_{t}|\le t-h+O(1/t)$: for all large $t$, $p$ lies in
$\B(w_{t},d(w_{t}))$ with margin $\mu_{t}\ge h/2$.

No inflation from $w_{t}$ escapes. Indeed, suppose
$r(w_{t},q_{t})=\infty$ for a foot $q_{t}\in m(w_{t})$, and let
$u_{t}$ be the direction of the inflation ray, so
$q_{t}=w_{t}-\rho_{t}u_{t}$ with $\rho_{t}:=d(w_{t})$. Freeness of
all balls $\B(\gamma_{t}(s),s)$ gives, for every $x\in\X$,
$\sca{x-q_{t}}{u_{t}}\le0$; the same then holds for every point of
$\cconv\X$, in particular for $w$. Substituting
$w-q_{t}=\rho_{t}u_{t}-tv$:
\[
0\;\ge\;\sca{w-q_{t}}{u_{t}}\;=\;\rho_{t}-t\sca{v}{u_{t}}
\;\ge\;\rho_{t}-t\;\ge\;0 ,
\]
forcing equality throughout: $\sca{v}{u_{t}}=1$, hence $u_{t}=v$,
$\rho_{t}=t$, and $q_{t}=w_{t}-tv=w\notin\X$ --- contradicting
$q_{t}\in\X$.

Hence $r(w_{t},q_{t})<\infty$ for every $t$ large, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:cutbranch} with $\varepsilon=h/8$ supplies
$a'_{t}\in\Mx$ with $d(a'_{t})\ge d(w_{t})-h/8\ge t-h/8$ and
$\mu(a'_{t},p)\ge h/4$. Thus $T_{h/4}(p)=\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strengthened Euclidean necessity]
\label{cor:euclidean}
For closed $\X\subseteq\R^{n}$, every $p\in\Rx\setminus\cconv\X$ lies
at positive depth in a maximal free half-space $L^{+}$ that is a limit
of medial balls in the sense of
Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}(b); the attained contacts of $\partial
L^{+}$ are the subsequential limits of their feet. In particular the
union in Theorem~6 of \cite{Bia} may be taken over maximal free
half-spaces arising as limits of medial balls.
\end{corollary}

\section{The horospherical dictionary in $\mathbb{H}^{n}$}\label{sec:dictionary}

The second layer is special to real hyperbolic space,
whose horospheres are intrinsically flat. It converts the geometry of a
maximal free horoball into Euclidean convexity through a classical
theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Motzkin \cite{Mot}]\label{thm:motzkin}
A non-empty closed set $C\subseteq\mathbb{E}^{N}$ is convex if and only
if it is \emph{Chebyshev}: every point of $\mathbb{E}^{N}$ has a unique
nearest point in $C$. Equivalently, $C$ is non-convex iff some point
has at least two nearest points in $C$.
\end{theorem}

We use the upper half-space model
$\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{(\xi,z):\xi\in\R^{n-1},z>0\}$ with
$\cosh d(p,q)=1+\lvert p-q\rvert^{2}/(2z_{p}z_{q})$. Isometries act
transitively on horoballs and restrict to Euclidean similarities of
the flat horosphere (preserving convexity), so for any maximal free
horoball it costs nothing to assume
\[
\Hb{}=\{z>1\},\quad \Sb{}=\{z=1\}\cong\R^{n-1},\quad
\X\subseteq\{z\le1\},\quad K=\X\cap\{z=1\}.
\]
For $a=(\alpha,t)$ and a contact $k=(\kappa,1)$,
$\cosh d(a,k)=1+\frac{\lvert\alpha-\kappa\rvert^{2}+(t-1)^{2}}{2t}$ is
strictly increasing in the \emph{Euclidean} position distance
$\lvert\alpha-\kappa\rvert$; so among contacts the feet of $a$ are the
Euclidean-nearest elements of $K$, and the medial directions are the
non-Chebyshev points of $K$.

Two structural facts about feet, used in both directions,
hold once we are in this picture. First, the feet of a drift ascend to
the horosphere; second, over a graph contact they never collapse to a
single point. Both follow from the leading-order shape of $\cosh d$ at a
high centre.

\begin{lemma}[Ascent of all feet]\label{lem:ascent2}
Let $\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$ be the maximal free horoball in direction
$\infty$, so $\sup_{\X}z=1$. Let $a_{k}=(\alpha_{k},t_{k})$ satisfy
$t_{k}\to\infty$ and $\lvert\alpha_{k}\rvert=o(t_{k})$ (i.e.\
$a_{k}\to\infty$ in the cone topology --- automatic for a drift toward
$\infty$). Then every foot $q_{k}=(\beta_{k},s_{k})\in m(a_{k})$ has
$s_{k}\to1$, whether $\beta_{k}$ stays bounded or escapes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and, using $\sup_{\X}z=1$, a point
$y_{\varepsilon}=(\nu_{\varepsilon},w_{\varepsilon})\in\X$ with
$w_{\varepsilon}>1-\varepsilon$ (position $\nu_{\varepsilon}$ fixed).
As $q_{k}$ is nearest, $\cosh d(a_{k},q_{k})\le\cosh d(a_{k},
y_{\varepsilon})$; dropping the non-negative position term on the left
and clearing $2t_{k}$,
\[
\frac{(t_{k}-s_{k})^{2}}{s_{k}}\ \le\
\frac{\lvert\alpha_{k}-\nu_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}+(t_{k}-w_{\varepsilon})
^{2}}{w_{\varepsilon}} .
\]
Here $\lvert\alpha_{k}-\nu_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}=o(t_{k}^{2})$
(as $\lvert\alpha_{k}\rvert=o(t_{k})$) and
$(t_{k}-s_{k})^{2},(t_{k}-w_{\varepsilon})^{2}=t_{k}^{2}(1+o(1))$;
dividing by $t_{k}^{2}$ gives $\frac{1+o(1)}{s_{k}}\le\frac{1+o(1)}
{w_{\varepsilon}}$, so $\liminf_{k}s_{k}\ge w_{\varepsilon}>
1-\varepsilon$. As $\varepsilon$ was arbitrary and $s_{k}\le1$,
$s_{k}\to1$.
\end{proof}

The companion fact --- that two feet over a graph
contact stay apart --- is the engine of necessity and removes the
tangential-bulk obstruction in sufficiency.

\begin{theorem}[No collapse at a graph contact]\label{thm:nocollapse}
Suppose near every contact the set $\X$ is a graph
$\{(\chi,1-\phi(\chi))\}$ with $\phi\ge0$ locally semiconvex and
$\{\phi=0\}$ the contact positions. Then no high centre has two feet
collapsing to a single point: for $a=(\alpha,t)$ the feet near a
contact are isolated minimisers of
$F_{t}(\chi)=\lvert\alpha-\chi\rvert^{2}+t^{2}\phi(\chi)$, and as
$t\to\infty$ each converges to a \emph{distinct} zero of $\phi$, i.e.\
a distinct contact. In particular the collapse alternative of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonmerge} is vacuous for graph-type $\X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $\cosh d(a,(\chi,1-\phi))=1+\frac{\lvert\alpha-\chi\rvert^{2}
+(t-1+\phi)^{2}}{2t(1-\phi)}$, minimising over $\chi$ is, to leading
order for large $t$, minimising
$F_{t}(\chi)=\lvert\alpha-\chi\rvert^{2}+t^{2}\phi(\chi)$
(\S\ref{thm:nocollapse} expansion). \emph{If $\phi$ is convex} on a
neighbourhood, $F_{t}$ is the sum of the strictly convex
$\lvert\alpha-\chi\rvert^{2}$ and the convex $t^{2}\phi$, hence
strictly convex with a \emph{unique} minimiser --- one foot, even
when $\operatorname{Hess}\phi(\kappa_{\ast})=0$ degenerates (the
quartic $\phi=\lvert\chi\rvert^{4}$ included). \emph{If $\phi$ is not
convex}, its zero set --- the contacts --- consists of separated
points (a touching set with a gap), and the minimisers of $F_{t}$
localise, as $t\to\infty$, to these separated zeros: two feet sit over
two \emph{distinct} contacts at a fixed separation. Either way feet
never coalesce to one point.
\end{proof}

\section{Sufficiency: a defective horoball reconstructs its interior}\label{sec:suff-main}

We prove the inclusion $\supseteq$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: a horospherically defective maximal free horoball
lies in $\Rx$. The mechanism is uniform --- a non-Chebyshev point of the
contact set is a medial direction at every height, and high enough
centres sweep the whole horoball. We give it first when $\X$ lies on the
horosphere (where it is sharp), then when the contacts are isolated from
the bulk, then for compact $\X$ in general.

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction on a horosphere]\label{thm:hsphere}
Let $\X$ lie on a horosphere of $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ (so $K=\X$). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item if $\X$ is not convex in $\Sb{}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$, the entire
horoball is reconstructed, $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$;
\item if $\X$ is convex, then $\Mx=\varnothing$ and
$\Rx=\varnothing$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every point of $\X$ has height $1$, so for any $a=(\alpha,t)$ the feet
$m(a)$ are exactly the Euclidean-nearest positions of $K=\X$ to
$\alpha$, for every $t>0$.

(b) If $\X$ is convex, Motzkin gives a unique nearest position for
each $\alpha$, so every $a$ has a single foot: $\Mx=\varnothing$.

(a) If $\X$ is non-convex, Motzkin gives $\alpha^{\ast}$ with two
distinct nearest contacts $k_{1},k_{2}$ at position distance
$\rho:=\rho_{K}(\alpha^{\ast})>0$. Then $a_{t}:=(\alpha^{\ast},t)$ has
$k_{1},k_{2}\in m(a_{t})$ for every $t>0$: it is medial, with
$\cosh d(a_{t})=1+\frac{\rho^{2}+(t-1)^{2}}{2t}$. For
$p=(\pi,z)\in\Hb{}$ (so $z>1$),
$p\in\B(a_{t},d(a_{t}))$ iff $\cosh d(p,a_{t})<\cosh d(a_{t})$, which
after clearing denominators and cancelling $-2zt$ reads
\[
(1-z)\,t^{2}\;+\;\bigl(\lvert\pi-\alpha^{\ast}\rvert^{2}+z^{2}-z\rho^{2}
-z\bigr)\;<\;0 .
\]
Since $z>1$ the leading coefficient $1-z$ is negative, so the
inequality holds for all $t$ large enough: $p\in\Rx$. As $p\in\Hb{}$
was arbitrary, $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Reconstruction, isolated contacts]\label{thm:hiso}
Let $\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$ be maximal free in $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ with contact
set $K$, and suppose the contacts are \emph{isolated from the bulk}:
$\X\cap\{z>1-\nu\}=K$ for some $\nu>0$. If $K$ is non-convex in
$\Sb{}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$ (i.e.\ $\Hb{}$ is horospherically
defective), then $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $\alpha^{\ast}$ and $k_{1},k_{2}$, $\rho$ as before (Motzkin on
the closed set $K$). For $x=(\chi,s)\in\X$ with $s\le1-\nu$,
\[
\cosh d(a_{t},x)-\cosh d(a_{t},k_{1})\ \ge\
\frac{(t-s)^{2}}{2ts}-\frac{\rho^{2}+(t-1)^{2}}{2t}
=\frac{(1-s)(t^{2}-s)-s\rho^{2}}{2ts},
\]
and $(1-s)(t^{2}-s)-s\rho^{2}\ge\nu(t^{2}-1)-\rho^{2}>0$ once
$t>\sqrt{1+\rho^{2}/\nu}$ --- a bound independent of $x$. So beyond
this $t$ no bulk point beats the nearest contacts, $m(a_{t})\subseteq
K$ consists of the (two or more) Euclidean-nearest contacts, and
$a_{t}$ is medial. The reconstruction inequality of
Theorem~\ref{thm:hsphere}(a) is unchanged (it used only the contacts,
at height $1$), so $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sufficiency for compact sets]\label{thm:suffcompact}
Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ be compact and graph-type at its
contacts. Then every horospherically defective maximal free horoball
$\Hb{}$ satisfies $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalise $\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$; $K$ is non-convex, so by Motzkin it has a
non-Chebyshev point. The feet of a high centre $a=(\alpha,t)$ are, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse}, one isolated minimiser of $F_{t}$ per
local contact cluster --- the tangential bulk that shadowed a
\emph{fixed} direction in Remark~\ref{rem:hsuff}(i) merely shifts each
minimiser slightly, without creating or destroying one. Hence the
nearest-foot map is single-valued off a separating set, and as
$\alpha$ crosses from the basin of one contact cluster to another the
two competing nearest distances exchange order; by continuity there is
$\alpha^{\ast}$ with two equidistant nearest feet --- a medial high
centre. Running this for $t\to\infty$, the reconstruction inequality
of Theorem~\ref{thm:hsphere}(a) (which used only that the two feet sit
at height $\to1$) gives $p\in\B(a,d(a))$ for every $p\in\Hb{}$ once
$t$ is large. So $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{proof}

\section{Necessity: a reconstructible point sits in a defective horoball}\label{sec:nec-main}

We prove the inclusion $\subseteq$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. For compact $\X$ a single first-order argument at
the deepest direction does it, with no drift analysis at all. For
non-compact $\X$ the contacts can recede to the ideal boundary, and the
right bookkeeping is a moving Euclidean diagram.

\subsection{The compact case: the deepest-shadow lemma}\label{sec:kernel}

Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ be compact,
$p\notin\Xhat$, and $D(\xi):=c_{\xi}-b_{\xi}(p)$ the depth of $p$ in the
maximal free horoball at the ideal point $\xi$. As $D$ is upper
semicontinuous on the compact $\bdry\mathbb{H}^{n}$ and positive
somewhere, it attains its maximum $\delta^{\star}=D(\xi^{\star})>0$.
Normalise $\xi^{\star}=\infty$, so $\Hb{}^{\star}=\{z>1\}$,
$p=(\pi,z_{p})$ with $z_{p}=e^{\delta^{\star}}$, and
$K^{\star}=\X\cap\{z=1\}$. The deepest horoball is already defective.

\begin{lemma}[Deepest shadow]\label{lem:deepshadow}
$\pi\in\operatorname{conv}(K^{\star})$ in $\R^{n-1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose not: a unit $u\in\R^{n-1}$ separates,
$\langle u,\kappa\rangle\le\langle u,\pi\rangle-\varepsilon$ for all
$\kappa\in K^{\star}$ and some $\varepsilon>0$. Tilt the direction
from $\infty$ toward the finite ideal point $Ru$ and let $R\to\infty$.
A normalised computation of the Busemann function gives, uniformly on
compacta,
\[
b_{Ru}(y)=b_{\infty}(y)-\tfrac{2}{R}\langle y',u\rangle+O(R^{-2}),
\qquad y=(y',y_{z}),\ b_{\infty}=-\log y_{z}.
\]
Hence $D(Ru)=\inf_{x\in\X}\bigl[(b_{\infty}(x)-b_{\infty}(p))-
\tfrac{2}{R}\langle x'-\pi,u\rangle\bigr]+O(R^{-2})$. Split $\X$ at a
small height threshold $\rho>0$. On contacts and near-contacts
($b_{\infty}(x)<\rho$, height $>e^{-\rho}$): by compactness their
positions cluster on $K^{\star}$ as $\rho\to0$, so
$\langle x'-\pi,u\rangle\le-\varepsilon/2$, and the bracket is
$\ge\delta^{\star}+\varepsilon/R$. On the bulk ($b_{\infty}(x)\ge\rho$,
positions bounded by $\operatorname{diam}\X=:C$): the bracket is
$\ge\delta^{\star}+\rho-2C/R\ge\delta^{\star}+\rho/2$ for large $R$.
Thus $D(Ru)\ge\delta^{\star}+\min(\varepsilon/R,\rho/2)>\delta^{\star}$,
contradicting the maximality of $\xi^{\star}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Screening]\label{lem:screening}
If $\pi\in K^{\star}$, then $(\pi,1)$ is the unique nearest point of
$\X$ to $p$, and $p\notin\Rx$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $x=(\chi,s)\in\X$, $s\le1$,
$\cosh d(p,x)-1=\frac{\lvert\pi-\chi\rvert^{2}+(z_{p}-s)^{2}}{2z_{p}s}
\ge\frac{(z_{p}-s)^{2}}{2z_{p}s}$, and $g(s)=(z_{p}-s)^{2}/s$ is
strictly decreasing on $(0,z_{p})$, so for $s<1$ it exceeds
$g(1)=(z_{p}-1)^{2}$; for $s=1$ it equals $\lvert\pi-\chi\rvert^{2}+
(z_{p}-1)^{2}$, minimised only at $\chi=\pi$. So $(\pi,1)\in K^{\star}
\subseteq\X$ is the unique nearest point. Then $d$ is differentiable
at $p$ with $\nabla d(p)$ the upward vertical, and the entire vertical
ray $\sigma(\tau)=(\pi,z_{p}e^{\tau})$ keeps $(\pi,1)$ as unique
nearest, with $d(\sigma(\tau))=\delta^{\star}+\tau\to\infty$: a
steepest-ascent trajectory of $d$ with a single foot, escaping to
infinity. By the gradient-flow characterisation of medial-axis
reconstruction \cite{Lie} (valid for distance functions to closed sets
in Hadamard manifolds via the semiconcavity of $d$ \cite{ACa}), such a
point lies in no medial ball: $p\notin\Rx$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Necessity for compact sets]\label{thm:kernelcompact}
Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ be compact and $p\in\Rx\setminus\Xhat$.
Then the deepest free horoball $\Hb{}^{\star}$ is horospherically
defective and contains $p$. Necessity holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:deepshadow}, $\pi\in\operatorname{conv}(K^{\star})$.
Were $K^{\star}$ convex, $\operatorname{conv}(K^{\star})=K^{\star}$, so
$\pi\in K^{\star}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:screening} gives $p\notin\Rx$
--- contrary to hypothesis. So $K^{\star}$ is non-convex:
$\Hb{}^{\star}$ is horospherically defective, and contains $p$ as
$\delta^{\star}>0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The non-compact case: the moving contact diagram}\label{sec:nec-noncompact}\label{sec:noncompact3}

When $\X$ is non-compact the feet of a drift may
escape to the ideal boundary, so the contact set $K^{\star}$ undercounts.
The missing data is the perpendicular \emph{offset} of each asymptotic
ray (Example~\ref{ex:parallel} in Part~II shows the direction alone is
insufficient), and the right object is a contact diagram whose asymptotic
sites \emph{move} with the height.

\begin{definition}[Asymptotic rays and the offset set]\label{def:asymray}
Fix a direction $\eta\in\partial_{\infty}\Sb{}=S^{n-2}$ and write
$\eta^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-2}$. The \emph{offset set} of $\X$ in
direction $\eta$ is
\[
B_{\eta}:=\Bigl\{b\in\eta^{\perp}\ :\ \exists\,x_{k}=(\beta_{k},s_{k})
\in\X,\ \langle\beta_{k},\eta\rangle\to+\infty,\ \beta_{k}-\langle
\beta_{k},\eta\rangle\eta\to b,\ s_{k}\to1\Bigr\},
\]
the perpendicular positions of the lines along which $\X$ asymptotes to
the horosphere in direction $\eta$. A maximal free horoball is
\emph{ray-defective in direction $\eta$} if $B_{\eta}$ is non-convex in
$\eta^{\perp}$. The full asymptotic-contact datum is $K^{\star}$
together with the family $(B_{\eta})_{\eta}$ --- points and oriented
lines of $\Sb{}$.
\end{definition}

We treat first the single direction with a common
profile, where the offset reduction is exact. Suppose $\X$ approaches the
horosphere only in direction $\eta=e_{1}$, along ray-graphs of a common
profile,
\[
\ell_{b}=\{(\chi\,\eta+b,\,1-\psi(\chi)):\chi\ge\chi_{0}\}\subseteq\X
\quad(b\in B_{\eta}),\qquad
\X\setminus\{z>1-\nu\}\subseteq\{z\le1-\nu\},
\]
with $\psi$ smooth, positive, convex, decreasing to $0$ (e.g.\
$\psi=\operatorname{sech}$), and write a centre as $a=(a_{1}\eta+a',t)$,
$a'\in\eta^{\perp}$.

\begin{lemma}[Feet see only the offset]\label{lem:footoffset}
For bounded $a_{1},a'$ and $t\to\infty$,
\[
\cosh d(a,\ell_{b})=\Phi(a_{1},t)+\frac{\lvert a'-b\rvert^{2}}{2t}
\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),
\]
where $\Phi(a_{1},t)=\min_{\chi}\frac{(a_{1}-\chi)^{2}+(t-1+\psi(\chi))
^{2}}{2t(1-\psi(\chi))}+1$ is independent of the offset $b$ and of the
ray. Hence for two rays $d(a,\ell_{b_{1}})\lessgtr d(a,\ell_{b_{2}})$
according as $\lvert a'-b_{1}\rvert\lessgtr\lvert a'-b_{2}\rvert$, and
$a$ is medial through them iff $\lvert a'-b_{1}\rvert=\lvert a'-b_{2}
\rvert$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The offset enters $\cosh d(a,\ell_{b})=\min_{\chi}\frac{N(\chi)+\lvert
a'-b\rvert^{2}}{2t(1-\psi(\chi))}+1$ only through the constant
$K:=\lvert a'-b\rvert^{2}$ added to $N(\chi)=(a_{1}-\chi)^{2}+(t-1+
\psi)^{2}$. By the envelope theorem,
$\frac{d}{dK}\min_{\chi}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{2t(1-\psi(\chi^{\ast}(K)))}$.
The minimiser $\chi^{\ast}$ lies at arclength $\sim\frac{2}{\lambda}
\log t\to\infty$ (balancing $\frac{t\psi}{2}$ against
$\frac{(\chi-a_{1})^{2}}{2t}$), where $\psi(\chi^{\ast})\to0$, so
$\frac{d}{dK}\min=\frac{1}{2t}(1+o(1))$ uniformly for bounded $K$.
Integrating from $K=0$ gives the claim; the $a_{1}$- and
$t$-dependent (but offset-free) part is $\Phi$, which cancels in any
comparison of two rays.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Single-direction defect $=$ Motzkin one dimension down]
\label{thm:onedirection}
Under the standing hypotheses, with the bulk uniformly below the
horosphere, a high centre $a=(a_{1}\eta+a',t)$ has its feet on the rays
whose offsets are Euclidean-nearest to $a'$ in $\eta^{\perp}$;
therefore $a$ is medial iff $a'$ has $\ge2$ nearest points in
$B_{\eta}$. Consequently reconstruction near the horoball occurs iff
$B_{\eta}$ is non-convex in $\eta^{\perp}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-2}$
(Motzkin), the medial offsets being exactly its non-Chebyshev points.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $t$ large the bulk ($z\le1-\nu$) is farther from $a$ than any ray
(the leading term $\frac{t}{2(1-\psi)}$ is minimised on the rays, where
$\psi\to0$), so feet lie on rays; by Lemma~\ref{lem:footoffset} the
nearest ray is the offset-nearest, and $a$ is medial iff $a'$ is
non-Chebyshev for $B_{\eta}$. If $B_{\eta}$ is non-convex, Motzkin
(Theorem~\ref{thm:motzkin}) supplies such an $a'$, and running
$a_{1},t$ up the corresponding medial family reconstructs an open set
(as in Proposition~\ref{prop:offsetsym}); if $B_{\eta}$ is convex it
is Chebyshev, every centre has a unique foot, and no medial point
arises near the horoball.
\end{proof}

Completing the square in
Lemma~\ref{lem:footoffset} unifies all configurations --- several
directions, unequal profiles, mixed attained and asymptotic contacts ---
into one Euclidean Motzkin problem with \emph{moving} contacts. With
$\psi(\chi)\sim e^{-\lambda\chi}$ the minimising arclength is
$\chi^{\ast}\sim\frac{2}{\lambda}\log t$, and
\[
\cosh d\bigl(a,\ell_{\eta,\lambda,b}\bigr)
=1+\frac{(t-1)^{2}}{2t}+\frac{\bigl\lvert\alpha-P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t)
\bigr\rvert^{2}}{2t}+o(1/t),\qquad
P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t):=\tfrac{2}{\lambda}(\log t)\,\eta+b ,
\]
the same form as for an attained contact (with $P\equiv\kappa$). So at
height $t$ every contact presents as a point of $\R^{n-1}$ --- attained
ones fixed, asymptotic ones escaping along $\eta$ --- and a high centre
$a=(\alpha,t)$ is medial iff $\alpha$ is non-Chebyshev for the moving set
\[
K(t)\;:=\;K^{\star}\ \cup\ \bigl\{P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t)\bigr\}
\ \subseteq\ \R^{n-1}.
\]
(This is the corrected form of the boundary-envelope idea discussed in
\S\ref{sec:devnotes}: not a diagram on the conformal sphere, on which all
asymptotic rays collapse to one pole, but an ordinary Voronoi/Motzkin
diagram in $\R^{n-1}$ whose sites move.) Sufficiency is then immediate
and gives the whole horoball, not a swept subregion.

\begin{theorem}[Defect $\Rightarrow$ whole horoball]\label{thm:movingsuff}
Suppose the moving contact set $K(t)$ is non-convex in $\R^{n-1}$ for
all large $t$ (with the bulk uniformly below the horosphere). Then
$\{z>1\}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Motzkin, $K(t)$ has a non-Chebyshev point $\alpha(t)$ --- a medial
centre $a(t)=(\alpha(t),t)$ with $\ge2$ equidistant nearest contacts,
hence $\ge2$ feet, the ball $\B(a(t),d(a(t)))$ free. For any fixed
$p=(\pi,z_{p})$ with $z_{p}>1$, the centres have $\lvert\alpha(t)\rvert
=O(\log t)=o(t)$, so
\[
\cosh d(p,a(t))=1+\tfrac{\lvert\pi-\alpha(t)\rvert^{2}+(z_{p}-t)^{2}}
{2z_{p}t}\sim\tfrac{t}{2z_{p}},\qquad
\cosh d(a(t))\sim\tfrac{t}{2},
\]
and since $z_{p}>1$ the former is the smaller for large $t$:
$p\in\B(a(t),d(a(t)))$. As $p\in\{z>1\}$ was arbitrary,
$\{z>1\}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{proof}

For necessity we need the converse --- that two
feet witness non-convexity of $K(t)$ --- and the only thing to rule out
is the two feet lying on one ray. No-collapse, applied to a single ray,
does exactly that.

\begin{proposition}[No collapse on a single ray]\label{prop:nocollapse-ray}
An asymptotic ray $\ell_{\eta,\lambda,b}=\{(\chi\eta+b,\,1-\psi(\chi))
\}$ with convex tail profile $\psi$ is a graph contact in the sense of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse} (height deficit $\phi=\psi$, convex on the
tail). Hence a high centre has a \emph{unique} foot on it: the
minimiser of $F_{t}(\chi)=\lvert\alpha-(\chi\eta+b)\rvert^{2}+t^{2}
\psi(\chi)$ is unique, its second derivative $2+t^{2}\psi''>0$.
Consequently a centre with two feet has them on two \emph{distinct}
rays (or a ray and an attained contact), and these present as two
distinct moving sites: $P_{\eta_{1},\lambda_{1},b_{1}}(t)\neq
P_{\eta_{2},\lambda_{2},b_{2}}(t)$ for large $t$, whether the rays
differ in direction, rate, or offset. So two feet force $K(t)$
non-convex.
\end{proposition}

One subtlety makes the moving picture correct
where a naive limit fails: the site a ray presents must be read at
\emph{finite} height. The site of a ray $r$ at height $t$ is the
horizontal position of its foot-arclength,
\[
  P_{r}(t)\;=\;\chi^{\ast}(t)\,\eta_{r}\;+\;b_{r}\bigl(\chi^{\ast}(t)\bigr),
  \qquad \chi^{\ast}(t)\sim\tfrac{2}{\lambda_{r}}\log t ,
\]
where $b_{r}(\chi)$ is the offset at arclength $\chi$. For a straight ray
this is the earlier $\frac{2}{\lambda}(\log t)\eta+b$; but the offset must
be taken at $\chi^{\ast}(t)$, not in the limit $\chi\to\infty$. The
distinction is decisive:

\begin{example}[Converging ridges: the limit offset is the wrong invariant]
\label{ex:converging}
In $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ take the two ridges $\{(\chi,\pm c/\chi,1-\psi(\chi))
\}$, whose offsets $\pm c/\chi\to0$. Both have the \emph{same} limit
offset $0$, so the limit-offset set $B_{\eta}=\{0\}$ is convex and would
predict no reconstruction. Yet the pair reconstructs the whole horoball
(verified numerically: two genuine feet whose separation is stable under
refinement, $\approx0.206$ at $t=400$ across an eightfold mesh
refinement, against a sheet's single foot which collapses to the mesh).
The finite-height sites resolve it: at height $t$ the ridges sit at
$P_{\pm}(t)=\chi^{\ast}(t)\,\eta\pm c/\chi^{\ast}(t)$, \emph{distinct} for
every finite $t$ (the foot-offset equals $c/\chi^{\ast}$ to four places:
$0.166$ at $t=50$, $0.070$ at $t=5000$). So $K(t)$ is non-convex at every
height even though its two sites coalesce as $t\to\infty$. Hence uniform
separation of the limiting rays is \emph{not} necessary for
reconstruction: distinctness at each finite $t$ is what matters, and that
is exactly what Proposition~\ref{prop:nocollapse-ray} supplies.
\end{example}

With no-collapse on a ray and the
finite-height site, necessity now closes: a reconstructing drift's two
feet land on two distinct sites, so $K(t)$ is non-convex at every large
height. No uniform separation of the limiting rays is needed --- and, as
Example~\ref{ex:converging} shows, none is available in general.

\begin{theorem}[Necessity for graph-type sets]\label{thm:rays-nec}
Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ be graph-type near the horosphere (height
deficit $\phi\ge0$ locally semiconvex), and let $p\in\Rx\setminus\Xhat$
lie in the maximal free horoball $\{z>1\}$ with $z_{p}>1$. Then the
finite-height site set $K(t)=K^{\star}\cup\{P_{r}(t)\}_{r}$ is non-convex
for all large $t$; the horoball is defective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $p\notin\Xhat$, the trichotomy (Theorem~\ref{thm:structure})
together with rigidity and screening (Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity},
Lemma~\ref{lem:screening}: a unique-foot inflation escapes to the ideal
boundary and cannot reconstruct) leaves only the drift branch --- medial
centres $a_{k}=(\alpha_{k},t_{k})\in\Mx$, $t_{k}\to\infty$, each with
$\ge2$ feet, the free balls converging to $\{z>1\}\ni p$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent2} every foot ascends, $s_{k}\to1$. Each foot is
therefore either bounded --- an attained contact in $K^{\star}$ --- or
escapes along a ray (maximal convex-profile piece of $\X$), where by
Proposition~\ref{prop:nocollapse-ray} it is the \emph{unique} foot on
that piece. So the two feet of $a_{k}$ sit on two \emph{distinct} sites:
two attained contacts, two ray-pieces, or one of each. Distinct sites are
distinct points of $K(t)$ at the height in question --- attained ones
fixed, ray ones at $P_{r}(t)=\chi^{\ast}(t)\eta_{r}+b_{r}(\chi^{\ast}(t))$,
separated because the pieces are disjoint escaping graphs with distinct
foot-arclength positions. Hence $K(t)$ is non-convex. The contacts and
pieces realising the two feet are $t$-independent features of $\X$, so
$K(t)$ is non-convex for all large $t$; by Motzkin the horoball is
defective, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:movingsuff} it reconstructs its whole
interior, consistently containing $p$.
\end{proof}

\section{The reconstruction theorem}\label{sec:assembly}

Both inclusions are now in hand. We state the
$\mathbb{H}^{n}$ characterisation in the moving-diagram form that covers
compact and non-compact sets uniformly, then specialise to the compact
case where the statement is cleanest.

\begin{theorem}[Moving-contact defect; graph-type $\X$]\label{conj:rays}
For graph-type $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ a maximal free horoball
$\Hb{}$ contributes to $\Rx$ \emph{if and only if} the finite-height
site set $K(t)=K^{\star}\cup\{P_{r}(t)\}_{r}$ is non-convex in
$\R^{n-1}$ for all large $t$; the non-Chebyshev points of $K(t)$ are the
medial offsets. Sufficiency is Theorem~\ref{thm:movingsuff}, necessity
Theorem~\ref{thm:rays-nec}. This single Euclidean diagram subsumes
$K^{\star}$ non-convex (compact case), $B_{\eta}$ non-convex
(Theorem~\ref{thm:onedirection}), $\ge2$ escape directions, unequal-rate
domination, and converging offsets (Example~\ref{ex:converging}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[The compact case, closed]\label{cor:compactclosed}
For compact graph-type $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$,
\[
\Rx=(\Xhat\setminus\X)\cup\bigcup\{\Hb{}\ \text{maximal free,
horospherically defective}\}.
\]
Conjecture~\ref{conj:corrected} is a theorem in this class.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\supseteq$: the first term is \cite[Thm.~3.1]{R1}, the second is
Theorem~\ref{thm:suffcompact}. $\subseteq$: every $p\in\Rx\setminus
\Xhat$ lies in its deepest horoball, horospherically defective, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:kernelcompact} (proved in \S\ref{sec:kernel} via the
deepest-shadow lemma --- no drift needed). Since
$K_{\infty}=\varnothing$ for compact $\X$, extended defect coincides
with horospherical.
\end{proof}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The reconstruction of
$\mathbb{H}^{n}$ is thus complete for every graph-type set, in every
dimension, compact or not: the hull term $\Xhat\setminus\X$ is reconstructed
by completeness alone \cite[Thm.~3.1]{R1}, and outside the hull a point is
reconstructed iff it lies in a horospherically defective maximal free
horoball, defect being measured by non-convexity of the (finite-height,
moving) contact set. What lies strictly beyond the method is the non-graph
regime --- where the contacts do not decompose into convex-profile pieces
--- and the move off $\mathbb{H}^{n}$, where horospheres curve
(\S\ref{sec:nonhyp}).

\section*{Part II. Genesis, examples, and extensions}

\section{Bitangency, splitness, and the corrected notion}\label{sec:taxonomy}

This part records how the statement of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} was arrived at: the working notions that failed,
the counterexample that forced the correction, the worked examples, and
the routes to necessity that the deepest-shadow and moving-diagram
arguments later subsumed. None of it is needed for Part~I, but it
explains every choice made there and isolates what remains open.

The starting point of \cite{R1} was the analogue of Bia\l{}o\.zyt's
defectiveness, measured against the horoball hull. We first recall why it
must be replaced.

\begin{definition}[Bitangent; defective]\label{def:bitangent}
A maximal free horoball $\Hb{\gamma}$ is \emph{bitangent} if
$\Sb{\gamma}\cap\X$ contains at least two points, and \emph{defective}
(the notion of \cite[Def.~2.5]{R1}) if
$(\Sb{\gamma}\cap\Xhat)\setminus\X\neq\emptyset$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why defectiveness must be replaced]
\label{rem:collapse}
For $\X=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}$ one has $\Xhat=\X$: in
the upper half-plane model with $x_{1,2}=(\pm s,1)$, every point
$z=(u,v)\notin\X$ with $v\le 1$ lies in the free horoball tangent to
the ideal boundary at $(u,0)$ of Euclidean radius
$\rho=\tfrac12\bigl(\min_{\pm}(u\mp s)^{2}+1\bigr)$, and every point
with $v>1$ lies in the free horoball $\{y>1\}$. (This corrects
Remark~2.6 of \cite{R1}: the horoball hull does not even contain the
geodesic segment $[x_{1},x_{2}]$, whose interior dips into
$\{y>1\}$.) Consequently no maximal free horoball is defective,
although $\Rx=\{y>1\}\cup\B_{0}\neq\emptyset$, where $\B_{0}$ is the
horoball tangent at $0$ through both points: Conjecture~5.1 of
\cite{R1} fails as stated. Both horoballs constituting $\Rx$ are
bitangent, which motivates Definition~\ref{def:bitangent}(a) and the
revision below. We do not reproduce the full computation here, the
original conjecture having been a working formulation.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}[Revised necessity-and-sufficiency]
\label{conj:revised}
Let $M$ be Hadamard and $\X$ closed. Then
\[
\Rx \;=\; (\Xhat\setminus\X)\;\cup\;
\bigcup\Bigl\{\Hb{\gamma} \;:\; \Hb{\gamma}\ \text{maximal free,
split (Def.~\ref{def:split}) or defective}\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{conjecture}

These two notions, and the \emph{split} refinement
introduced to repair them, organise the rest of the genesis. We compare
them, prove the split sufficiency theorem that the hull collapse forced,
and then exhibit the dimension-three counterexample that retired
splitness in favour of horospherical defect.

The two notions are incomparable in general; they agree for compact
$\X$ in $\R^{n}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:taxonomy}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{(Bitangent $\not\Rightarrow$ defective, Hadamard)} For
$\X$ a two-point set in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$
(Remark~\ref{rem:collapse}), the two bitangent maximal free horoballs
are not defective, since $\Xhat=\X$.
\item \textbf{(Defective $\not\Rightarrow$ bitangent, already in
$\R^{n}$)} For $\X=\operatorname{graph}(e^{x})\cup\{(5,0)\}
\subseteq\R^{2}$, the maximal free half-space $\{y<0\}$ has the single
attained contact $(5,0)$, yet it is defective: points $(3-\theta,0)$,
$\theta\ge0$ small, lie in $\cconv\X\setminus\X$ on the boundary line.
Mass escaping to infinity in the hull can even produce defective
half-spaces whose witness is unreachable by contact sequences: for
$\X=\{(5,0)\}\cup\{(-k^{2},-k):k\in\mathbb{N}\}$, the half-space
$\{y<-0\}$\,\footnote{Precisely: the maximal free half-space in
direction $-e_{2}$ through the contact $(5,0)$.} is defective with
witnesses $(5-c,0)$, $c\in(0,C]$, obtained from convex combinations
with weights $\sim1/k$ on $(-k^{2},-k)$, while every sequence in $\X$
with $y\to0$ is eventually constant equal to $(5,0)$.
\item \textbf{(Characterisation for compact $\X$ in $\R^{n}$)} If
$\X\subseteq\R^{n}$ is compact, a maximal free half-space
$L^{+}=\{\sca{v}{\cdot}>c\}$, $c=\max_{\X}\sca{v}{\cdot}$, is
defective if and only if the slice $\partial L^{+}\cap\X$ is
\emph{not convex}. In particular bitangency
($\#(\partial L^{+}\cap\X)\ge2$) is necessary but not sufficient for
defectiveness: a segment has a convex slice
(cf.\ Example~\ref{ex:arc}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Remark~\ref{rem:collapse}. (b) The witness claims follow from
computing limits of convex combinations as indicated; closedness of
each $\X$ is immediate, and freeness and maximality of the half-spaces
follow since $\inf$ is $0$, attained at $(5,0)$ in both cases. (c)
($\Leftarrow$) If the slice is not convex, pick
$x_{1},x_{2}\in\partial L^{+}\cap\X$ and
$z\in[x_{1},x_{2}]\setminus\X$; then
$z\in\conv(\X\cap\partial L^{+})\setminus\X\subseteq
(\cconv\X\cap\partial L^{+})\setminus\X$ is a witness.
($\Rightarrow$) For compact
$\X$, $\conv\X$ is compact and
$\cconv\X\cap\partial L^{+}=\conv(\X\cap\partial L^{+})$: writing
$w=\sum\lambda_{i}x_{i}$ with $x_{i}\in\X$, $\sca{v}{w}=c$ and
$\sca{v}{x_{i}}\le c$ force $\sca{v}{x_{i}}=c$ whenever
$\lambda_{i}>0$. A defective witness $w\notin\X$ then lies in
$\conv(\X\cap\partial L^{+})\setminus(\X\cap\partial L^{+})$, so the
slice differs from its convex hull: it is not convex.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:taxonomy}(b) shows that
Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} genuinely needs the disjunction:
defective horoballs cover reconstruction produced by asymptotic
contacts (feet escaping to infinity,
Remark~\ref{rem:escape-feet}), bitangent ones cover the
hull-collapse regime where defectiveness is blind. Whether on a
Hadamard manifold the drift branch with escaping feet always lands in
a \emph{defective} horoball is open; a robust common generalisation
would count \emph{contact ends} (limits of contact sequences in
$M\cup\bdry M$), but example (b) second part shows that even this
undercounts the defective family in the presence of escaping deep
mass. We leave the right asymptotic notion open
(Question~\ref{q:asymptotic}).
\end{remark}

\section{Sufficiency: split horoballs, and the failure of bare
bitangency}\label{sec:sufficiency}

\begin{example}[Bitangency does not imply reconstruction]
\label{ex:arc}
In the upper half-plane model of $\mathbb{H}^{2}$ let
$\X:=\{(u,1) : -1\le u\le 1\}$, a compact horocyclic arc, and let
$\gamma$ be the upward vertical ray, so that
$b_{\gamma}(x,y)=-\log y$ and $c_{\gamma}=0$. The maximal free
horoball $\Hb{\gamma}=\{y>1\}$ has contact set
$\Sb{\gamma}\cap\X=\X$: it is bitangent with a continuum of contacts.
Nevertheless $\Mx=\emptyset$: for $(a,T)\notin\X$,
\[
\cosh d\bigl((a,T),(u,1)\bigr)\;=\;1+\frac{(u-a)^{2}+(T-1)^{2}}{2T}
\]
is strictly increasing in $(u-a)^{2}$, so the nearest point of the
arc is the unique point with $u=\min(\max(a,-1),1)$. Hence
$\Rx=\emptyset\not\supseteq\Hb{\gamma}$: \emph{bitangent sufficiency
is false}, and the second disjunct of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} must be the split family of
Definition~\ref{def:split}. The Euclidean shadow ($\X$ a segment in
$\R^{2}$) is handled correctly by Theorem~6 of \cite{Bia} because the
boundary slice is convex and the half-space is not defective
(Proposition~\ref{prop:taxonomy}(c)): what reconstruction detects is
a \emph{gap} in the contact set, not multiplicity.
\end{example}

\begin{definition}[Split horoball]\label{def:split}
A maximal free horoball $\Hb{\gamma}$ is \emph{split at scale
$\eta>0$} if
\[
\X\cap\{b_{\gamma}\le c_{\gamma}+\eta\}=F_{1}\cup F_{2},\qquad
\dist(F_{1},F_{2})>0,\qquad
F_{i}\cap \Sb{\gamma}\neq\emptyset\ \ (i=1,2),
\]
with $F_{1},F_{2}$ closed and nonempty.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:split-vs-defective}
A split horoball is bitangent. In $\R^{n}$ a split half-space is
defective: choose contacts $x_{i}\in F_{i}\cap\partial L^{+}$ and
bisect repeatedly, always replacing the endpoint lying in the same
cluster as the midpoint; the pair distance halves, so after finitely
many steps it drops below $2\dist(F_{1},F_{2})$, at which point the
midpoint can lie in neither cluster, hence not in $\X$ --- a witness.
Thus splitness is invisible in the Euclidean statement; on a Hadamard
manifold it is the part of defectiveness that survives the collapse
of the hull (Remark~\ref{rem:collapse}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Pure descent from a contact]\label{lem:pure}
Let $\Hb{\gamma}$ be maximal free, normalised $c_{\gamma}=0$, let
$x_{0}\in \Sb{\gamma}\cap\X$, and let $\sigma_{0}$ be the asymptotic
ray from $x_{0}$ to $\xi=\gamma(\infty)$. Then for all $t>0$:
$b_{\gamma}(\sigma_{0}(t))=-t$, $d(\sigma_{0}(t))=t$, and
$m(\sigma_{0}(t))=\{x_{0}\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By (H3), $b_{\gamma}\circ\sigma_{0}$ has slope $-1$ and vanishes at
$t=0$. For $x\in\X$ the $1$-Lipschitz bound gives
$d(x,\sigma_{0}(t))\ge b_{\gamma}(x)-b_{\gamma}(\sigma_{0}(t))\ge t$,
while $d(x_{0},\sigma_{0}(t))=t$; hence $d(\sigma_{0}(t))=t$ with
$x_{0}$ a foot. If $x$ is any foot, equality
$d(x,\sigma_{0}(t))=t$ forces $b_{\gamma}(x)=0$ and unit-rate descent
of $b_{\gamma}$ along the segment from $x$ to $\sigma_{0}(t)$; with
$|\nabla b_{\gamma}|\equiv1$ (H1), the unit-speed segment satisfies
$\dot c=-\nabla b_{\gamma}(c)$, so it is an integral curve of the
locally Lipschitz field $-\nabla b_{\gamma}$ through
$\sigma_{0}(t)$, as is $\sigma_{0}$; uniqueness of integral curves
gives $x=\sigma_{0}(0)=x_{0}$ (cf.\ Step~1 of \cite[Thm.~4.1]{R1}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Split sufficiency]\label{thm:split}
Let $M$ be Hadamard, $\X$ closed, and $\Hb{\gamma}$ split at scale
$\eta$ with clusters $F_{1},F_{2}$. Choose contacts
$x_{i}\in F_{i}\cap \Sb{\gamma}$, let $\sigma_{i}$ be the asymptotic
rays from $x_{i}$ to $\xi=\gamma(\infty)$, and set
$L_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\sigma_{1}(t),\sigma_{2}(t))$. If
$\eta>L_{\infty}/2$, then $\Hb{\gamma}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalise $c_{\gamma}=0$ and write $b:=b_{\gamma}$; by (H3),
normalised at the contacts, $b_{\sigma_{1}}=b_{\sigma_{2}}=b$. The
function $f(t):=d(\sigma_{1}(t),\sigma_{2}(t))$ is convex and bounded
(the rays are asymptotic to the same ideal point), hence
non-increasing, with $f(t)\downarrow L_{\infty}$. Fix $t$ with
$f(t)\le2\eta$ and let $J_{t}:=[\sigma_{1}(t),\sigma_{2}(t)]$ be the
geodesic segment.

\emph{Step 1: all feet over $J_{t}$ lie in $F_{1}\cup F_{2}$.} Let
$z\in J_{t}$ and $x\in m(z)$. Convexity of $b$ along $J_{t}$ gives
$b(z)\le\max_{i}b(\sigma_{i}(t))=-t$. Since $z$ lies on a segment of
length $f(t)$, there is an $i$ with $d(z,\sigma_{i}(t))\le f(t)/2$,
whence
$d(z)\le d(z,x_{i})\le d(z,\sigma_{i}(t))+d(\sigma_{i}(t),x_{i})\le
t+f(t)/2$. The Lipschitz bound then yields
$b(x)\le b(z)+d(z,x)=b(z)+d(z)\le f(t)/2\le\eta$, so
$x\in\X\cap\{b\le\eta\}=F_{1}\cup F_{2}$.

\emph{Step 2: a medial point $z_{t}\in J_{t}$ with $d(z_{t})\ge t$.}
Set $A_{i}:=\{z\in J_{t} : m(z)\subseteq F_{i}\}$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:pure}, $\sigma_{i}(t)\in A_{i}$, so both sets are
nonempty; they are disjoint because feet sets are nonempty and
$F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\emptyset$. Each $A_{i}$ is relatively open: if
$z_{k}\to z$ in $J_{t}$ and each $z_{k}$ has a foot
$x_{k}\in m(z_{k})\setminus F_{1}$, then $x_{k}\in F_{2}$ by Step~1;
the $x_{k}$ are bounded ($d(z_{k})\le t+f(t)/2$ with $z_{k}$ in the
compact $J_{t}$), so a subsequence converges to some $x$ with
$d(z,x)=\lim d(z_{k},x_{k})=\lim d(z_{k})=d(z)$ and
$x\in F_{2}\cap\X$ (both closed); thus $z\notin A_{1}$, and
contrapositively $A_{1}$ is relatively open; symmetrically $A_{2}$.
Since $J_{t}$ is connected, it is not the union of the two disjoint
nonempty relatively open sets $A_{1},A_{2}$: there is
$z_{t}\in J_{t}$ with feet in both clusters, hence
$\#m(z_{t})\ge2$ and $z_{t}\in\Mx$. Finally, for every $x\in\X$,
$d(x,z_{t})\ge b(x)-b(z_{t})\ge t$, so $d(z_{t})\ge t$.

\emph{Step 3: the medial balls $\B(z_{t},d(z_{t}))$ exhaust
$\Hb{\gamma}$.} Let $p\in\Hb{\gamma}$ and $\beta:=-b(p)>0$. The
quantity $d(p,\sigma_{i}(t))-t$ is non-increasing in $t$ with limit
$b_{\sigma_{i}}(p)=-\beta$, so
$d(p,\sigma_{i}(t))\le t-\beta+\varepsilon(t)$ with
$\varepsilon(t)\downarrow0$. Convexity of $d(p,\cdot)$ along
$J_{t}$ gives
$d(p,z_{t})\le\max_{i}d(p,\sigma_{i}(t))\le t-\beta+\varepsilon(t)$.
For $t$ large enough that $\varepsilon(t)<\beta$ and $f(t)\le2\eta$,
\[
d(p,z_{t})\;<\;t\;\le\;d(z_{t}),
\]
so $p\in\B(z_{t},d(z_{t}))\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:pinched}
If the contact rays satisfy $L_{\infty}=0$ --- in particular whenever
$M$ has sectional curvature $\le-\kappa^{2}<0$, since asymptotic
geodesics then converge --- every split horoball, at every scale
$\eta>0$, is contained in $\Rx$.
\end{corollary}

Beyond split horoballs, the following triviality records the
sufficiency of the limit objects produced by the necessity branches:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:medial-horoball}
If a maximal free horoball $\Hb{}$ is exhausted by medial balls
(for every compact $K\subseteq\Hb{}$ there is $a\in\Mx$ with
$K\subseteq\B(a,d(a))$), then $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$. In particular the
drift horoballs of Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two} satisfy
$\{b<s_{\infty}\}\subseteq\Rx$: their approximating centres are
medial by hypothesis. For the single-foot escape horoballs of
Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-one} no such conclusion is available ---
consistently with their single attained contact and with
Example~\ref{ex:arc}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Immediate: every point of $\Hb{}$ lies in some such compact $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where this leaves the conjecture]\label{rem:leaves}
Sufficiency now reads: $\Xhat\setminus\X$ always
(\cite[Thm.~3.1]{R1}); defective horoballs always
(\cite[Thm.~4.1]{R1}); split horoballs under
$\eta>L_{\infty}/2$, hence unconditionally when $L_{\infty}=0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:split}, Corollary~\ref{cor:pinched}). Two gaps
remain. On the sufficiency side: the regime
$0<2\eta\le L_{\infty}$, where the horoball is split only at scales
below half the asymptotic separation of the contact rays (possible
when those rays bound a flat half-strip); there neither
Theorem~\ref{thm:split} nor defectiveness applies. On the necessity
side: the branches of \S\ref{sec:structure} deliver maximal free
horoballs with at least one attained contact (drift: subsequential
limits of feet; ray escape: the inflation foot), and for
reconstructible $p$ these must be upgraded to
\emph{split-or-defective} (Question~\ref{q:merge}). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity} the upgrade cannot come from a single
inflation: the second contact cluster must be injected by equidistant
motion of centres, for which the pincer of Theorem~\ref{thm:split} is
the prototype.
\end{remark}

\section{Progress on the split upgrade}\label{sec:upgrade}

Throughout this section $p\in\X^{c}\setminus\Xhat$. Normalise all
Busemann functions at a basepoint ($b_{\xi}(o)=0$); then
$(\xi,y)\mapsto b_{\xi}(y)$ is continuous on $\bdry M\times M$
\cite[II.8]{BH}, so $c_{\xi}:=\inf_{\X}b_{\xi}\in[-\infty,\infty)$ is
upper semicontinuous in $\xi$ and the \emph{depth function}
\[
D(\xi)\;:=\;c_{\xi}-b_{\xi}(p)
\]
is upper semicontinuous on the compact $\bdry M$; it is independent of
the normalisation, positive somewhere precisely because
$p\notin\Xhat$, and attains its maximum
$\delta^{\star}:=\max_{\xi}D(\xi)>0$ at some $\xi^{\star}$.

\begin{theorem}[Drift or asymptotic tangency]\label{thm:dichotomy}
Let $p\in\X^{c}\setminus\Xhat$, let $\gamma^{\star}$ be a ray
asymptotic to a depth-maximising $\xi^{\star}$, and write
$b^{\star}:=b_{\gamma^{\star}}$, $c^{\star}:=c_{\gamma^{\star}}$.
Then either $T_{\delta^{\star}/4}(p)=\infty$ (\emph{drift}), or for
all sufficiently large $t$ the inflation from $\gamma^{\star}(t)$
through any foot escapes, and in that case:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $d(\gamma^{\star}(t))=t+c^{\star}+o(1)$ (asymptotically exact
free radii along the deepest axis);
\item every choice of feet $x_{t}\in m(\gamma^{\star}(t))$ satisfies
$b^{\star}(x_{t})\to c^{\star}$ (asymptotic contacts of the deepest
horoball);
\item every subsequential limit $\xi'$ of the escape directions is
itself depth-maximising: $D(\xi')=\delta^{\star}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in\X$ and every $t$, the non-increasing limit defining
$b^{\star}$ gives $d(x,\gamma^{\star}(t))-t\ge b^{\star}(x)\ge
c^{\star}$, so $d(\gamma^{\star}(t))\ge t+c^{\star}$; set
$\Delta_{t}:=d(\gamma^{\star}(t))-t-c^{\star}\ge0$ and
$\varepsilon(t):=d(p,\gamma^{\star}(t))-t-b^{\star}(p)\downarrow0$.
The margin of $p$ in the maximal free ball at $\gamma^{\star}(t)$ is
\[
\mu_{t}\;=\;d(\gamma^{\star}(t))-d(p,\gamma^{\star}(t))
\;=\;\Delta_{t}+\delta^{\star}-\varepsilon(t)\;>\;0
\quad\text{for large } t .
\]
If the inflations from $\gamma^{\star}(t)$ are cut for an unbounded
set of $t$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cutbranch} (with
$\varepsilon=\mu_{t}/4$) produces $a'_{t}\in\Mx$ with
$d(a'_{t})\ge d(\gamma^{\star}(t))-\mu_{t}/4\to\infty$ and
$\mu(a'_{t},p)\ge\mu_{t}/2\ge(\delta^{\star}-\varepsilon(t))/2\ge
\delta^{\star}/4$ eventually: drift. Otherwise the inflation from
$\gamma^{\star}(t)$ escapes for all large $t$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-one} places $p$ at depth $\ge\mu_{t}$ in the
maximal free horoball of the escape direction $\xi_{t}$, whence
\[
\Delta_{t}+\delta^{\star}-\varepsilon(t)\;=\;\mu_{t}\;\le\;
D(\xi_{t})\;\le\;\delta^{\star},
\]
so $\Delta_{t}\le\varepsilon(t)\to0$, proving (a). For (b),
$c^{\star}\le b^{\star}(x_{t})\le
d(x_{t},\gamma^{\star}(t))-t=c^{\star}+\Delta_{t}$. For (c),
$D(\xi_{t})\ge\mu_{t}\to\delta^{\star}$ and upper semicontinuity give
$D(\xi')\ge\delta^{\star}$ for any subsequential limit, while
$\le\delta^{\star}$ by maximality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
In the non-drift case the deepest horoball is asymptotically tangent
along its own axis: the obstruction to drift is \emph{exactly} an
asymptotic-contact regime. The next example shows that this regime
is, at least in the model case, the territory of defectiveness ---
the hull does \emph{not} collapse against asymptotic strands.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Asymptotic strand: defective but not split]
\label{ex:strand}
In the upper half-plane model of $\mathbb{H}^{2}$ let
\[
\X\;:=\;\{x_{1}\}\cup
\{(u,\,1-e^{u}) : u\le-1\},\qquad x_{1}:=(0,1),
\]
a contact point together with a strand asymptotic to the horosphere
$\{y=1\}$ from below. For the upward ray $\gamma$
($b_{\gamma}=-\log y$, $c_{\gamma}=0$), the maximal free horoball
$\Hb{\gamma}=\{y>1\}$ has the single attained contact $x_{1}$, so it
is not split (a second cluster meeting $\Sb{\gamma}$ does not exist).
It \emph{is} defective: $w:=(-10,1)$ is a trapped witness. Indeed, a
free horoball containing $w$ centred at $\infty$ is some
$\{y>c\}$ with $c<1$, which meets the strand (heights
$\to1$). A free horoball centred at $\xi\in\R$ is a Euclidean disk
tangent at $(\xi,0)$ of radius $\rho$; containing $w$ forces
$(\xi+10)^{2}+1<2\rho$, freeness against $x_{1}$ forces
$2\rho\le\xi^{2}+1$ (so $\xi<-5$), and then freeness against the
strand point $(\xi,1-e^{\xi})$ forces $2\rho\le1-e^{\xi}<1$ ---
contradicting $2\rho>(\xi+10)^{2}+1\ge1$. Hence $w$ lies in no free
horoball: $w\in(\Sb{\gamma}\cap\Xhat)\setminus\X$, and
$\Hb{\gamma}\subseteq\Rx$ by \cite[Thm.~4.1]{R1}. Defectiveness thus
covers the asymptotic-contact regime in which splitness is
unavailable, complementing Example~\ref{ex:arc}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Split upgrade on $\mathbb{H}^{2}$:
non-degenerate drift]\label{prop:h2-upgrade}
Let $M=\mathbb{H}^{2}$ and let $p$ be in the drift case, with medial
balls $B_{k}:=\B(a_{k},r_{k})\ni p$ (margins $\ge\delta$,
$r_{k}\to\infty$) as in Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}, whose feet
pairs $q_{k}\neq q'_{k}$ subconverge to \emph{distinct} contacts
$x^{\star}\neq x^{\star\prime}$ of the limit horoball
$\Hb{}^{\star}$. Then $\Hb{}^{\star}$ is split; consequently
(Corollary~\ref{cor:pinched}) $\Hb{}^{\star}\subseteq\Rx$, and $p$
lies in a split maximal free horoball at depth $\ge\delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Work in the upper half-plane model, normalised so that the limit
direction is $\xi=\infty$, $b(x,y)=-\log y$, and the limit level
$s_{\infty}$ is the horosphere $\{y=Y\}$; then
$\X\subseteq\{y\le Y\}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}(a)), the
contacts are $x^{\star}=(u_{1},Y)$, $x^{\star\prime}=(u_{2},Y)$ with
$G:=u_{2}-u_{1}>0$, and $b(x^{\star})=s_{\infty}=c_{\xi}$, attained.
Hyperbolic balls are Euclidean disks, so each $B_{k}$ is a Euclidean
disk; by the exhaustion property of Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}(b)
the $B_{k}$ eventually contain Euclidean disks of any prescribed
radius inside $\{y>Y\}$, hence their Euclidean radii satisfy
$R_{k}\to\infty$.

\emph{Clearance.} The boundary circle of $B_{k}$ passes through
$q_{k}\to(u_{1},Y)$ and $q'_{k}\to(u_{2},Y)$, whose heights are
$\le Y$ (they lie in $\X$). Let $\ell_{k}$ be the chord through
them; $\ell_{k}\le Y$ on the segment, and the lower boundary arc lies
below $\ell_{k}$, with sagitta at horizontal distance $\ge G/4$ from
both feet at least $2\beta_{k}$, where
$\beta_{k}:=G^{2}/(128R_{k})>0$ (parabolic approximation of a circle
of radius $R_{k}\gg G$; the feet positions converge to $u_{1},u_{2}$,
so for large $k$ the middle half
$I:=[u_{1}+G/4,\,u_{2}-G/4]$ of the positions lies strictly between
them). Consequently, for large $k$, every point with position in $I$
and height in $[Y-\beta_{k},\,Y]$ lies strictly between the lower arc
and the upper arc, i.e.\ in the open disk $B_{k}$; freeness forbids
$\X$ there.

\emph{Partition.} In $b$-levels, heights $[Y-\beta_{k},Y]$ are
exactly $\{s_{\infty}\le b\le s_{\infty}+\eta_{k}\}$ with
$\eta_{k}:=-\log(1-\beta_{k}/Y)>0$, and points with $b\le
s_{\infty}+\eta_{k}$ have height $\ge Y-\beta_{k}$. Hence
$\X\cap\{b\le s_{\infty}+\eta_{k}\}$ contains no point with position
in $I$, and splits as $F_{1}\cup F_{2}$ by position ($\le u_{1}+G/4$
versus $\ge u_{2}-G/4$). Both are closed, contain the contacts
$x^{\star}$, $x^{\star\prime}$ respectively, and have positive
hyperbolic separation: any path joining them either stays below
height $H$ at horizontal cost $\ge(G/2)/H$, or climbs to height $H$
at cost $\ge2\log(H/Y)$, so
$\dist(F_{1},F_{2})\ge\min(G/(2Y),\,2\log\tfrac{G}{4Y}+2)>0$. Thus
$\Hb{}^{\star}$ is split at scale $\eta_{k}$; since
$L_{\infty}=0$ on $\mathbb{H}^{2}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:split} applies
at every scale.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The remaining core]\label{rem:core}
After Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:h2-upgrade}, what remains of
Question~\ref{q:merge} is exactly:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{degenerate drift}: feet pairs merging
($d(q_{k},q'_{k})\to0$) or escaping to infinity --- the clearance
scale $\beta_{k}$ and the separation both degenerate with $G$;
\item \emph{the non-drift case}: upgrading the asymptotic tangency of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy} to defectiveness, for which
Example~\ref{ex:strand} is the model;
\item \emph{dimension $\ge3$}: the cleared region is a disk-shaped
shadow in the horosphere $\cong\R^{n-1}$, whose complement is
connected --- contact chains may detour around it. This is not a
mere proof-technique obstruction: \S\ref{sec:dim3} shows that for
$n\ge3$ the split-or-defective conjecture is \emph{false}, and that
splitness is a two-dimensional artefact. The remaining cases (i),
(ii) are therefore moot for Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} as stated;
they survive only relative to the corrected notion of
\S\ref{sec:dim3}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{The conjecture fails in dimension three}\label{sec:dim3}

The split refinement was forced by the collapse of the horoball hull
(Remark~\ref{rem:collapse}); the two-point set in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$
recovered reconstruction through bitangency, two \emph{separated}
contacts. We now show that in $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ a single connected
contact set already defeats Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised}: splitness
is a coincidence of dimension two.

\begin{theorem}[A counterexample in $\mathbb{H}^{3}$]\label{thm:circle}
Work in the upper half-space model
$\mathbb{H}^{3}=\{(x,y,z):z>0\}$, $\cosh d(p,q)=1+\lvert
p-q\rvert^{2}/(2z_{p}z_{q})$, and let
\[
\X\;=\;\{(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,1):\theta\in[0,2\pi)\},
\]
the unit circle on the horosphere $\{z=1\}$. Write
$R=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}$ and
$\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{R^{2}+(z-1)^{2}<1\}$ (the open Euclidean ball of
centre $(0,0,1)$ and radius $1$, a free horoball centred at the ideal
point $0$). Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $\Xhat=\X$: the horoball hull collapses to the circle;
\item $\Rx=\{z>1\}\cup\mathcal{H}_{0}$, a non-empty open set; it is
exactly the union of the two maximal free horoballs whose horospheres
contain all of $\X$;
\item every maximal free horoball meets $\X$ in a connected set
(either a single point or the whole circle); hence none is split,
and by \emph{(a)} none is defective;
\item consequently the right-hand side of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} is empty while $\Rx\neq\emptyset$. The
conjecture is false for $n=3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The medial axis is the $z$-axis: for $a=(0,0,h)$, $h>0$,
$\cosh d(a,(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,1))=1+\frac{1+(h-1)^{2}}{2h}=
\frac{h^{2}+2}{2h}$ independently of $\theta$, so all of $\X$ is
equidistant and $a$ is medial; any point off the axis has, by strict
monotonicity of $\cosh d$ in $-2R\cos\theta$, a unique nearest point,
so is not medial.

\emph{(b)} A point $p=(x,y,z)$ lies in the medial ball
$\B(a,d(a))$ iff $\cosh d(p,a)<\cosh d(a)$, i.e.\
$1+\frac{R^{2}+(z-h)^{2}}{2zh}<\frac{h^{2}+2}{2h}$. Clearing
denominators ($2zh>0$) and cancelling $-2zh$,
\[
R^{2}+z^{2}-2z+(1-z)\,h^{2}\;<\;0 .\tag{$\ast$}
\]
If $z>1$ the coefficient $(1-z)$ is negative, so $(\ast)$ holds for
$h$ large: $\{z>1\}\subseteq\Rx$. If $z=1$, $(\ast)$ reads $R^{2}<1$.
If $z<1$, the left side is increasing in $h^{2}$, with infimum
$R^{2}+z^{2}-2z$ as $h\to0^{+}$, so $(\ast)$ is solvable iff
$R^{2}+(z-1)^{2}<1$. Since off-axis points are not medial, these
axis balls give all of $\Rx$; their union is
$\{z>1\}\cup\mathcal{H}_{0}$. The horospheres $\{z=1\}$ (ideal point
$\infty$) and $\partial\mathcal{H}_{0}$ (ideal point $0$) both contain
the entire circle.

\emph{(a)} Each $q=(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\notin\X$ lies in a free open
horoball: if $z_{0}>1$, in $\{z>1\}$; if $R_{0}^{2}+(z_{0}-1)^{2}<1$,
in $\mathcal{H}_{0}$; otherwise $z_{0}\le1$, $R_{0}>0$, and the
horoball centred at the ideal point $(x_{0},y_{0},0)$ --- the
Euclidean ball of centre $(x_{0},y_{0},\rho)$ and radius $\rho$ ---
is free iff $\rho\le\tfrac12((R_{0}-1)^{2}+1)$ and contains $q$ iff
$\rho>z_{0}/2$; these are compatible because
$z_{0}-1<(R_{0}-1)^{2}$ (strict, as $q\notin\X$). Hence
$M\setminus\X$ is covered by free horoballs and $\Xhat=\X$.

\emph{(c)} A maximal free horoball is centred at an ideal point
$\xi$. For $\xi=\infty$ it is $\{z>1\}$, contact set the whole
circle. For finite $\xi$ of Euclidean norm $\sigma$ it is the
Euclidean ball of centre $(\xi,\rho^{\ast})$, radius
$\rho^{\ast}=\tfrac12((\sigma-1)^{2}+1)$, and the squared Euclidean
distance from its centre to $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,1)$ equals
$(\rho^{\ast}-1)^{2}+\sigma^{2}-2\sigma\cos\theta'+1$ ($\theta'$
measured from $\xi$), minimised at the unique $\theta'=0$ when
$\sigma>0$ --- one contact --- and constant when $\sigma=0$ --- the
whole circle ($\mathcal{H}_{0}$). In every case the contact set is
connected, so cannot be partitioned into two non-empty closed pieces
at positive distance: no maximal free horoball is split. By \emph{(a)},
$\Xhat\setminus\X=\varnothing$, so none is defective either.

\emph{(d)} The right-hand side of Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} is
$(\Xhat\setminus\X)\cup\bigcup\{\text{split or defective}\}=
\varnothing$, whereas $\Rx=\{z>1\}\cup\mathcal{H}_{0}\neq\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Splitness is a two-dimensional artefact]
\label{rem:artefact}
A horosphere $S_{\gamma}$ of a Hadamard manifold is intrinsically
flat, $S_{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$. The reconstruction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:circle} is driven by the contact set $K=S_{\gamma}
\cap\X$ failing to be \emph{intrinsically convex} in $S_{\gamma}$:
the circle's flat convex hull is the disk, and the reconstructed
points sit over the disk. In dimension $n=2$ the horosphere is a
line, $S_{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{E}^{1}$, where ``convex hull strictly
larger than $K$'' is the same as ``$K$ has a gap'', i.e.\ $K$
disconnects --- which is precisely splitness. For $n-1\ge2$ a
connected set (the circle) can have strictly larger convex hull, so
splitness (a connectivity property) is strictly weaker than the
operative condition and misses the circle entirely. This is the
exact mechanism by which Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} fails.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The corrected notion passes the tests]
\label{prop:hdefect}
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{(Circle.)} In Theorem~\ref{thm:circle} the horospherically
defective maximal free horoballs are exactly $\{z>1\}$ and
$\mathcal{H}_{0}$ (contact set the whole circle, flat hull the disk);
their union is $\Rx$. The single-contact horoballs are not
horospherically defective.
\item \emph{(Euclidean reduction.)} For $M=\R^{n}$ and compact $\X$,
$\Hb{}=L^{+}$ is horospherically defective iff the slice
$L\cap\X$ is non-convex iff $L$ is defective in the sense of
\cite{Bia} (Proposition~\ref{prop:taxonomy}(c)). Thus
Definition~\ref{def:hdefect} reduces to the hypothesis of Theorem~6.
\item \emph{(Planar reduction.)} For $M=\mathbb{H}^{2}$ a horoball
whose contact set is closed equals\,/\,refines splitness: a contact
set $K\subset\Sb{}\cong\mathbb{E}^{1}$ has
$\operatorname{hconv}(K)\neq K$ iff $K$ has a gap, the
on-the-horosphere shadow of the split condition
(Definition~\ref{def:split}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Computed in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:circle}(c): only
$\xi\in\{0,\infty\}$ give multi-point (whole-circle) contact, with
flat hull the disk $\neq$ circle; finite $\xi\neq0$ give a single
contact, flat hull a point. The union of the two is
$\{z>1\}\cup\mathcal{H}_{0}=\Rx$ by (b) of that theorem.
(b) For compact $\X$ the face
$\cconv\X\cap L=\conv(L\cap\X)$, so the ambient defect witness
$w\in(\cconv\X\cap L)\setminus\X$ exists iff $\conv(L\cap\X)\neq
L\cap\X$; and on the hyperplane $L$ the intrinsic and ambient convex
hulls coincide. (c) On a line the closed convex hull of $K$ is the
smallest interval containing it, which differs from $K$ exactly when
$K$ omits an interior point, i.e.\ has a gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Corrected necessity-and-sufficiency]
\label{conj:corrected}
Let $M$ be Hadamard and $\X$ closed. Then
\[
\Rx \;=\; (\Xhat\setminus\X)\;\cup\;
\bigcup\Bigl\{\Hb{\gamma} \;:\; \Hb{\gamma}\ \text{maximal free,
horospherically defective}\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{conjecture}

Three caveats keep this honest. First, the strand
(Example~\ref{ex:strand}) shows that contacts can be \emph{asymptotic}
--- $\X$ approaching $\Sb{\gamma}$ without meeting it --- so that
$K_{\gamma}$ undercounts; there $\Hb{\gamma}$ is defective in the
ambient sense but not horospherically defective, so the corrected
union must be read as horospherically defective \emph{or} defective,
and the genuinely correct object is presumably the flat convex hull
of the \emph{asymptotic} contact set
$\overline{\{x\in\X:b_{\gamma}(x)\to c_{\gamma}\}}$ in a suitable
horospherical compactification (Question~\ref{q:asymptotic}).
Second, for a horospherically defective $\Hb{\gamma}$ it is the
points lying \emph{over} $\operatorname{hconv}_{\gamma}(K_{\gamma})
\setminus\X$ that should be reconstructed; whether this is the whole
horoball (as for the circle and in $\R^{n}$) or a proper subregion in
general needs the inflation analysis of \S\ref{sec:inflation}
re-run with horospherical feet. Third, sufficiency
($\supseteq$) is now itself open in dimension $\ge3$: the split
sufficiency Theorem~\ref{thm:split} must be upgraded to a
horospherical-hull statement, the natural vehicle being a
\emph{wall} of medial centres swept over a triangulation of
$\operatorname{hconv}_{\gamma}(K_{\gamma})$ rather than the single
segment $J_{t}$ of the planar pincer.

\begin{corollary}[The circle, completed]\label{cor:circle-suff}
For the unit circle of Theorem~\ref{thm:circle}, both maximal free
horoballs with whole-circle contact --- $\{z>1\}$ and $\mathcal{H}_{0}$
--- satisfy $\Hb{}\subseteq\Rx$, because the circle is non-convex on
each of their (flat) horospheres. Hence
$\Rx=\{z>1\}\cup\mathcal{H}_{0}$ equals the union of the
horospherically defective horoballs, and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:corrected} holds, as an equality, for this set.
The example that refuted Conjecture~\ref{conj:revised} thus confirms
its repair.
\end{corollary}

\section{Further results and development notes}\label{sec:devnotes}

We collect here the results that are correct and
informative but were superseded, as proof routes, by the deepest-shadow
lemma and the moving diagram of Part~I: the earlier non-merging route to
necessity, the drift-or-hull reduction and its extended hull, the
$\mathbb{H}^{2}$ non-compact analysis, and the offset/boundary-envelope
development that led to the moving contact diagram. The rigidity remark
that redirected the search opens the account.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:vacuity}
Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity} redirects the search for a necessity
mechanism. Escaping inflations cannot start at medial centres, so a
single inflation can never tag two feet on a limit horosphere;
bitangency must instead be produced by moving centres \emph{along
equidistant loci} of two contact clusters --- which is exactly what
the pincer in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:split} does, and what the
medial families of the worked examples (e.g.\ the bisector family of
a two-point set in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$) actually look like. An earlier
draft of this note stated a bitangency theorem for escaping
inflations from medial centres; the rigidity above shows its
hypothesis is unsatisfiable.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The earlier necessity route: the
non-merging estimate}\label{sec:hnec}
Before the deepest-shadow lemma, necessity was approached through the
feet of a drift directly: they ascend to the horosphere and, unless they
merge, land on two distinct contacts, forcing non-convexity. The estimate
below shows merging can only happen by collapse onto a single contact,
which a graph contact never permits. For compact $\X$ this gives
necessity for bounded drifts; the deepest-shadow lemma of Part~I later
removed the drift hypothesis entirely.

\begin{proposition}[Contact feet force a defect]\label{prop:contactfeet}
If some medial centre $a$ reconstructing $p$ has two feet
$q\neq q'$ both lying in $K$, then $K$ is non-convex and $\Hb{}$ is
horospherically defective. No further hypotheses.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$q,q'$ are nearest points of $\X$ to $a=(\alpha,t)$, both in
$K\subseteq\{z=1\}$. By monotonicity at height $1$, their positions
$\kappa,\kappa'$ are Euclidean-nearest to $\alpha$ in $K$, at a common
distance; since $\kappa\neq\kappa'$, the point $\alpha$ has two nearest
points in $K$. So $K$ is not Chebyshev, hence by
Theorem~\ref{thm:motzkin} not convex.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ascent]\label{lem:ascent}
Let $a_{k}=(\alpha_{k},t_{k})$ be medial with $t_{k}\to\infty$ and
$\alpha_{k}\to\alpha_{\infty}$, and let $q_{k}=(\beta_{k},s_{k})\in
m(a_{k})$ have bounded positions $\beta_{k}$. Then
$1-s_{k}=O(t_{k}^{-2})$; in particular every subsequential limit of
$q_{k}$ lies in $K$, and $h_{k}:=(1-s_{k})t_{k}^{2}$ is bounded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a contact $k_{0}=(\kappa_{0},1)\in K$. As $q_{k}$ is a nearest
point, $\cosh d(a_{k},q_{k})\le\cosh d(a_{k},k_{0})$, i.e.
\[
\frac{\lvert\alpha_{k}-\beta_{k}\rvert^{2}+(t_{k}-s_{k})^{2}}{s_{k}}
\;\le\;\lvert\alpha_{k}-\kappa_{0}\rvert^{2}+(t_{k}-1)^{2}.
\]
Dropping the non-negative position term and using
$\frac{(t_{k}-s_{k})^{2}}{s_{k}}-(t_{k}-1)^{2}=
\frac{(1-s_{k})(t_{k}^{2}-s_{k})}{s_{k}}$ gives
$\frac{(1-s_{k})(t_{k}^{2}-s_{k})}{s_{k}}\le
\lvert\alpha_{k}-\kappa_{0}\rvert^{2}\le C$, whence
$(1-s_{k})t_{k}^{2}\le C'$ for large $k$. Closedness of $\X$ then
places limits of $q_{k}$ in $\X\cap\{z=1\}=K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Non-merging estimate]\label{thm:nonmerge}
Let $a_{k}=(\alpha_{k},t_{k})$ be medial centres of a drift
reconstructing $p\in\Hb{}$ ($t_{k}\to\infty$, $\alpha_{k}\to
\alpha_{\infty}$, margins bounded below), with distinct feet
$q_{k}\neq q_{k}'$ of bounded position. Passing to a subsequence, let
$q_{k}\to q_{\ast}=(\kappa_{\ast},1)$ and $q_{k}'\to
q_{\ast}'=(\kappa_{\ast}',1)$ in $K$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent}). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the heights ascend sharply, $h_{\ast}=\lim(1-s_{k})t_{k}^{2}=0$
and likewise for $q_{k}'$; both $\kappa_{\ast},\kappa_{\ast}'$ are
Euclidean-nearest to $\alpha_{\infty}$ in $K$;
\item \emph{(estimate)} either $\kappa_{\ast}=\kappa_{\ast}'$
(\emph{collapse}), or $\kappa_{\ast}\neq\kappa_{\ast}'$ and then
\[
d(q_{k},q_{k}')\ \longrightarrow\
\operatorname{arccosh}\!\Bigl(1+\tfrac12\lvert\kappa_{\ast}-
\kappa_{\ast}'\rvert^{2}\Bigr)\;>\;0 ;
\]
there is no intermediate decay rate;
\item in the non-collapse case $K$ has two distinct nearest points to
$\alpha_{\infty}$, so by Motzkin it is non-convex: $\Hb{}$ is
horospherically defective and contains $p$. Necessity holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $h_{k}=(1-s_{k})t_{k}^{2}$, $h_{k}'=(1-s_{k}')t_{k}^{2}$, bounded
by Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent}; pass to limits $h_{\ast},h_{\ast}'\ge0$.
For any contact $(\kappa,1)$ the nearest-point inequality
$\cosh d(a_{k},q_{k})\le\cosh d(a_{k},(\kappa,1))$, multiplied by
$2t_{k}$ and rearranged with
$\frac{(t_{k}-s_{k})^{2}}{s_{k}}-(t_{k}-1)^{2}=
\frac{(1-s_{k})(t_{k}^{2}-s_{k})}{s_{k}}\to h_{\ast}$, passes to the
limit as
\[
\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa_{\ast}\rvert^{2}+h_{\ast}\ \le\
\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa\rvert^{2}\qquad(\forall\,\kappa\in K),
\tag{$\dagger$}
\]
and symmetrically
$\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa_{\ast}'\rvert^{2}+h_{\ast}'\le
\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa\rvert^{2}$. Putting $\kappa=
\kappa_{\ast}'$ in the first and $\kappa=\kappa_{\ast}$ in the second
and adding yields $h_{\ast}+h_{\ast}'\le0$, so $h_{\ast}=h_{\ast}'=0$:
this proves (a), and ($\dagger$) becomes
$\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa_{\ast}\rvert=
\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa_{\ast}'\rvert=
\min_{\kappa\in K}\lvert\alpha_{\infty}-\kappa\rvert$.

For (b), $d(q_{k},q_{k}')\to d(q_{\ast},q_{\ast}')$, and for two points
at height $1$,
$\cosh d(q_{\ast},q_{\ast}')=1+\tfrac12\lvert\kappa_{\ast}-
\kappa_{\ast}'\rvert^{2}$. If $\kappa_{\ast}\neq\kappa_{\ast}'$ this is
$>1$, the stated bound; the absence of intermediate rates is the
dichotomy ``equal or not''. For (c), distinct equidistant nearest
points show $K$ non-Chebyshev; Motzkin gives non-convexity, and
$p$ lies in $\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$ by construction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The two ways out]\label{rem:collapse-residue}
The estimate leaves two ways for necessity to be threatened:
\emph{collapse} ($q_{k},q_{k}'\to$ a single contact) and \emph{escape}
($q_{k}$ unbounded). The next section shows collapse never occurs for
graph-type $\X$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse}), so for bounded drifts
the feet always converge to two distinct contacts and necessity holds
outright; the sole genuine residue is escape, treated in
\S\ref{sec:residue} via the extended hull.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Collapse does not occur]\label{rem:nocollapse-num}
This is confirmed numerically: for $\phi=\chi^{4}$ (convex, degenerate)
$F_{t}$ has a single critical point for every off-axis $\alpha$ and
all $t$ --- the root of $\chi+2t^{2}\chi^{3}=\alpha$, unique since the
left side is strictly increasing --- while for a double well
$\phi=(\chi^{2}-\varepsilon^{2})^{2}$ the two feet lock onto
$\pm\varepsilon$ (two separated contacts), never merging.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Necessity for bounded drifts; compact sets]
\label{cor:compactnec}
For graph-type $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$, every $p\in\Rx\setminus
\Xhat$ reconstructed by a drift with \emph{bounded} feet lies in a
horospherically defective horoball. In particular this holds for every
compact $\X$, whose feet are automatically bounded: necessity holds
with no residue.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Bounded feet ascend to contacts (Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent}); by
Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse} they cannot collapse, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonmerge} they converge to two distinct contacts.
Then $K$ is non-Chebyshev, hence non-convex (Motzkin), and the horoball
is horospherically defective and contains $p$. For compact $\X$ all
feet lie in the bounded set $\X$, so no drift can escape.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is proved, and what remains]\label{rem:hsuff}
Theorems~\ref{thm:hsphere}--\ref{thm:hiso} prove sufficiency for every
horospherically defective horoball in $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ whose contacts
are attained and isolated from the bulk; in particular the corrected
conjecture's $\supseteq$ holds, and Corollary~\ref{cor:circle-suff}
closes the circle counterexample. Three frontiers remain. \emph{(i)
Tangential bulk.} If $\X$ approaches the horosphere tangentially at a
contact (mass $(\chi_{j},1-\eta_{j})$ with
$\eta_{j}=o(\lvert\chi_{j}-\kappa\rvert)$), a fixed medial direction
can be shadowed; one expects reconstruction to survive through a
\emph{moving} medial direction, but the clean Motzkin argument no
longer applies verbatim. \emph{(ii) Curved horospheres.} Outside
$\mathbb{H}^{n}$ the horosphere need not be flat, and even
``intrinsically convex'' must be reread (the induced metric is a
Hadamard metric only under curvature pinching); Motzkin's theorem must
be replaced by its Chebyshev analogue in the relevant Alexandrov
class. \emph{(iii) Necessity.} The reverse inclusion is the
horospherical form of the upgrade (Question~\ref{q:merge}): a
reconstructible $p\notin\Xhat$ sits in a medial ball whose two feet,
followed along the structure trichotomy of \S\ref{sec:structure},
must be shown to limit onto a \emph{non-convex} contact set. By
Motzkin this is equivalent to the limit contacts failing to be
Chebyshev --- exactly the multiplicity that the two feet $q\neq q'$
encode, provided they neither merge nor are filled in by $\X$ in the
limit. This reduces necessity to a quantitative non-merging estimate,
the higher-dimensional descendant of the planar
Proposition~\ref{prop:h2-upgrade}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Drift-or-hull and the extended
horospherical hull}\label{sec:residue}
The reduction of $\subseteq$ to drift, and the extended hull that governs
the escaping (non-compact) tail, were the framework before the moving
diagram. The extended hull adjoins to the attained contacts the
\emph{ideal} contacts --- directions in which $\X$ asymptotes to the
horosphere --- and recovers both the circle (a horospherical defect) and
the strand (an ambient, asymptotic defect). It captures the right shape
of the asymptotic contact set but, as Example~\ref{ex:parallel} shows,
not its location; that defect is what the finite-height moving site of
Part~I repairs.

\begin{proposition}[Drift-or-hull, and its kernel]
\label{rem:spherecentre}
Let $p\in\Rx\setminus\Xhat$. Running the structure theorem
(Theorem~\ref{thm:structure}) along a free-horoball ray through $p$:
if the inflations are cut, $p$ drifts and
Corollary~\ref{cor:compactnec} applies. The only alternative is that
\emph{every} free-horoball direction through $p$ escapes; we claim
this cannot persist for a reconstructible $p$, but the claim is not
elementary. Two mechanisms force a defective horoball:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{(unbounded bisector)} the witness feet $q_{0}\neq q_{0}'$
span a complete totally geodesic bisector, carrying medial centres of
radius $\to\infty$; when one such subfamily inherits a uniform margin
from the depth of $p$, $p$ drifts. The three-point set in
$\mathbb{H}^{2}$ realises this --- its circumcentre is a \emph{bounded}
local maximum of $d$, yet $p$ near it drifts along the bisector rays,
so ``bounded medial $\Rightarrow$ hull'' is false; what is true is the
weaker drift-or-hull.
\item \emph{(gap horoball)} for an arc with an opening (a ``C''), the
centre is reconstructed by a \emph{bounded} medial ball yet lies in
the maximal free horoball entering through the gap, whose horosphere
meets $\X$ at the two gap edges --- a $2$-contact, horospherically
defective horoball. Here necessity holds with no drift at all.
\end{enumerate}
The geodesic sphere is the boundary case: feet form a continuum that
blocks every horoball, so $o\in\Xhat$ (verified in the half-space
model) and there is nothing to prove. Transporting the two witness
feet onto a common free horosphere, against the rigidity of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity}, looked like the residual content of
$\subseteq$. \emph{It is not needed:} \S\ref{sec:kernel} bypasses the
flow altogether for compact $\X$ --- the deepest free horoball through
$p$ is already defective (Theorem~\ref{thm:kernelcompact}) --- so the
kernel is dissolved, not merely reduced.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[Extended horospherical hull]\label{def:exthull}
Compactify the flat horosphere $\Sb{}\cong\R^{n-1}$ to a closed disc
$\overline{\Sb{}}=\R^{n-1}\cup\partial_{\infty}\Sb{}$. To the attained
contacts $K\subseteq\R^{n-1}$ adjoin the \emph{ideal contacts}
\[
K_{\infty}:=\Bigl\{\eta\in\partial_{\infty}\Sb{}\ :\ \exists\,
x_{j}=(\chi_{j},s_{j})\in\X,\ \tfrac{\chi_{j}}{\lvert\chi_{j}\rvert}
\to\eta,\ \lvert\chi_{j}\rvert\to\infty,\ s_{j}\to1\Bigr\},
\]
the directions in which $\X$ asymptotes to the horosphere. Call
$\Hb{}$ \emph{extended-defective} if
$\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(K\cup K_{\infty})\cap\R^{n-1}\neq K$
--- the closed convex hull (in $\overline{\Sb{}}$) of attained and
ideal contacts strictly exceeds $K$.
\end{definition}

\begin{conjecture}[Unified necessity-and-sufficiency, $\mathbb{H}^{n}$]
\label{conj:unified}
$\displaystyle \Rx=(\Xhat\setminus\X)\cup\bigcup\{\Hb{}\ \text{maximal
free, extended-defective}\}.$
\end{conjecture}

Extended defect unifies the two mechanisms. \emph{Circle}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:circle}): $K$ the circle, $K_{\infty}=\varnothing$,
$\operatorname{conv}=$ disc $\neq$ circle --- extended-defective, as a
horospherical defect. \emph{Strand} (Example~\ref{ex:strand}):
$K=\{(0,1)\}$, and the strand $\chi\to-\infty$ at height $\to1$ gives
$K_{\infty}=\{-\infty\}$, so $\operatorname{conv}(\{0\}\cup\{-\infty\})$
is the ray $(-\infty,0]$, whose finite part $\neq\{0\}$ --- extended-
defective, as an asymptotic (ambient) defect, with witness any
$(-c,1)$, $c>0$ (matching $w=(-10,1)$). Definition~\ref{def:hdefect}
is the case $K_{\infty}=\varnothing$; the ambient defect of
\cite[Thm.~4.1]{R1} is recovered when an interior witness is
shielded, which $K_{\infty}\neq\varnothing$ encodes.

\begin{proposition}[Compactified non-merging: one escaping foot]
\label{prop:compactnonmerge}
In $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ let $\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$ be maximal free and let $p$ be
reconstructed by a drift $a_{k}=(\alpha_{k},t_{k})$, $t_{k}\to\infty$,
with feet $q_{k}\to q_{\ast}=(\kappa_{\ast},1)\in K$ \emph{(bounded)}
and $q_{k}'=(\beta_{k}',s_{k}')$ with $\lvert\beta_{k}'\rvert\to\infty$
\emph{(escaping)}; the latter ascends, $s_{k}'\to1$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent2}. Then
$\eta:=\lim\beta_{k}'/\lvert\beta_{k}'\rvert\in K_{\infty}$, the points
$\kappa_{\ast}\in\R^{n-1}$ and $\eta\in\partial_{\infty}\Sb{}$ are
distinct in $\overline{\Sb{}}$, and the closed ray
$\kappa_{\ast}+\R_{\ge0}\,\eta\subseteq\overline{\operatorname{conv}}
(K\cup K_{\infty})$ has interior points outside $\X$. Hence $\Hb{}$ is
extended-defective (Definition~\ref{def:exthull}) and contains $p$:
necessity holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The escaping foot $q_{k}'\in\X$ has position $\to\infty$ along $\eta$
and height $\to1$, so $\eta\in K_{\infty}$ by definition. In the disc
$\overline{\Sb{}}$ a finite point and an ideal point are distinct, and
$\overline{\operatorname{conv}}$ of $\{\kappa_{\ast},\eta\}$ contains
the Euclidean ray from $\kappa_{\ast}$ in direction $\eta$; its
interior points $\kappa_{\ast}+r\eta$ ($r>0$) lie on $\{z=1\}$, so are
in $\X$ only if they are contacts in $K$. Were the whole ray in $K$,
the medial centres $a_{k}$ --- equidistant from $q_{k}$ and $q_{k}'$
with both feet ascending --- would, by the monotonicity of position
distance, have a foot at the projection of $\alpha_{k}$ to this convex
contact ray, contradicting two separated feet for large $k$ (no-collapse,
Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse}). So some ray point is a witness, $\Hb{}$
is extended-defective. Finally $p\in\Hb{}$ by the drift (margin
$\ge\delta$, Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two escaping feet in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$]
\label{prop:twoescape}
Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{2}$ be closed and graph-type, $\Hb{}=
\{z>1\}$ the maximal free horoball of a drift reconstructing
$p\notin\Xhat$, and suppose both of the drift's feet escape
($\lvert\beta_{k}\rvert,\lvert\beta_{k}'\rvert\to\infty$). Then the
feet run off to the two opposite ideal points, $K_{\infty}=
\{-\infty,+\infty\}$, the extended hull is all of $\R$, and $\Hb{}$ is
extended-defective and contains $p$: necessity holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both feet ascend by Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent2}, so each escape direction
$\eta=\lim\beta_{k}/\lvert\beta_{k}\rvert\in\{-\infty,+\infty\}$ lies
in $K_{\infty}$. They are
distinct: if $\beta_{k},\beta_{k}'\to+\infty$ together, then for large
$k$ both feet lie on the single right-hand graph branch of $\X$, on
which (Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse}, $F_{t}$ strictly convex) the
nearest point of a centre is unique --- contradicting two feet. So one
foot runs to $-\infty$, the other to $+\infty$, and $K_{\infty}=
\{-\infty,+\infty\}$. A closed convex subset of $\R$ whose recession
set contains both $\pm\infty$ is all of $\R$, so
$\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(K\cup K_{\infty})\cap\R=\R\neq K$ (a
proper closed subset for any $\X\neq\Sb{}$): $\Hb{}$ is
extended-defective. As $p$ has depth $\delta^{\star}>0$,
$p\in\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status of the residue]\label{rem:residuestatus}
For compact $\X$ ($K_{\infty}=\varnothing$) the kernel is dissolved
(\S\ref{sec:kernel}, Theorem~\ref{thm:kernelcompact}). The non-compact
tail $K_{\infty}\neq\varnothing$ has sufficiency from \cite[Thm.~4.1]
{R1} (ambient defect), and necessity from
Proposition~\ref{prop:compactnonmerge} (one foot bounded, one escaping
--- the strand) and Proposition~\ref{prop:twoescape} (both feet
escaping, $\mathbb{H}^{2}$), the ascent of escaping feet now being
automatic (Lemma~\ref{lem:ascent2}). \emph{Thus in $\mathbb{H}^{2}$ the
unified Conjecture~\ref{conj:unified} is a theorem, both directions,
compact and non-compact alike.} For $n\ge3$ the non-compact case was
the last holdout, since the direction-only ideal-contact set cannot
distinguish a single asymptotic ridge (no reconstruction) from two
parallel ones (reconstruction) --- Example~\ref{ex:parallel}. Refining
the ideal contact by its perpendicular \emph{offset} and reading the
moving sites at finite height closes it too
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rays-nec}, \S\ref{sec:noncompact3}): the unified
statement is now a theorem for every graph-type set in every dimension.
(The compact case is closed in every dimension, \S\ref{sec:kernel}.)
\end{remark}

\subsection{The offset, and the boundary-envelope
analysis}\label{sec:genesis-offset}
The direction-only ideal-contact set cannot distinguish a single
asymptotic ridge from two parallel ones. The example below isolates the
missing datum --- the perpendicular offset --- and the development that
follows led, after one false turn (a conformal-sphere envelope) and one
correction (the moving Euclidean diagram), to the picture of Part~I.

For \emph{anchored} non-compact sets the deepest-shadow method extends
cleanly: when the deepest infimum is attained on a bounded set of
minimisers (an attained contact realising the depth, with the bulk
uniformly below the horosphere outside a compact set), the tilt of
Lemma~\ref{lem:deepshadow} applies verbatim --- the attained
minimisers rise at rate $\ge\varepsilon$ under an infinitesimal tilt
toward the side on which $\pi$ separates, contradicting maximality ---
so $\pi$ lies in the recession-cone hull
$\overline{\operatorname{conv}}(K^{\star})+\operatorname{cone}
(K_{\infty})$, and screening (Lemma~\ref{lem:screening}, dimension- and
compactness-free) finishes necessity as before. This covers the
strand, the rotationally symmetric valley (whose rim supplies a full
sphere $K_{\infty}=S^{n-2}$, numerically confirmed), and every set with
an attained anchor.

\emph{But the direction-only data $K_{\infty}\subseteq\partial_{\infty}
\Sb{}$ is not enough when there is no anchor}, and this is a genuine
$n\ge3$ obstruction, not a technicality. The witness is a pair of
configurations in $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ that share \emph{every} directional
invariant yet behave oppositely.

\begin{example}[Single versus parallel ridge]\label{ex:parallel}
In $\mathbb{H}^{3}$ let
\[
\X_{1}=\{(s,0,1-\operatorname{sech}s)\},\qquad
\X_{2}=\{(s,c,1-\operatorname{sech}s)\}\cup\{(s,-c,1-\operatorname{sech}
s)\}\quad(c>0),
\]
a single ridge and two parallel ridges, all running to the ideal
direction $\eta=(1,0)$ at height $\to1$. Both have $K^{\star}=
\varnothing$ and the \emph{same} ideal-contact set $K_{\infty}=
\{\eta\}$. Yet $\Rx(\X_{1})$ reconstructs nothing near the horoball
(a single graph branch is Chebyshev; numerically no point with two feet
at separated parameters), while $\X_{2}$ reconstructs the strip between
its ridges (medial centres on $\{y=0\}$ with one foot per ridge;
numerically $(s,0,z)\in\Rx$ for $z>1$). Any invariant built from
$K^{\star}$ and the \emph{directions} $K_{\infty}$ alone --- in
particular the recession-cone hull --- assigns both the same value and
so cannot be correct.
\end{example}

This retracts the closure claimed in an earlier draft: the
recession-cone hull captures the right \emph{shape} (a sector, a ray, a
line) but not its \emph{location}. We now formulate the correct object
and prove the mechanism that resolves Example~\ref{ex:parallel}.

In Example~\ref{ex:parallel}, $\eta=(1,0)$, $\eta^{\perp}$ is the
$y$-axis; for $\X_{1}$, $B_{\eta}=\{0\}$ (convex --- not ray-defective);
for $\X_{2}$, $B_{\eta}=\{+c,-c\}$ (non-convex --- ray-defective). So
the offset set distinguishes them where the direction cannot.

\begin{proposition}[Offset symmetry forces reconstruction]
\label{prop:offsetsym}
Let $\X\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}$ be invariant under the reflection of
$\eta^{\perp}$ fixing a hyperplane $H\ni0$, and suppose
$B_{\eta}\not\subseteq H$ (two asymptotic rays swapped by the
reflection, with offsets off $H$). Then the fixed locus
$\{(\alpha,t):\alpha^{\perp}\in H\}$ consists of medial points
(reflection pairs a foot on one ray with its mirror on the other), and
their balls reconstruct an open subset of $\{z>1\}$: $\Rx\neq
\varnothing$, witnessing ray-defectiveness. The single-ray $\X_{1}$,
having no such symmetry and a Chebyshev (singleton) offset set,
reconstructs nothing near the horoball.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reflection $\sigma$ of $\eta^{\perp}$ extends to a hyperbolic
isometry fixing $\infty$ and the locus $L=\{\alpha^{\perp}\in H\}$. For
$a\in L$, $\sigma$ fixes $a$ and permutes $\X$, so the nearest point
and its mirror are equidistant from $a$; as $B_{\eta}\not\subseteq H$
these are two \emph{distinct} feet (on the two swapped rays), so $a$ is
medial. Letting $a$ run up $L$ gives medial balls whose union is a
non-empty open set, exactly as for two ridges in distinct directions
(\S\ref{sec:residue}, numerically the whole strip). For $\X_{1}$ the
foot map on a single asymptotic graph is single-valued, so no medial
point arises near the horoball.
\end{proof}

The remaining notes record the moving-diagram
analysis as it was developed: the multi-direction picture obtained by
completing the square, the closed-form sufficiency, the assessment of a
proposed boundary-envelope unification, and the honest status at each
stage. The conclusions are exactly those proved cleanly in
\S\ref{sec:nec-noncompact}; the discussion here documents the route and
the dead ends, in particular why confinement of the drift is neither
available nor needed.

This is the promised reduction, now a theorem: the multi-scale obstruction
is real but inert, because the $\log t$-scale penalty is common to all
rays of one profile and cancels. Numerically the medial offsets track
Motzkin exactly --- for $B_{\eta}=\{-2,0,2\}$ they converge to the
non-Chebyshev points $\pm1$ as the height grows, and the convex
continuum $B_{\eta}=[-2,2]$ reconstructs nothing.

\paragraph{The moving-contact picture (multi-direction).}
Completing the square in Lemma~\ref{lem:footoffset} unifies \emph{all}
the cases --- several directions, unequal profiles, mixed
attained/asymptotic --- into a single Euclidean Motzkin problem with
\emph{moving} contacts. Indeed, with $\psi(\chi)\sim e^{-\lambda\chi}$
the minimising arclength is $\chi^{\ast}\sim\frac{2}{\lambda}\log t$,
and the expansion collapses to
\[
\cosh d\bigl(a,\ell_{\eta,\lambda,b}\bigr)
=1+\frac{(t-1)^{2}}{2t}+\frac{\bigl\lvert\alpha-P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t)
\bigr\rvert^{2}}{2t}+o(1/t),\qquad
P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t):=\tfrac{2}{\lambda}(\log t)\,\eta+b ,
\]
the same form as for an attained contact $\kappa$ (which has
$P\equiv\kappa$). \emph{So at height $t$ every contact --- attained or
asymptotic --- presents as a point of $\R^{n-1}$:} the attained ones
fixed at $\kappa$, the asymptotic ones \emph{escaping} along their
direction $\eta$ at speed $\tfrac{2}{\lambda}\log t$, offset by $b$.
The identity is exact in the limit (verified numerically: the ratio of
the two sides tends to $1$ to four places by $t=10^{3}$). Consequently
a high centre $a=(\alpha,t)$ has its feet at the Euclidean-nearest
effective positions, and is medial iff $\alpha$ is non-Chebyshev for
the moving set
\[
K(t)\;:=\;K^{\star}\ \cup\ \bigl\{P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t)\bigr\}
\ \subseteq\ \R^{n-1}.
\]

This is the correct form of the boundary-envelope idea: not a power
diagram on $\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ --- on
which every asymptotic ray collapses to the \emph{single} pole $\infty$
(bounded height, position $\to\infty$), so directions and offsets are
invisible --- but an ordinary Voronoi/Motzkin diagram in $\R^{n-1}$
whose sites \emph{move} as $t\to\infty$. The fragmented cases are now
one statement: \emph{a single direction} gives sites $\frac{2}{\lambda}
(\log t)\eta+b$ on a common escaping line, so $K(t)$ is non-convex iff
the offsets $B_{\eta}$ are (Theorem~\ref{thm:onedirection}); \emph{many
directions} give sites escaping along distinct rays, whose convex hull
has a vertex per extreme direction; \emph{unequal rates} make the
smaller-$\lambda$ sites escape faster, so they reach the hull first and
dominate (the slower cells vanish); and \emph{attained contacts}, being
fixed while the asymptotic sites recede to infinity, are overtaken
unless they themselves sit on the hull. Gemini's envelope intuition is
thus right, with the conformal sphere replaced by the moving Euclidean
diagram and the weight replaced by the escape speed
$\frac{2}{\lambda}\log t$.

\paragraph{Sufficiency is the whole horoball, not a swept region.}
One might expect the reconstructed set to be a thin region swept by the
moving medial centres --- a horospherical cylinder over the offset
bisector. It is not: numerically, parallel ridges at offset $\pm c$
reconstruct \emph{all} of $\{z>1\}$ (every offset, far ahead, behind),
exactly as the circle does. The reason is the same high-centre
mechanism, and it gives sufficiency in closed form.

So defect implies the whole horoball, exactly as in the compact case
--- no region refinement, against Point~3 of the boundary-envelope
proposal. The proof used the confinement $\lvert\alpha(t)\rvert=
O(\log t)$ of the \emph{chosen} witness centres --- the Motzkin
non-Chebyshev point of $K(t)$, which we are free to take inside
$\operatorname{conv}K(t)$: this keeps \emph{their} effective-position
expansion valid, since beyond $\lvert\alpha\rvert=o(t)$ the $\cosh$
ceases to be quadratic. But confinement here is a property we
\emph{impose} on the witnesses we pick, not one that holds for all
reconstructing centres, and the stronger reading of Point~1 --- that
\emph{every} medial centre is so confined, provable by comparison
against a fixed attained contact --- is false. It is vacuous in the
open case, where there is no attained contact to compare against
($K^{\star}=\varnothing$, as for the parallel ridges of
Example~\ref{ex:parallel}); and it fails outright, because the entire
bisector plane of two asymptotic rays consists of medial centres, each
reconstructing the whole horoball however far it has drifted laterally
(numerically, centres at lateral distance up to $75\log t$ from the
sites still reconstruct a fixed $p$, with two feet on the two rays).
Confinement is therefore not the route to necessity.

The route that does work is the no-collapse mechanism of
Theorem~\ref{thm:nocollapse}, now applied to a single ray.

\begin{remark}[Honest status]\label{rem:noncompact3}
What is now \emph{proved}: the offset --- not just the direction --- is
the correct refinement (Definition~\ref{def:asymray},
Example~\ref{ex:parallel}); and for asymptotic approach in a
\emph{single} direction with a common profile, defect reduces exactly
to Motzkin in $\eta^{\perp}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:onedirection}), the
medial offsets tracking the non-Chebyshev points (numerically exact).
The supposed multi-scale obstruction turned out to be inert: the
$\log t$-scale ray penalty is common to all rays of one profile and
cancels under the envelope theorem (Lemma~\ref{lem:footoffset}).
Moreover the whole cross-talk now has a clean form: by completing the
square, an asymptotic ray presents at height $t$ as a contact at the
moving point $P_{\eta,\lambda,b}(t)=\frac{2}{\lambda}(\log t)\eta+b$
(identity verified to four places), so several directions, unequal
profiles, and mixed attained/asymptotic configurations all become one
Euclidean Motzkin problem on the moving set $K(t)$ --- now \emph{both}
halves proved. Sufficiency: $K(t)$ non-convex implies the \emph{whole}
horoball is reconstructed (Theorem~\ref{thm:movingsuff}), with no
swept-cylinder refinement. Necessity: a reconstructing drift's two feet
sit on two distinct sites at every height (Theorem~\ref{thm:rays-nec}),
so $K(t)$ is non-convex --- read at \emph{finite} height, where the
moving site of a ray is $P_{r}(t)=\chi^{\ast}(t)\eta+b(\chi^{\ast}(t))$.
The earlier ``non-merging'' worry was a mis-statement of the gap:
uniform separation of the limiting rays is \emph{not} needed and can
even fail, as converging ridges $\pm c/\chi$ show
(Example~\ref{ex:converging}) --- their sites coalesce yet they
reconstruct, because $K(t)$ is non-convex at each finite $t$.
Confinement of the drift, contrary to the boundary-envelope proposal's
Point~1, is neither available (the bisector plane reconstructs at every
lateral distance) nor needed. So the hyperbolic case is now closed for
every graph-type set in every dimension --- compact or not, anchored or
not --- both directions (Theorem~\ref{conj:rays}). What lies strictly
beyond is only the non-graph regime (wild, non-semiconvex asymptotic
approach), where the decomposition into convex-profile pieces fails, and
the move off $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ (\S\ref{sec:nonhyp}).
\end{remark}

\section{Toward non-hyperbolic Hadamard manifolds}\label{sec:nonhyp}

The $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ theory rested on two pillars that fail in
general, and naming their failure delimits the next step precisely.

\emph{Pillar 1: homogeneity.} ``WLOG $\Hb{}=\{z>1\}$'' used the
transitive action of $\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb{H}^{n})$ on
horoballs. This survives only in two-point homogeneous (rank-one
symmetric) spaces and Damek--Ricci spaces; for a general Hadamard
manifold each horoball must be treated in place.

\emph{Pillar 2: flat horospheres.} The whole Motzkin reduction --- feet
of high centres are the intrinsic-nearest contacts, and convexity of
the contact set is Euclidean --- used that horospheres of
$\mathbb{H}^{n}$ are intrinsically flat. The Gauss equation locates
the obstruction quantitatively: for a horosphere $S$ with shape
operator $\mathrm{II}$ (positive semidefinite, as horoballs are
convex), a tangent $2$-plane has intrinsic curvature
\[
K_{S}=K_{M}+\det(\mathrm{II}|_{\text{plane}}).
\]
In $\mathbb{H}^{n}$, $S$ is umbilic, $\mathrm{II}=\mathrm{Id}$ and
$K_{M}=-1$, so $K_{S}=-1+1=0$: flat, and Euclidean Motzkin applies.
Under pinching $-b^{2}\le\sec\le-a^{2}<0$ one has principal curvatures
in $[a,b]$, hence
\[
K_{S}\in[\,a^{2}-b^{2},\ b^{2}-a^{2}\,],
\]
which can be \emph{positive}. So horospheres are in general not
$\mathrm{CAT}(0)$, and the foundational equivalence
``Chebyshev $\Leftrightarrow$ convex'' (Theorem~\ref{thm:motzkin})
need not hold --- on positively curved spaces Chebyshev sets can be
non-convex. This, not homogeneity, is the deep obstacle.

\emph{What should survive.} The structural backbone is curvature-free
or robust: completeness gives $\Xhat\setminus\X\subseteq\Rx$
\cite[Thm.~3.1]{R1}; rigidity (Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity}),
the drift theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-two}, via the horofunction
boundary), and the structure trichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:structure}) are stated for all Hadamard $M$. What
must be rebuilt is the local dictionary at the horosphere:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Feet $\to$ intrinsic-nearest contacts.} As a centre drifts
to $\xi$ along the axis through the horosphere (which it meets
orthogonally), the comparison of $d_{M}(a,\cdot)$ on $S_{\gamma}$
should localise to the induced metric $d_{S_{\gamma}}$; this is the
general replacement for the monotonicity used throughout
\S\ref{sec:dictionary} and \S\ref{sec:suff-main} and appears provable from
Jacobi-field control of the second fundamental form.
\item \emph{A Chebyshev/convexity theorem in $S_{\gamma}$.} With (i),
both sufficiency and necessity need only: in the induced geometry of the
horosphere, a closed contact set with a centre having $\ge2$ nearest
points is non-convex, and conversely. This is Motzkin's
theorem in an Alexandrov space with two-sided curvature bounds ---
true under suitable hypotheses, the precise class being the question.
\end{enumerate}

Two natural arenas present themselves, with different trade-offs;
which to develop first is the fork below.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Rank-one symmetric spaces} ($\mathbb{CH}^{n}$,
$\mathbb{HH}^{n}$, $\mathbb{OH}^{2}$) and Damek--Ricci spaces:
homogeneity (Pillar 1) is retained, horoballs are still a single
orbit, but horospheres are nilpotent groups (Heisenberg-type) with
left-invariant metrics --- non-flat, sub-Riemannian in the limit.
Motzkin becomes a Chebyshev question in a Carnot group.
\item \emph{Pinched negative curvature} (general $-b^{2}\le\sec\le
-a^{2}$): no homogeneity, horospheres Alexandrov with two-sided
bounds; the cleanest setting for (i)--(ii) above as pure comparison
geometry, at the cost of explicit models.
\end{itemize}

\begin{question}[The necessity gap beyond $\mathbb{H}^{n}$]
\label{q:merge}
In $\mathbb{H}^{n}$ this is now settled for graph-type sets
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rays-nec}, Theorem~\ref{conj:rays}); the questions
below concern the move to a general Hadamard manifold, where the
moving-Motzkin diagram is unavailable. Theorem~\ref{thm:circle} retires
the split-or-defective form of the question; the live version is
whether Conjecture~\ref{conj:corrected} holds: for $p\in\Rx\setminus
\Xhat$, must some maximal free horoball containing $p$ be
horospherically defective (Definition~\ref{def:hdefect})? By
Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity} the upgrade cannot come from a single
inflation: the medial witness of $p$ injects its further contacts
through equidistant motion of centres, as in the pincer of
Theorem~\ref{thm:split}. The concrete target is an
\emph{equidistant-flow theorem}: starting from $a\in\Mx$ and
$p\in\B(a,d(a))$, grow free balls with centres on the equidistant
locus of the contact set, and show the family drifts to a horoball
whose horospherical contact hull contains the shadow of $p$. For
$n=2$ this is the split upgrade of \S\ref{sec:upgrade}
(Remark~\ref{rem:core} delimits its open core); for $n\ge3$ the
single segment $J_{t}$ of the planar pincer must become a swept
$(n-1)$-dimensional wall over
$\operatorname{hconv}_{\gamma}(K_{\gamma})$.
\end{question}

\begin{question}\label{q:asymptotic}
Formulate the correct asymptotic notion of horospherical contact
hull --- the flat convex hull in $\Sb{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{E}^{n-1}$ of
the \emph{asymptotic} contact set
$\overline{\{x\in\X:b_{\gamma}(x)\to c_{\gamma}\}}$, in a horospherical
compactification --- so that Conjecture~\ref{conj:corrected} both
covers the asymptotic strand (Example~\ref{ex:strand},
Proposition~\ref{prop:taxonomy}(b)), where contacts recede to the
ideal boundary, and reduces to Definition~\ref{def:hdefect} when
contacts are attained. Then identify, for a horospherically
defective horoball, the exact reconstructed subregion (the second
caveat after Conjecture~\ref{conj:corrected}), and prove the
resulting equality.
\end{question}

\begin{question}\label{q:positive-curv}
Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-one} uses only global minimality of the
extended foot-geodesic. On a complete, non-Hadamard manifold the
statement survives whenever the inflation ray is globally minimizing;
the flat cylinder (where bitangency without multiplicity is possible,
$\X=S^{1}\times\{0\}$) shows that bitangency must then be read as
``two minimizing geodesics''. Determine the sharp generality.
\end{question}
