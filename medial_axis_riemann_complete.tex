\begin{abstract}
Let $\X$ be a nonempty proper closed subset of a complete Riemannian
manifold $M$. The \emph{two-geodesic medial axis} $\Mg$ is the set of
points admitting at least two minimising geodesics to $\X$ --- the
multi-geodesic part of the cut locus of $\X$ --- and the associated
\emph{reconstruction} $\Rg$ is the union of the open free balls
$B(a,\dX(a))$ over $a\in\Mg$. We characterise $\Rg$ on every complete
$M$, with no curvature, genericity, analyticity, spread, or
cardinality hypothesis. Two mechanisms drive the theory. A
\emph{capture theorem} shows that along any solution of the
differential inclusion $\Phi'\in-\conv U(\Phi)$, driven by the unit
directions of minimising geodesics, the distance $\dX$ grows at unit
rate until the first approach to $\Mg$; hence every point $p$ either
lies at depth arbitrarily close to $\dX(p)$ inside medial balls, or
its trajectory escapes to infinity. In particular $\Rg=M\setminus\X$ on every
compact manifold, for every closed $\X$ --- even a single point ---
answering a question of Bia\l{}o\.zyt. Second, normalising free
balls by their reach, the locally uniform limits
$\psi=\lim\,[d(\cdot,a_{k})-\dX(a_{k})]$ over medial centres ---
\emph{medial limit functions} --- satisfy
$\{\psi<0\}\subseteq\Rg$, and $\Rg$ is exactly the union of these
sublevel sets; every point is captured, drift-swallowed, or
escaping. Bia\l{}o\.zyt's Euclidean characterisation, and the
compact, spherical, hyperbolic, and Hadamard pictures, are
specialisations;
on the flat cylinder we prove a sharp end-certificate dichotomy. A
topological layer follows: the escape class coincides with the
Aubry set of $\dX$ in the sense of Cannarsa--Cheng--Fathi, so the
captured part of the complement always has the homotopy type of
$\Mg$, and when $\sup\dX<\infty$ the complement, the axis, and the
cut locus are homotopy equivalent --- uniform capture recovers the
Ford--Voronoi spines of cusped hyperbolic manifolds and the Voronoi
spines of crystallographic quotients, and for smooth $\X$ the
captured set is the mapping cylinder of the swallow map onto the
cut locus. Finally, every swallowed-uncaptured point lies in an
\emph{ideal} free horoball at depth no smaller than its depth in
any finite medial ball; consequently the \emph{medial complex} ---
$\Mg$ with the ideal free horoballs glued along the pattern of
their overlaps --- always carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$.
Complete reconstruction does not transport homotopy type, already
for three points in the plane; the medial complex repairs the leak
unconditionally.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The reconstruction problem}

Fix a nonempty closed set $\X$ in a metric space $M$ and, for each
point
$p$ outside $\X$, ask for the nearest points of $\X$. Where the answer
is unique the geometry of $\X$ is locally transparent; the interesting
locus is the set of $p$ with an \emph{ambiguous} nearest-point
answer. In the plane this is the classical \emph{medial axis} or
\emph{skeleton}, a one-dimensional graph that threads the region
between the features of $\X$ and has been used, since Blum, as a
shape descriptor in vision, in mesh generation, and in the topology of
domains \cite{Lie,CL,Dam}. Two features of the axis make it a genuine
\emph{descriptor} rather than a mere by-product: each axis point $a$
carries a scalar, the distance $\dX(a)$ to $\X$, and the open ball
$B(a,\dX(a))$ of that radius is a \emph{free ball} --- a maximal ball
that meets $\X$ only in its boundary. The union of the free balls over
the axis,
\[
  \Rx=\bigcup_{a\in\Mx}B\bigl(a,\dX(a)\bigr),
\]
is the \emph{reconstruction} of the complement of $\X$ from the axis.

One wants to know when the reconstruction is faithful, i.e.\ when
$\Rx=M\setminus\X$. The obstruction is easy to see and hard to
characterise. If $\X$ is convex in $\R^{n}$ the axis is empty and
$\Rx=\varnothing$: convex sets cast no medial shadow, so their
complements are not reconstructed at all. If $\X$ is a pair of points
in the plane, the axis is the perpendicular bisector and its free
balls reconstruct almost everything: the central ball already
swallows the open segment between the points, and balls centred ever
farther out along the bisector fill the two open half-planes bounded
by the line through the pair --- the deeper the point, the more
remote the ball that reaches it, and the organising object is the
\emph{limit} of the escaping balls, an open half-plane. What no ball
ever reaches are the two collinear rays beyond the points. The
interplay between the local balls, which fill the ``inside'' of the
hull, and the escaping balls, whose limits fill the outer half-spaces
of defective faces of the hull, is the whole content of the Euclidean
theorem.

\subsection{Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem, the starting point}

Bia\l{}o\.zyt \cite{Bia} settled the Euclidean case completely. Write
$\conv\X$ for the convex hull and $\overline{\conv}\,\X$ for its
closure. A supporting hyperplane $L$ of $\overline{\conv}\,\X$ is
\emph{defective} if $\X\cap L\neq\overline{\conv}\,\X\cap L$, i.e.\ if
$\X$ fails to fill the contact face that the hull presents on $L$; for
each supporting $L=\{\sca{v}{\cdot}=c\}$ (with $\X\subseteq
\{\sca{v}{\cdot}\le c\}$) let $L^{+}=\{\sca{v}{\cdot}>c\}$ be the open
outer half-space.

\begin{theorem}[Bia\l{}o\.zyt {\cite[Thm.~6]{Bia}}]\label{thm:bia}
Let $\X\subseteq\R^{n}$ be closed and nonempty. Then $\X$ is
reconstructible, i.e.\ $\Rx=\R^{n}\setminus\X$, if and only if
\[
  \R^{n}\setminus\X\ \subseteq\ \overline{\conv}\,\X\ \cup\!\!
  \bigcup_{L\ \text{defective}}\!\!L^{+}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Two features of this statement organise everything that follows.
First, the hull $\overline{\conv}\,\X$ is reconstructed by
\emph{ordinary} (finite) medial balls: every point of
$\overline{\conv}\,\X\setminus\X$ lies in a free ball centred on the
axis \cite{Bia}. Second, the outer half-spaces
$L^{+}$ of defective supporting hyperplanes are reconstructed only in
the \emph{limit}, by free balls whose centres escape to infinity along
a direction normal to $L$; a supporting hyperplane contributes such a
limit precisely when its contact set is defective --- equivalently,
by Motzkin's theorem \cite{Mot}, when a point of $L$ just outside the
contact face is non-Chebyshev. This is the dichotomy \emph{local
balls vs.\ escaping balls} in its cleanest form, and the first goal
of this paper is to find its counterpart on a curved manifold, where
``half-space'' must become ``horoball'', ``convex hull'' must become
something curvature-dependent, and the very notion of ``two nearest
points'' must be re-examined because a single nearest point can be
reached by several competing geodesics. The second goal is the
question Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem raises but cannot ask in $\R^{n}$:
what happens on a \emph{compact} manifold, where there is no
infinity for the centres to escape to --- a question posed explicitly
in \cite[\S5.2]{Bia}. The third goal, occupying the second half of
the paper, is topological: when, and in what form, the axis carries
the \emph{homotopy type} of what it reconstructs.

\subsection{From $\R^{n}$ to a manifold: what breaks}

Let $M$ be a complete Riemannian manifold. Three Euclidean
conveniences fail at once.

\emph{(i) Geodesics are not unique.} Two points can be joined by
several minimising geodesics, and the nearest-point map to $\X$ can be
single-valued (a unique foot $y\in\X$) yet ambiguous as a
\emph{geodesic} problem (several minimisers reaching that same $y$).
The sphere makes this vivid: for $\X$ a single point, every other
point has that point as its unique nearest point, yet from the
antipode it is reached by a whole sphere's worth of minimising
geodesics --- one nearest point, many nearest geodesics.
This forces a distinction, invisible in $\R^{n}$, between the
\emph{two-point} axis $\Mx$ (two nearest \emph{points}) and the
\emph{two-geodesic} axis $\Mg$ (two minimising \emph{geodesics});
$\Mx\subseteq\Mg$, and it is $\Mg$ --- the multi-geodesic part of the
cut locus of $\X$ --- that carries the reconstruction theory.

\emph{(ii) Convexity is not linear.} There is no ``$\conv\X$''; its
role is played by different objects in different curvatures --- the
ordinary hull only in the flat case, a horoconvex hull in
nonpositive curvature, and \emph{nothing at all} on a compact
manifold, where every complement turns out to be reconstructed and
the hull condition is vacuous.

\emph{(iii) The distance function turns.} In $\R^{n}$ the ascending
gradient flow of $\dX$ runs along straight rays until it meets the
bisector; on a manifold the minimising geodesic to $\X$ bends, its
foot slides, and the natural ``margin'' one would monotonically
accumulate along the flow genuinely oscillates as the active contact
set changes (Remark~\ref{rem:notmonotone}). This is the analytic
heart of the difficulty and the reason a naive gradient-flow argument
stalls exactly at the cut points, where it is asked to continue past
a singularity of $\dX$. A further, quieter difficulty compounds it:
$\Mg$ is \emph{not closed} --- minimising geodesics can merge in the
limit at focal degenerations --- so ``the first time the flow meets
the axis'' need not exist.

The resolution, developed below, is to change the question the flow
is asked to answer. Rather than flowing \emph{through} the axis and
tracking a monotone quantity, we flow \emph{up to} the axis and read
off a margin that is exact on the complement of the axis and merely
needs to survive to the \emph{first approach} --- a formulation
indifferent to whether the axis is closed and to everything that
happens at focal and conjugate points. This severs the argument from
every regularity subtlety at cut points, and it is what makes the
theory unconditional.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:results}

The paper proves three groups of theorems: metric, asymptotic, and
topological.

\emph{Capture (\S\ref{sec:capture}).} Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} shows
that along any trajectory of the ascent inclusion
$\Phi'\in-\conv U(\Phi)$, and up to the first approach to $\Mg$, the
distance to $\X$ grows at rate exactly one while the trajectory
itself is $1$-Lipschitz, so the starting point sits at depth
$\ge\dX(p)$ inside every ball centred on the moving trajectory. The
trajectory therefore either approaches $\Mg$ in finite time
(\emph{capture}: $p\in\Rg$, at depth arbitrarily close to $\dX(p)$)
or escapes to infinity (\emph{escape}, impossible on a compact
manifold). Hence Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}: $\Rg=M\setminus\X$ on
\emph{every} compact manifold, for every closed $\X\neq\varnothing$
--- including a single point, which the two-point axis cannot detect
at all. This answers the compact reconstruction question of
\cite[\S5.2]{Bia} affirmatively for the two-geodesic axis, with no
hypothesis whatsoever. A spread refinement
(Corollary~\ref{cor:spread}) gives the two-point statement
$\Rx=M\setminus\X$ on every complete $M$ with
$\sup\dX<\inj(M)$.

\emph{Drift and the characterisation (\S\ref{sec:drift}).} On a
non-compact manifold, capture is not the whole story: a point may be
reconstructed by remote balls whose centres run to infinity while its
own trajectory escapes. Definition~\ref{def:mlf} packages the limits
of these balls as \emph{medial limit functions} --- limits
$\psi=\lim[d(\cdot,a_{k})-\dX(a_{k})]$ of free-ball functions
normalised by their own \emph{reach} rather than by a basepoint ---
and Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow} shows each reconstructs its whole open
sublevel set, in three lines, on every complete manifold.
Theorem~\ref{thm:char} is the resulting characterisation,
\[
  \Rg=\bigcup\bigl\{\{\psi<0\}:\ \psi\ \text{a medial limit
  function}\bigr\},
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} the trichotomy \emph{captured /
drift-swallowed / escaping}, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:reconstructible} the induced reconstructibility
criterion --- the direct analogue, on an arbitrary complete manifold,
of Bia\l{}o\.zyt's Theorem~\ref{thm:bia}. On a Hadamard manifold the
ideal medial limit functions are Busemann functions up to an additive
level, and the reach normalisation absorbs what would otherwise be a
delicate ``radial offset'' bookkeeping into the definition
(Remark~\ref{rem:offset}).

\emph{Specialisations (\S\ref{sec:spec}).} We work out, in
corollaries and worked examples, the flat picture (where the
abstract characterisation aligns with Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem, its
half-space limits identified by his defective-hyperplane
certificate); total
reconstruction on the sphere, on every spherical space form, and on
every compact manifold, together with the two-point theorem on the
round sphere (Corollary~\ref{cor:sphere}); the hyperbolic and
Hadamard pictures (medial limit functions as Busemann functions at a
level); and the non-compact positive-curvature examples where
reconstruction is genuinely partial. Beyond these, we prove a faithful certificate on a
manifold covered by neither Theorem~\ref{thm:bia} nor the Hadamard
formulation: on the flat cylinder --- flat but not simply
connected --- the free
end-region above $\sup_{\X}z$ is reconstructed in full if the top
contact set omits a circle position, and not at all if it is the
full circle (Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder}); a single contact
point suffices, the wrap-around geodesic acting as the second
contact.

\emph{The homotopy layer (\S\ref{sec:homotopy}).} The escape class
of the trichotomy turns out to be exactly the \emph{Aubry set} of
$\dX$ in the sense of Cannarsa--Cheng--Fathi \cite{CCF}
(Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}), so their theorem reads: the captured part
of the complement always has the homotopy type of $\Mg$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}). When $\sup\dX<\infty$ --- every compact
$M$, every coarsely dense $\X$ --- capture supplies the uniform
clock that boundedness of the domain supplied for Lieutier
\cite{Lie} and Albano--Cannarsa--Nguyen--Sinestrari \cite{ACNS},
and $M\setminus\X\simeq\Mg\simeq\Cut(\X)$ on every complete
manifold, components unbounded or not
(Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy}). Two classical geometries fall out:
uniform capture recovers the Ford--Voronoi spines of finite-volume
cusped hyperbolic manifolds --- dual to the Epstein--Penner
decomposition \cite{EP} --- and the Voronoi spines of
crystallographic quotients (Corollary~\ref{cor:spines}); and for a
smooth closed submanifold $\X$ the captured part of the complement
is, with no further hypothesis, the mapping cylinder of the swallow
map onto the cut locus --- a strong deformation retraction, with
the Thom-space collapse of Basu--Prasad \cite{BP} as its inward
dual (Proposition~\ref{prop:thom}). The flat cylinder with
$|\X|=1$ shows the bound $\sup\dX<\infty$ is not cosmetic: the
reconstruction is complete, $\Rg=C\setminus\X$, yet a figure-eight
complement faces a contractible axis
(Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak}) --- \emph{homotopy equivalence
follows capture, and swallowing does not transmit it}.

\emph{The medial complex (\S\ref{sec:complex}).} The final group of
results repairs that leak, unconditionally. The engine is a covering
theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}): \emph{the finite layer
does not leak} --- every swallowed-uncaptured point lies in an
\emph{ideal} free horoball, at depth no smaller than its depth in
any finite medial ball. The proof runs the capture clock along the
transition between the captured set and the Aubry set: either the
clocks of nearby captured points blow up, dragging medial centres to
infinity with the margin intact, or the transition ray grazes the
cut locus --- and then rides it to infinity, with the same effect.
Consequently $\Rg$ is the captured set plus the ideal cells
(Corollary~\ref{cor:idealdecomp}), and the \emph{medial complex} ---
$\Mg$ with the ideal free horoballs glued along the diagram of the
cover --- always carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$, of
$M\setminus\X$ whenever the reconstruction is complete
(Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}): on the cylinder it rebuilds the
figure-eight from a contractible axis and two ideal circles. The
leak the complex repairs is not a curvature phenomenon: three points
in the plane already reconstruct completely while their complement,
a wedge of three circles, faces a contractible Voronoi tripod ---
the loops living in the corner cones where two ideal half-planes
overlap inside the Aubry set (Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak}); for
generic finite planar contact sets the leak has rank exactly the
number of convex-hull corners (Remark~\ref{rem:flatleak}).

Section~\ref{sec:discussion} steps back and states plainly what the
two layers of the theory --- the \emph{unconditional} dynamical
layer, proved here in full, and the \emph{faithful} certificate
layer, of which Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem is the flat instance --- do
and do not deliver, and how the topological results sit across
them. Section~\ref{sec:open} collects what remains open, most of it
on the faithful (escape) side.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related}

The distance function from a closed set, its singular set, and its
gradient dynamics have a substantial literature, and the detailed
comparisons are placed where they bite: with the singular
gradient-flow theorems of Lieutier \cite{Lie} and
Albano--Cannarsa--Nguyen--Sinestrari \cite{ACNS} and the
propagation theory of Cannarsa--Cheng--Fathi \cite{CCF} in
Remark~\ref{rem:lieutier} and \S\ref{sec:homotopy}; with the
critical point theory of distance functions of Grove--Shiohama
\cite{GS,Grv,Che} and the $\lambda$-medial axis of Chazal--Lieutier
\cite{CL} in Remark~\ref{rem:critical}; with the structure theory
of the cut locus of a closed set of Tanaka--Sabau \cite{TS} and
Miura--Tanaka \cite{MT} in \S\ref{sec:prelim}; and with the
cut-locus topology of submanifolds of Basu--Prasad \cite{BP,Pra} in
\S\ref{sec:homotopy}. What is new here, in one sentence each: the
capture theorem extracts a quantitative reconstruction margin, for
an arbitrary closed set in an arbitrary complete manifold, by
\emph{stopping} at the (non-closed) axis where the flow theorems are
designed to continue; the medial limit functions make the escaping
balls a theorem-free limit object on any complete manifold; and the
homotopy results localise the failure of topological faithfulness
in the Aubry set and repair it with the ideal cells of the medial
complex.

\medskip

Throughout, we illustrate the definitions and theorems with explicit
examples in $\R^{2}$ and $\R^{3}$, on the round sphere, in the
hyperbolic plane, on surfaces of revolution, and on the flat
cylinder; the examples are chosen so that every phenomenon the
general theory permits --- capture, finite and ideal swallowing,
escape, shielding, homotopy leak and its repair --- is witnessed by
a computation the reader can check by hand.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

Throughout, $M$ is a complete Riemannian manifold; by the
Hopf--Rinow theorem it is a proper geodesic metric space (closed
balls are compact, and any two points are joined by a minimising
geodesic). $\X\subseteq M$ is closed with $\varnothing\neq\X\neq M$,
and $\dX=\dist(\cdot,\X)$ is the distance to $\X$, a $1$-Lipschitz
function, positive exactly off $\X$. All geodesics are parametrised
by arc length. We state the standing hypotheses once, to prevent a
misreading the subject invites: \emph{complete metric, closed $\X$
--- nothing else}. No curvature assumption of any kind (sign,
constancy, pinching, bounds) appears in
\S\ref{sec:prelim}--\S\ref{sec:drift}; the curvature of $M$ may vary
and change sign freely.

\subsection{The two axes and the two reconstructions}

For $x\in M\setminus\X$ a \emph{minimising geodesic to $\X$} is a
unit-speed geodesic $\gamma:[0,\dX(x)]\to M$ with $\gamma(0)=x$ and
$\gamma(\dX(x))\in\X$; its \emph{foot} is $\gamma(\dX(x))$. Set
\[
  U(x)=\bigl\{\gamma'(0):\ \gamma\ \text{a minimising geodesic to
  }\X,\ \gamma(0)=x\bigr\}\subseteq T^{1}_{x}M,
\]
the set of unit initial directions of such geodesics (a geodesic is
determined by its initial vector, so distinct minimisers have
distinct directions). Define the two medial axes
\[
  \Mx=\{a:\ a\ \text{has}\ \ge2\ \text{nearest \emph{points} in}\
  \X\},\qquad
  \Mg=\{x\in M\setminus\X:\ |U(x)|\ge2\},
\]
the \emph{two-point} and \emph{two-geodesic} axes. Because two
distinct minimising geodesics may share a foot, $\Mx\subseteq\Mg$;
and $\Mg$ is the multi-geodesic part of the cut locus of $\X$ --- in
general a proper dense subset of it, the cut locus also containing
focal points reached by a unique minimiser. Their reconstructions
are
\[
  \Rx=\bigcup_{a\in\Mx}B\bigl(a,\dX(a)\bigr),\qquad
  \Rg=\bigcup_{a\in\Mg}B\bigl(a,\dX(a)\bigr),\qquad
  \Rx\subseteq\Rg,
\]
unions of \emph{open} free balls. When we use $\Mg$ as a target to
be reached we write $\Sigma:=\Mg$; $r(a)=\dX(a)$ is the \emph{reach}
of a centre $a$. In $\R^{n}$ geodesics between points are unique, so
$\Mx=\Mg$ and $\Rx=\Rg$, and the two theories coincide; the split is
a purely curved phenomenon.

\begin{remark}[Why the two-geodesic axis is the right object]
\label{rem:whymg}
The two-point axis $\Mx$ is the intuitive skeleton, but it is blind
to a whole class of reconstructions. A single point
$\X=\{x_{0}\}$ on the round sphere has $\Mx=\varnothing$ --- every
point off $x_{0}$ has the unique nearest point $x_{0}$ --- yet the
antipode $\bar{x}_{0}$ is a genuine medial centre: the entire sphere
minus $x_{0}$ is a free ball about $\bar{x}_{0}$, because
$\bar{x}_{0}$ is joined to $x_{0}$ by a sphere's worth of minimising
geodesics, all of length $\pi$. Thus
$\bar{x}_{0}\in\Mg\setminus\Mx$ and
$\Rg=\Sn\setminus\{x_{0}\}$ while $\Rx=\varnothing$. The
reconstruction theory sees this only through $\Mg$. This example,
trivial as it is, already shows that the compact theorem below
\emph{cannot} hold for $\Mx$ (a single point is never reconstructed
by two-point balls) and \emph{must} be stated for $\Mg$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The direction field}

\begin{lemma}[Direction field: compactness and semicontinuity]
\label{lem:usc}
For every $x\in M\setminus\X$ the set $U(x)$ is nonempty and compact,
and the multimap $x\mapsto U(x)$ is upper semicontinuous: if
$x_{k}\to x$ in $M\setminus\X$ and $u_{k}\in U(x_{k})$, $u_{k}\to u$
(in $TM$), then $u\in U(x)$. In particular $U$ is single-valued and
continuous at every point of $(M\setminus\X)\setminus\Sigma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Nonemptiness and compactness: minimisers exist by properness, and a
limit of initial vectors of minimising geodesics from the same $x$ is
the initial vector of a limit geodesic, which minimises by continuity
of $\dX$ and of lengths. Upper semicontinuity: let
$\gamma_{k}$ be the minimising geodesics with
$\gamma_{k}'(0)=u_{k}$. By smooth dependence of geodesics on initial
conditions, $\gamma_{k}\to\gamma$ locally uniformly where
$\gamma'(0)=u$; the lengths $\dX(x_{k})\to\dX(x)$, and the endpoints
$\gamma_{k}(\dX(x_{k}))\in\X$ converge to $\gamma(\dX(x))\in\X$ ($\X$
closed). So $\gamma$ is a geodesic from $x$ to $\X$ of length
$\dX(x)$: minimising, i.e.\ $u\in U(x)$. At a point with
$U(x)=\{u\}$, upper semicontinuity of a compact-valued multimap into a
singleton forces continuity of every selection near $x$ --- note this
is continuity \emph{at} $x$ only; $U$ may be multivalued arbitrarily
close by.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[First variation of $\dX$; {\cite[\S3]{MM}}]
\label{lem:firstvar}
For $x\in M\setminus\X$ and $w\in T_{x}M$, the one-sided directional
derivative of $\dX$ exists and equals
\[
  \frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0^{+}}\dX\bigl(\exp_{x}(sw)\bigr)
  =\min_{u\in U(x)}\sca{w}{-u}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
``$\le$'': fix $u\in U(x)$ with geodesic $\gamma$, and fix a small
$\delta>0$. The point $\gamma(\delta)$ lies in a normal ball around
$x$, so $q\mapsto d(q,\gamma(\delta))$ is smooth near $x$ with
gradient $-u$ at $x$. Since points of $\gamma$ satisfy
$\dX(\gamma(\delta))=\dX(x)-\delta$,
\[
  \dX\bigl(\exp_{x}(sw)\bigr)
  \ \le\ d\bigl(\exp_{x}(sw),\gamma(\delta)\bigr)+\dX(x)-\delta
  \ =\ \dX(x)+s\,\sca{w}{-u}+O_{\delta}(s^{2}),
\]
so $\limsup_{s\downarrow0}s^{-1}[\dX(\exp_{x}(sw))-\dX(x)]\le
\sca{w}{-u}$ for every $u\in U(x)$, hence $\le$ the minimum.

``$\ge$'': let $s_{k}\downarrow0$ realise the $\liminf$, put
$x_{k}=\exp_{x}(s_{k}w)$, pick $u_{k}\in U(x_{k})$ with geodesics
$\gamma_{k}$, and set $\delta_{k}=\sqrt{s_{k}}$. The broken path
through $\gamma_{k}(\delta_{k})$ gives
$\dX(x)\le d(x,\gamma_{k}(\delta_{k}))+\dX(x_{k})-\delta_{k}$, and in
normal coordinates at $x_{k}$, where
$v_{k}:=\exp_{x_{k}}^{-1}(x)=-s_{k}w+O(s_{k}^{2})$,
\[
  d\bigl(x,\gamma_{k}(\delta_{k})\bigr)
  \ \le\ |\delta_{k}u_{k}-v_{k}|+O(\delta_{k}^{3})
  \ \le\ \delta_{k}-\sca{v_{k}}{u_{k}}+\frac{|v_{k}|^{2}}{2\delta_{k}}
  +O(\delta_{k}^{3})
  \ =\ \delta_{k}+s_{k}\sca{w}{u_{k}}+O(s_{k}^{3/2}),
\]
using $\sqrt{1+\tau}\le1+\tau/2$ and the $O(r^{2})$ metric distortion
of normal coordinates. Hence
$\dX(x_{k})-\dX(x)\ge s_{k}\sca{w}{-u_{k}}-O(s_{k}^{3/2})$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:usc} a subsequence of $u_{k}$ converges to some
$u\in U(x)$, so the $\liminf$ is $\ge\sca{w}{-u}\ge\min_{u\in U(x)}
\sca{w}{-u}$. (Cf.\ \cite[\S3]{MM}, where the semiconcavity of $\dX$
on $M\setminus\X$ --- not needed here --- is also established.)
\end{proof}

Intuitively: $\dX$ increases fastest, at unit rate, in the direction
directly \emph{away} from a nearest foot; at a two-geodesic point the
rate in a given direction $w$ is dictated by the \emph{least
favourable} competing foot, which is what makes the min appear and
what makes the gradient flow turn.

\paragraph{Prior art on the distance from a closed set.} The
dichotomy beneath everything here --- $\dX$ is differentiable
exactly where the foot direction is unique, singular exactly on
$\Sigma$ --- is classical, and its fine structure theory is active.
Tanaka and Sabau \cite{TS} characterise the differentiable points
of the distance from an arbitrary closed subset in Finsler
generality and prove that on surfaces its cut locus is a local
tree, statements new even in the Riemannian case; Miura and Tanaka
\cite{MT} prove a delta-convex structure theorem for the singular
set. The capture argument needs none of this finer structure ---
only Lemmas~\ref{lem:usc} and~\ref{lem:firstvar} above --- but the
structure theory is what a regularity upgrade of the homotopy layer
of \S\ref{sec:homotopy}, and a CW model of the medial complex of
\S\ref{sec:complex} (Remark~\ref{rem:complexlimits}(c)), would draw
on.

\subsection{The ascent inclusion and capture curves}

On the open set $M\setminus\X$ define the multimap
\[
  G(x)\ :=\ \conv\bigl(-U(x)\bigr)\ \subseteq\ T_{x}M ,
\]
the convex hull of the reversed minimising directions. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:usc}, $G$ is upper semicontinuous with nonempty
compact convex values contained in the closed unit ball. A
\emph{capture curve} from $p\in M\setminus\X$ is a locally Lipschitz
curve $\Phi:[0,T_{\max})\to M\setminus\X$ with $\Phi_{0}=p$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:incl}
  \Phi'(t)\in G(\Phi_{t})\qquad\text{for a.e.\ }t ,
\end{equation}
maximally extended in $M\setminus\X$. Local existence of solutions of
\eqref{eq:incl}, and extension as long as the trajectory stays in a
compact subset of the open domain, are the standard theory of
differential inclusions with upper semicontinuous convex compact
right-hand side (Aubin--Cellina \cite[\S2.1]{AC}), applied in charts
and concatenated; $|\Phi'|\le1$ a.e., so trajectories are
$1$-Lipschitz. We emphasise that \emph{no} uniqueness, canonicity, or
semiconcave-gradient selection is needed: any solution serves. (At a
point of $\Sigma$ the inclusion may branch; off $\Sigma$ it is the
classical ODE of the field $-u$.) When $U$ is single-valued along a
trajectory the curve simply extends the current minimising geodesic
beyond $x$, switching fields continuously as the foot slides.

\subsection{Free-ball functions and medial limit functions}

For $a\in M\setminus\X$ set
\[
  \psi_{a}:=d(\cdot,a)-\dX(a)\ :\ M\to\R ,
\]
a $1$-Lipschitz function, negative exactly on the open free ball
$B(a,\dX(a))$, with $\psi_{a}\ge0$ on $\X$. It is normalised by the
reach --- $\min\psi_{a}=-\dX(a)$, attained at $a$ --- rather than by
a basepoint; this intrinsic normalisation, ``measure the ball by how
deep it goes, not by how far its centre is'', is what makes the
limits below basepoint-free, and it is the technical device that
dissolves the offset bookkeeping of the Hadamard theory
(Remark~\ref{rem:offset}).

\begin{definition}[Medial limit functions]\label{def:mlf}
A function $\psi:M\to\R$ is a \emph{medial limit function} of $\X$ if
there are $a_{k}\in\Mg$ with $\psi_{a_{k}}\to\psi$ locally uniformly.
It is \emph{finite} if $(a_{k})$ has a bounded subsequence and
\emph{ideal} otherwise (then $\psi$ is a horofunction-type limit:
$d(a_{k},\cdot)\to\infty$ locally uniformly while
$\psi$ stays finite). Every $\psi$ is $1$-Lipschitz, and
$\psi\ge0$ on $\X$ (each $\psi_{a_{k}}$ is), so $\{\psi<0\}$ is an open
set disjoint from $\X$: the \emph{free horoball} of $\psi$. For finite
$\psi$ along $a_{k}\to a$: if $a\in\Mg$ then $\psi=\psi_{a}$ is a
free-ball function; in general ($\Mg$ is not closed) $\psi=\psi_{a}$
with $a$ a focal-degenerate limit of medial centres, or even a point
of $\X$ itself (medial centres collapsing into a corner of $\X$ with
$\dX(a_{k})\to0$), where $\psi_{a}=d(\cdot,a)$ and the free horoball
is empty --- still usable, by Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}, which never
needs the limit centre itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the horofunction compactification]
\label{rem:horo}
$M$ proper makes $C(M)$ with the topology of locally uniform
convergence a compact home for $1$-Lipschitz functions
normalised at a point (Arzel\`a--Ascoli). The classical horofunction
compactification \cite{Gro,BH} normalises $d(\cdot,a_{k})$ by a
basepoint value; our $\psi$'s differ from it by the scalar
$d(o,a_{k})-\dX(a_{k})$ --- the \emph{offset}. Working with
reach-normalised limits absorbs the offset into the function itself;
see Remark~\ref{rem:offset}. On a Hadamard manifold every ideal
medial limit function is a Busemann function plus a constant
(horofunctions of a proper $\mathrm{CAT}(0)$ space are Busemann
\cite[II.8.13]{BH}), so ideal medial limit functions are exactly
``Busemann function $b_{\xi}$ $+$ level $c$''.
\end{remark}

\section{The capture theorem}\label{sec:capture}

The capture theorem is the engine of the compact case and one of the
two inputs to the general characterisation. Its statement is a clean
dichotomy; its proof is a one-line differential identity valid off
the axis, plus a compactness argument for the escape alternative.

\begin{theorem}[Capture or escape]\label{thm:capture}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed, $p\in M\setminus\X$, and let $\Phi$
be any capture curve from $p$; assume $p\notin\Sigma$ (otherwise $p$
is captured at time $0$: $a=p$ contains $p$ at depth $\dX(p)$ in its
own medial ball). Let
\[
  T\ :=\ \sup\bigl\{t\in[0,T_{\max}):\ \Phi_{s}\notin\Sigma\ \ \forall
  s\in[0,t]\bigr\}\ \in\ [0,T_{\max}]
\]
($T=0$ is possible: $\Sigma$ need not be closed, and the trajectory
may approach it instantly; this lands in case \textup{(i)}). Then on
$[0,T)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rateone}
  \dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(p)+t,
  \qquad
  d(p,\Phi_{t})\le t,
  \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
  \dX(\Phi_{t})-d(p,\Phi_{t})\ \ge\ \dX(p),
\end{equation}
and exactly one of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\emph{(Capture.)} $T<T_{\max}$. Then there exist $a\in\Mg$
arbitrarily close to $\Phi_{T}$ \textup{(}$a=\Phi_{T}$ itself if
$\Phi_{T}\in\Sigma$\textup{)} with
\[
  \dX(a)-d(p,a)\ \ge\ \dX(p)-\varepsilon
  \qquad(\varepsilon>0\ \text{arbitrary}),
\]
so $p\in B(a,\dX(a))$ for such $a$: \quad $p\in\Rg$, at depth
arbitrarily close to $\dX(p)$, and $T\le\operatorname{diam}(M)-\dX(p)$.
\item\emph{(Escape.)} $T=T_{\max}$. Then $T_{\max}=\infty$, the
trajectory never meets $\Sigma$, $\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(p)+t\to\infty$, and
$\Phi_{t}$ leaves every compact subset of $M$. This is possible only
if $M$ is non-compact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The rate-one identity.} Fix $t\in[0,T)$; on $[0,t]$ the
trajectory avoids $\Sigma$, so $U(\Phi_{s})=\{u(\Phi_{s})\}$ is a
singleton for every $s\le t$ and \eqref{eq:incl} reads
$\Phi'(s)=-u(\Phi_{s})$ at a.e.\ $s$. The map $h(s):=\dX(\Phi_{s})$ is
Lipschitz. Let $s$ be any point where both $\Phi'(s)$ exists (with the
inclusion valid) and $h'(s)$ exists --- almost every $s\in[0,t]$. Write
$v=\Phi'(s)=-u(\Phi_{s})$, a unit vector. In a chart around $\Phi_{s}$,
differentiability gives
$d\bigl(\Phi_{s+\sigma},\exp_{\Phi_{s}}(\sigma v)\bigr)=o(\sigma)$ as
$\sigma\downarrow0$, and $\dX$ is $1$-Lipschitz, so
\[
  h(s+\sigma)=\dX\bigl(\exp_{\Phi_{s}}(\sigma v)\bigr)+o(\sigma)
  =h(s)+\sigma\min_{u\in U(\Phi_{s})}\sca{v}{-u}+o(\sigma)
  =h(s)+\sigma\,\sca{-u}{-u}+o(\sigma),
\]
by Lemma~\ref{lem:firstvar} and single-valuedness. Hence
$h'(s)=|u|^{2}=1$ at a.e.\ $s\in[0,t]$, and integrating,
$h(t)=\dX(p)+t$. The bound $d(p,\Phi_{t})\le t$ is the $1$-Lipschitz
property of the trajectory, and the margin inequality in
\eqref{eq:rateone} is their difference. Note that no closedness of
$\Sigma$, and no structure at focal or conjugate points, was used: a
point of $(M\setminus\X)\setminus\Sigma$ in the closure of $\Sigma$ is
simply a point where $U$ is single-valued, and the identity passes
through it.

\emph{Dichotomy.} Suppose first $T<T_{\max}$. If $\Phi_{T}\in\Sigma$,
continuity of $\dX(\Phi_{\cdot})-d(p,\Phi_{\cdot})$ and
\eqref{eq:rateone} on $[0,T)$ give
$\dX(\Phi_{T})-d(p,\Phi_{T})\ge\dX(p)>0$, so $a=\Phi_{T}\in\Mg$
contains $p$ in its open medial ball at depth $\ge\dX(p)$. If
$\Phi_{T}\notin\Sigma$, then by definition of $T$ as a supremum, every
interval $(T,T+\delta]\subseteq[0,T_{\max})$ contains a time
$s_{\delta}$ with $\Phi_{s_{\delta}}\in\Sigma$; letting
$\delta\downarrow0$ and using continuity of the margin,
$a=\Phi_{s_{\delta}}\in\Mg$ satisfies
$\dX(a)-d(p,a)\ge\dX(p)-\varepsilon$ for $\delta$ small. In both cases
$p\in B(a,\dX(a))$ with $a\in\Mg$, i.e.\ $p\in\Rg$; and
$\dX\le\operatorname{diam}(M)$ bounds $T$ via \eqref{eq:rateone}.

Now suppose $T=T_{\max}$: the trajectory never meets $\Sigma$, and
\eqref{eq:rateone} holds on all of $[0,T_{\max})$. If
$T_{\max}<\infty$, then $\Phi([0,T_{\max}))\subseteq
\bar B(p,T_{\max})\cap\{\dX\ge\dX(p)\}$, a compact subset of the open
set $M\setminus\X$ (properness of $M$); by the extension property of
solutions this contradicts maximality. So $T_{\max}=\infty$ and
$\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(p)+t\to\infty$; since $\dX$ is bounded on compact
sets, $\Phi_{t}$ leaves every compact set. On compact $M$, $\dX\le
\operatorname{diam}(M)$ makes this impossible, so case (i) occurs.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Total reconstruction on compact manifolds,
unconditional]\label{cor:compact}
Let $M$ be a compact Riemannian manifold and $\X\subseteq M$ closed,
$\varnothing\neq\X\neq M$. Then
\[
  \boxed{\ \Rg=M\setminus\X\ },
\]
and quantitatively: every $p\in M\setminus\X$ lies at depth
$\ge\dX(p)-\varepsilon$ (any $\varepsilon>0$) inside a medial ball of
$\Mg$. No curvature, genericity, analyticity, spread, or cardinality
hypothesis is required; in particular $|\X|=1$ is allowed, where the
two-point axis has $\Mx=\varnothing$ but the two-geodesic axis still
reconstructs everything.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\Rg\subseteq M\setminus\X$ since medial balls are free. Conversely,
if $p\in\Mg$ then $p\in B(p,\dX(p))\subseteq\Rg$; otherwise start a
capture curve at $p$ (local existence, \S\ref{sec:prelim}); by
Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} escape is impossible on compact $M$, so $p$
is captured.
\end{proof}

In particular the reconstruction question on compact Riemannian
manifolds, raised for the medial axis in \cite[\S5.2]{Bia}, has an
unconditional affirmative answer for the two-geodesic axis.

\begin{corollary}[Two-point axis under spread]\label{cor:spread}
Let $M$ be complete with $\inj(M):=\inf_{q\in M}\inj(q)>0$, and
suppose
\[
  \sup_{M\setminus\X}\dX\ <\ \inj(M)
  \qquad\text{--- e.g.\ $\X$ $\delta$-dense with $\delta<\inj(M)$.}
\]
Then $\Rx=M\setminus\X$ for the two-point axis. Compactness is not
needed: it would enter only to preclude escape, which the spread
hypothesis already does. In $\R^{n}$ ($\inj=\infty$) the statement
recovers the classical fact that every coarsely dense closed set
reconstructs its complement: $\sup\dX<\infty$ implies
$\Rx=\R^{n}\setminus\X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Escape is impossible: along an escaping capture curve,
$\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(p)+t\to\infty$ would exceed $\sup\dX$. So every
$p\in(M\setminus\X)\setminus\Mg$ is captured
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}(i), within time $\sup\dX-\dX(p)$), giving
$a\in\Mg$ with $p\in B(a,\dX(a))$; for $p\in\Mg$ take $a=p$. Two
distinct minimising geodesics from $a$ with the same endpoint
$x_{0}$ would give two distinct vectors of norm
$d(a,x_{0})=\dX(a)<\inj(M)\le\inj(a)$ with the same image under
$\exp_{a}$, contradicting injectivity of $\exp_{a}$ on the open ball
of radius $\inj(a)$. So the two
geodesics of $a$ have distinct endpoints: $a\in\Mx$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why stopping at $\Sigma$, not flowing through it, is the
correct formulation]\label{rem:notmonotone}
Two natural strengthenings of Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} are false, and
identifying their failure is what shaped the proof. First, \emph{the
margin $\dX(\Phi)-d(p,\Phi)$ is not monotone through multi-geodesic
arcs}, already in $\R^{2}$: for $\X=\{(0,0),(0,2)\}$ and $p=(1,0.9)$,
the Lieutier flow reaches the bisector $\{y=1\}$ and slides along it,
and the margin decreases strictly (from $\approx1.345$ toward the
limit $1$, the depth of $p$ in the limiting half-plane). Second, even
the quadratic margin $\dX^{2}-d(p,\cdot)^{2}$, which \emph{is}
monotone along unique-foot and bisector arcs in $\R^{n}$, fails at
turning points where the active contact set changes (a three-point
configuration in $\R^{2}$ suffices: $\X=\{(0,0),(0,2),(10,5)\}$, with
the flow from $p=(1,0.9)$ turning at $\approx(5.75,1)$, where the
derivative of the quadratic margin is negative). Both phenomena live
\emph{on} $\Sigma$; on its complement the rate-one identity
\eqref{eq:rateone} is exact. Since reaching $\Sigma$ is the goal, not
an obstacle, the correct move is to stop there --- capture is
achieved, with the margin intact, \emph{before} the first
multi-geodesic time. The failure of closedness of $\Sigma$ (focal
degenerations, e.g.\ the evolute of an ellipse in $\R^{2}$, or the
conjugate-cusp endpoints of an ellipsoid's cut segment) is equally
harmless: such points are unique-geodesic points, the field $-u$ is
continuous at them (Lemma~\ref{lem:usc}), and the trajectory passes
through with $\dX$ still growing at rate one, the foot sliding
continuously.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the singular gradient flows]
\label{rem:lieutier}
For semiconcave $\dX$ one can canonically select the minimal-norm
element of $G$ (Lieutier's generalised gradient \cite{Lie}, in
$\R^{n}$; \cite{ACa,MM} for the semiconcavity). Two theorems run that
canonical flow \emph{into} the singular set: Lieutier's homotopy
equivalence of a bounded domain with its medial axis \cite{Lie}, and
its Riemannian counterpart by Albano, Cannarsa, Nguyen and Sinestrari
\cite{ACNS}, who prove that the singular set of the boundary distance
of a bounded domain on a Riemannian manifold is invariant under the
generalised gradient flow and deduce the homotopy equivalence of
domain and singular set; see Cannarsa--Cheng--Fathi \cite{CCF} for
the current form of the propagation-of-singularities method behind
such results. The rate-one growth of $\dX$ off the singular set is
classical in that literature; what Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} takes
from the flow is different, and in a sense opposite. Our argument
analyses the inclusion only \emph{off} $\Sigma$, where every
selection agrees with the naive one (extend the unique minimising
geodesic); it imposes no semiconcave or minimal selection --- any
relaxed solution serves; and it \emph{terminates} at $\Sigma$, where
those flows are designed to continue. The deliverable is not a
retraction but the quantitative margin $\dX(a)-d(p,a)\ge\dX(p)$ at
the first approach --- a reconstruction statement with no counterpart
in the homotopy results --- extracted on an arbitrary complete
manifold, for an arbitrary closed $\X$, with the escape alternative
live. This is why no analogue of the swallow property past cut points
is needed: the trajectory is stopped at first approach, and the
reconstruction is read off the margin accumulated strictly before it.
The price --- capture produces a centre only \emph{near} the first
$\Sigma$-approach when $\Phi_{T}\notin\Sigma$ --- costs an
arbitrarily small $\varepsilon$ of depth, invisible to the open-ball
statement. Section~\ref{sec:homotopy} completes the comparison in
the opposite direction: importing their invariance theorem, capture
supplies the uniform clock that boundedness of the domain supplied
in \cite{Lie,ACNS}, extending the homotopy equivalence to every
complete $M$ with $\sup\dX<\infty$; and the Aubry set of \cite{CCF}
turns out to be exactly the escape class of
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}, which locates the failure beyond that
bound.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Capture as critical point theory of $\dX$]
\label{rem:critical}
The inclusion \eqref{eq:incl} is also the engine of the critical
point theory of distance functions of Grove--Shiohama
\cite{GS,Che,Grv}: $q\in M\setminus\X$ is \emph{critical} for
$\dX$ iff $0\in\conv U(q)$, which forces $|U(q)|\ge2$ --- so all
critical points of $\dX$ lie on $\Mg$ --- and the isotopy lemma
deforms sublevel sets across critical-value-free intervals along
precisely such gradient-like curves. In that vocabulary
Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} is an isotopy lemma made quantitative
and unconditional: it assumes no critical-value-free interval ---
the trajectory is stopped at its first approach to the
(non-closed) set $\Mg\supseteq\{\text{critical points}\}$,
whenever that occurs --- and it returns not an isotopy but the
margin $\dX(a)-d(p,a)\ge\dX(p)$: a reconstruction statement. The
min-type Morse theory of Gershkovich--Rubinstein \cite{GR}
structures the same objects on nonpositively curved manifolds,
where germs of $\dX$ are min-type; and in $\R^{n}$ the \emph{weak
feature size} of Chazal--Lieutier \cite{CL} --- the smallest
critical value of $\dX$, below which their $\lambda$-medial axis
carries the homotopy type of the complement --- is the global
critical scale whose pointwise, per-trajectory analogue is the
capture margin. A Riemannian $\lambda$-medial axis built on that
margin is a natural project we do not open here.
\end{remark}

\section{Drift: the swallow, the characterisation, the trichotomy}
\label{sec:drift}

Capture is complete on compact manifolds, but on a non-compact $M$ a
point can be reconstructed by medial balls whose centres go to
infinity while its own capture curve escapes. The primal example is
$\X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}\subseteq\R^{2}$: a point $p$ in an open half-plane
bounded by the line $x_{0}x_{1}$, placed beyond $x_{0}$ at an obtuse
angle, has a capture curve that runs straight to infinity with the
single foot $x_{0}$ --- yet $p$ is swallowed by remote bisector
balls $B(a_{k},r(a_{k}))$: a single sufficiently distant ball
already contains it, and so does every later member of the escaping
sequence $a_{k}\to\infty$. The limit of those balls is a
half-plane, and the correct general home for such limits is the space
of medial limit functions of Definition~\ref{def:mlf}.

\begin{lemma}[Swallow, general and offset-free]\label{lem:swallow}
Let $M$ be any complete Riemannian manifold, $\X$ closed, and $\psi$ a
medial limit function of $\X$. Then
\[
  \{\psi<0\}\ \subseteq\ \Rg .
\]
If the witnessing centres lie in $\Mx$, then $\{\psi<0\}\subseteq\Rx$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\psi=\lim\psi_{a_{k}}$ locally uniformly, $a_{k}\in\Mg$, and let
$q\in\{\psi<0\}$. Then $\psi_{a_{k}}(q)\to\psi(q)<0$, so for large $k$
\[
  d(q,a_{k})-\dX(a_{k})=\psi_{a_{k}}(q)<0,
\]
i.e.\ $q\in B(a_{k},\dX(a_{k}))$ with $a_{k}\in\Mg$: $q\in\Rg$.
\end{proof}

There is no anchoring of feet, no curvature bound, no pinching, no
offset hypothesis: the entire content of the swallowing step ---
which in curved settings historically required delicate control of
where the escaping centres' feet accumulate --- is absorbed into the
normalisation of $\psi_{a}$ by the reach.

\begin{lemma}[Compactness of reconstructing sequences]\label{lem:cpt}
Let $p\in\Rg$, and let $a_{k}\in\Mg$ be any sequence with
$\limsup_{k}\bigl[\dX(a_{k})-d(p,a_{k})\bigr]>0$ (e.g.\ a sequence of
centres whose balls contain $p$ with margin bounded away from $0$; a
single ball repeated qualifies). Then a subsequence of
$(\psi_{a_{k}})$ converges locally uniformly to a medial limit
function $\psi$ with $\psi(p)<0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The functions $\psi_{a_{k}}$ are $1$-Lipschitz and
$\psi_{a_{k}}(p)=d(p,a_{k})-\dX(a_{k})$ is bounded: below by
$-\dX(p)$ (since $\dX(a_{k})\le\dX(p)+d(p,a_{k})$) and above by $0$
along the qualifying subsequence. By Arzel\`a--Ascoli on the proper
space $M$, a further subsequence converges locally uniformly; the
limit is a medial limit function, and
$\psi(p)=\lim\psi_{a_{k}}(p)\le-\limsup[\dX(a_{k})-d(p,a_{k})]<0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Characterisation on an arbitrary complete manifold]
\label{thm:char}
For every complete Riemannian manifold $M$ and every closed
$\X\subseteq M$, $\varnothing\neq\X\neq M$,
\[
  \boxed{\ \Rg\ =\ \bigcup\bigl\{\{\psi<0\}:\ \psi\ \text{a medial
  limit function of}\ \X\bigr\}\ }.
\]
The same identity holds for $\Rx$ and $\Mx$ in place of $\Rg$ and
$\Mg$. (Every finite medial limit function is itself of the form
$\psi_{a}=d(\cdot,a)-\dX(a)$ with $a$ in the closure of $\Mg$ taken
in $M$: a focal-degenerate limit of medial centres, or ---
degenerately --- a point of $\X$, where $\dX(a)=0$ and the sublevel
set is empty. Either way its sublevel set is swallowed by the lemma,
which never needs the limit centre itself.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
``$\supseteq$'' is Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}. ``$\subseteq$'': if
$p\in\Rg$ then $p\in B(a,\dX(a))$ for some $a\in\Mg$, and
$\psi_{a}$ --- a medial limit function via the constant sequence ---
has $\psi_{a}(p)<0$.
\end{proof}

The identity is deliberately economical, and one should read its
force correctly. Its finite members alone give a tautology --- every
ball is its own limit --- so the set equality is not the point. The
point is the pair of facts it packages. \emph{First}, the
\emph{ideal} members reconstruct their entire open sublevel set,
bare, with no anchoring or pinching hypothesis: this is
Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow} applied to horofunction-type limits.
\emph{Second}, combined with Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} it yields a
clean trichotomy.

\begin{theorem}[Trichotomy on a complete manifold]\label{thm:trichotomy}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed, $p\in M\setminus\X$. Exactly one of
the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item\emph{(Captured.)} Some capture curve from $p$ approaches $\Mg$
in finite time. Then $p\in\Rg$, at depth arbitrarily close to
$\dX(p)$.
\item\emph{(Swallowed, uncaptured.)} No capture curve from $p$
approaches $\Mg$ in finite time, but $\psi(p)<0$ for some medial limit
function $\psi$. Then $p\in\Rg$: reconstructed, but only by centres
$p$'s own ascent cannot find --- a remote ball, or a drifting sequence.
\item\emph{(Escaping.)} Neither. Then $p\notin\Rg$, and every capture
curve from $p$ escapes to infinity with $\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(p)+t$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $\Rg=\{p:\ \textup{(1) or (2)}\}$, and on compact $M$
class \textup{(1)} is all of $M\setminus\X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The classes are exclusive and exhaustive by construction. (1)
$\Rightarrow p\in\Rg$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}(i); (2)
$\Rightarrow p\in\Rg$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}. Conversely, if
$p\in\Rg$ then $p\in B(a,\dX(a))$ for some $a\in\Mg$ and the finite
medial limit function $\psi_{a}$ has $\psi_{a}(p)<0$, so $p$ lies in
class (2) whenever it is not in class (1) --- never in class (3).
Finally, a point not in class (1) has every capture curve uncaptured,
hence escaping, by the dichotomy of Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}.
\end{proof}

Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem is not a description of the reconstructed set
but a \emph{reconstructibility criterion}: it says exactly when a
closed set is recovered in full. The trichotomy yields the direct
analogue on any complete manifold, because $\Rg$ is precisely classes
(1)--(2) while class (3) is its complement.

\begin{corollary}[Reconstructibility criterion]
\label{cor:reconstructible}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed, $\varnothing\neq\X\neq M$. The
following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $\X$ is reconstructible: $\Rg=M\setminus\X$.
\item $M\setminus\X\ \subseteq\ \bigcup\bigl\{\{\psi<0\}:\ \psi\
\text{a medial limit function of}\ \X\bigr\}$.
\item Every $p\in M\setminus\X$ is captured or drift-swallowed;
equivalently, the escaping class \textup{(3)} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} is empty.
\item For every $p\in M\setminus\X$ whose capture curves escape to
infinity, some medial limit function $\psi$ satisfies $\psi(p)<0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$(a)\Leftrightarrow(b)$: by Theorem~\ref{thm:char} the right-hand side
of (b) equals $\Rg$, and $\Rg\subseteq M\setminus\X$ always, so (b) is
the reverse inclusion, i.e.\ (a). $(a)\Leftrightarrow(c)$: by
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}, $\Rg$ is exactly classes (1)--(2) and is
disjoint from class (3); since the three classes partition
$M\setminus\X$, $\Rg=M\setminus\X$ iff class (3) is empty.
$(c)\Leftrightarrow(d)$: a point outside class (1) escapes
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}), so it lies in class (2) --- swallowed ---
iff some $\psi$ has $\psi(p)<0$; captured points lie in $\Rg$
regardless, so emptiness of class (3) is exactly (d).
\end{proof}

Two remarks fix its meaning. First, \emph{on a compact manifold the
criterion is vacuously satisfied}: escape is impossible
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}), class (3) is empty, and $\X$ is
reconstructible for free (Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}). The criterion
therefore has content only on non-compact $M$ --- exactly where
Bia\l{}o\.zyt's does, $\R^{n}$ being non-compact. Second, condition
(b) is the \emph{unconditional} form: it refers to the medial limit
functions, hence implicitly to the axis $\Mg$. Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem
is the \emph{faithful} sharpening of (b) in the flat case, replacing
``$M\setminus\X\subseteq\bigcup\{\psi<0\}$'' by the axis-free hull
condition
\[
  \R^{n}\setminus\X\ \subseteq\ \overline{\conv}\,\X\ \cup\!\!
  \bigcup_{L\ \text{defective}}\!\!L^{+}
\]
of Theorem~\ref{thm:bia} (Corollary~\ref{cor:flat}); on a Hadamard
manifold the corresponding faithful sharpening replaces the ideal
sublevel sets by defective horoballs $\{b_{\xi}<-c(\xi)\}$
(\S\ref{sec:hadamard}, Remark~\ref{rem:offset}). Thus
Corollary~\ref{cor:reconstructible} is the general analogue of
Bia\l{}o\.zyt's main theorem, and the passage from (b) to a
checkable-from-$\X$ certificate is precisely the faithful-layer
problem: solved in flat geometry, formulated in Hadamard geometry
(\S\ref{sec:hadamard}), and open in mixed curvature
(\S\ref{sec:open}).

\begin{remark}[The witnesses of class (2)]\label{rem:class2}
Both kinds of witness genuinely occur, already for
$\X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}\subseteq\R^{2}$ and $p$ beyond $x_{0}$ in an open
half-plane, at an obtuse angle to the bisector: every capture curve
from $p$ runs straight to infinity with the single foot $x_{0}$, yet
$p$ lies in a single \emph{finite} bisector ball (and also in the
ideal half-plane limit of the bisector balls). Class (2) is thus
``reconstruction is non-local for $p$'': the certificate cannot be
found by ascending from $p$, which is exactly the phenomenon that
separates the non-compact theory from the compact one and forces the
faithful certificates below to quantify over supporting half-spaces
or horoballs rather than over flow lines.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The Hadamard offset, dissolved]\label{rem:offset}
Let $M$ be Hadamard (complete, simply connected, $\sect\le0$). Ideal
medial limit functions are exactly $b_{\xi}+c$ with $\xi\in\bdry M$
and $c\in\R$ (Remark~\ref{rem:horo}), normalised so that $b_{\xi}$
vanishes on the horosphere of the maximal free horoball $\Hb{}$ at
$\xi$; necessarily $c\ge0$ when $\X$ meets every neighbourhood of
that horosphere. Call $\Hb{}$ \emph{defective at level $c$} if
$b_{\xi}+c$ is a medial limit function; then
Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow} reconstructs $\{b_{\xi}<-c\}$ --- the
horoball shrunk by $c$. Relative to a basepoint $o$, the same drift
carries a scalar ``radial offset''
$\mathrm{off}_{k}=d(o,a_{k})-\dX(a_{k})$ --- the curved form of the
$\beta$ of \S\ref{sec:euclid} --- through the exact identity
$\psi_{a_{k}}=\bigl[d(\cdot,a_{k})-d(o,a_{k})\bigr]+\mathrm{off}_{k}$:
the bracket converges to the basepoint-normalised horofunction, so
the level of the limit is the limit offset, re-based to the
horosphere of $\Hb{}$. What in a
basepoint-normalised treatment looks like a gap --- ``only the deep
part $\{b_{\xi}<-c\}$ is reconstructed when the asymptotic offset
does not vanish'' --- is simply the content of the level: the drift
\emph{is} a level-$c$ certificate and reconstructs exactly
$\{b_{\xi}<-c\}$, while the full-horoball statement is the assertion
that a level-$0$ certificate exists. The exact level
\[
  c(\xi)\ :=\ \inf\{c\ge0:\ b_{\xi}+c\ \text{is a medial limit
  function}\}
\]
is a question about $\X$ and the horosphere geometry, not about the
medial axis. The level vanishes in every instance computed in this
paper --- the flat case (Corollary~\ref{cor:flat}) and the cylinder
ends (Remark~\ref{rem:cylinder}) --- and no example with
$c(\xi)>0$ is known (\S\ref{sec:open}(2)).
\end{remark}

\section{Specialisations and worked examples}\label{sec:spec}

We now specialise Theorems~\ref{thm:char}--\ref{thm:trichotomy} to
the classical geometries and illustrate every key definition with an
explicit example. Throughout, the \emph{unconditional} content ---
the abstract characterisation --- is supplied by the theorems above;
the \emph{faithful} content --- a certificate readable from $\X$ and
$M$ --- is supplied by Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem in the flat case, is
vacuous in the compact case, and is proved here for the ends of the
flat cylinder (\S\ref{sec:cylinder}), the first such certificate
beyond Euclidean space.

\subsection{Euclidean space: Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem as the flat
instance}\label{sec:euclid}

In $\R^{n}$ geodesics between points are unique, so $\Mg=\Mx$ and
$\Rg=\Rx$. We identify the medial limit functions. A finite medial
limit function is a free-ball function $y\mapsto|y-a|-r$ with $a$ a
limit of medial centres. For an \emph{ideal} one, let
$a_{k}\to\infty$ along a unit direction $u$ (i.e.\
$a_{k}/|a_{k}|\to u$) with reaches $r_{k}=\dX(a_{k})$. Then, for
each fixed $y$,
\[
  \psi_{a_{k}}(y)=|y-a_{k}|-r_{k}
  =\bigl(|y-a_{k}|-|a_{k}|\bigr)+\bigl(|a_{k}|-r_{k}\bigr)
  \longrightarrow\ -\sca{y}{u}+\beta,
\]
where the first bracket tends to $-\sca{y}{u}$ (a standard Euclidean
computation) and convergence of $\psi_{a_{k}}$ forces the offset
$|a_{k}|-r_{k}$ to a finite limit $\beta$. So every ideal medial limit
function is \emph{affine},
\[
  \psi(y)=\beta-\sca{y}{u},\qquad\{\psi<0\}=\{\sca{y}{u}>\beta\},
\]
an open half-space with outward normal $u$. Conversely a supporting
half-space limit of medial balls is exactly such a $\psi$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:char} therefore reads, in $\R^{n}$:
\[
  \Rx\ =\ \Bigl(\bigcup\text{finite medial balls}\Bigr)\ \cup\
  \Bigl(\bigcup\bigl\{\text{open half-spaces that are ideal medial
  limits}\bigr\}\Bigr).
\]
This is Bia\l{}o\.zyt's decomposition. The finite balls fill
$\overline{\conv}\,\X\setminus\X$ --- every point of the closed hull
off $\X$ lies in a free medial ball \cite{Bia}; the half-spaces are
the outer half-spaces $L^{+}$ of supporting hyperplanes $L$ that
arise as ideal limits. The analysis of \cite{Bia} identifies
\emph{which} supporting hyperplanes occur: precisely the
\emph{defective} ones, $\X\cap
L\neq\overline{\conv}\,\X\cap L$ (equivalently, by Motzkin's theorem
\cite{Mot}, those carrying a non-Chebyshev point just outside the
contact face). Thus:

\begin{corollary}[Flat specialisation]\label{cor:flat}
In $\R^{n}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:char} together with the two Euclidean
inputs of \cite{Bia} just quoted --- the closed hull off $\X$ is
covered by free medial balls, and the ideal half-space limits are
exactly the outer half-spaces of the defective supporting
hyperplanes --- gives
\[
  \Rx\ =\ \bigl(\overline{\conv}\,\X\setminus\X\bigr)\ \cup\!\!
  \bigcup_{L\ \text{defective}}\!\!L^{+},
\]
and the reconstructibility criterion this yields --- $\X$ is
reconstructible iff
$\R^{n}\setminus\X\subseteq\overline{\conv}\,\X\cup\bigcup_{L}L^{+}$
--- is exactly Theorem~\ref{thm:bia}.
The drift over a defective $L$ runs at level $0$ relative to $L$
($\beta$ equals the support value of $L$): the escaping bisector
centres accumulate their feet on the defective contact face, and
their half-space limit is $L^{+}$ itself, unshrunk. The
escaping class \textup{(3)} of Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} is
exactly Bia\l{}o\.zyt's unreconstructed set --- the rays leaving
$\X$ with a single foot and no defective facet around them.
\end{corollary}

Here the split of labour is exact and instructive: the abstract
characterisation (unconditional) delivers ``$\Rx$ is balls $\cup$
half-space limits''; Bia\l{}o\.zyt's certificate (the faithful
layer) delivers ``the half-space limits are exactly the
defective supporting hyperplanes.'' This is the template the whole
paper follows on other geometries.

\begin{example}[Two points]\label{ex:twopoints}
$\X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}\subseteq\R^{2}$, so $\overline{\conv}\,\X$ is the
segment $[x_{0},x_{1}]$ and the axis $\Mx$ is its perpendicular
bisector. There is exactly one defective supporting line: the line
$\ell$ \emph{through} $x_{0}x_{1}$ itself, on which
$\overline{\conv}\,\X\cap\ell=[x_{0},x_{1}]$ while $\X\cap\ell=
\{x_{0},x_{1}\}$ --- two points, not the whole segment. It supports
from both sides, so it is defective in both orientations, with the two
open half-planes bounded by $\ell$ as outer half-spaces $L^{+}$; these
are the ideal (half-plane) medial limits of the escaping bisector
balls. By Corollary~\ref{cor:flat},
\[
  \Rx=\bigl([x_{0},x_{1}]\setminus\{x_{0},x_{1}\}\bigr)\ \cup\
  \{\text{two open half-planes}\}\ =\
  \R^{2}\setminus\bigl(\ell\setminus(x_{0},x_{1})\bigr),
\]
i.e.\ \emph{everything except the two outer rays} of $\ell$ beyond the
endpoints (together with $\X$ itself). Note what is and is not
reconstructed: the \emph{open} segment $(x_{0},x_{1})$ \emph{is}
reconstructed --- by finite bisector balls, which contain every
interior segment point --- while the two collinear rays $\{x_{0}+
t(x_{0}-x_{1}):t>0\}$ and its mirror are on \emph{no} free ball and are
missed by the open half-planes. The failure of reconstructibility for
a two-point set is exactly the failure to cover these rays. This is
the primal drift example of Remark~\ref{rem:class2}: a point in an
open half-plane at an obtuse angle to the bisector is reconstructed
only through the bisector balls and their ideal half-plane limit
(class (2)), not by its own escaping capture curve.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A triangle: the outer half-spaces close up]
\label{ex:arc}
Let $\X$ be the three vertices of an equilateral triangle, so
$\overline{\conv}\,\X$ is the solid triangle. A supporting line through
a single vertex has $\X\cap L=\overline{\conv}\,\X\cap L=\{v\}$ ---
not defective. A supporting line \emph{along an edge} has
$\overline{\conv}\,\X\cap L$ the whole edge while $\X\cap L$ is its two
endpoints --- defective, with a genuinely nonempty outer half-plane
$L^{+}$ reconstructed by the ideal drift of the escaping bisector
centres of those two vertices (which are legitimate medial centres far
out beyond the edge, the third vertex being strictly farther). The
three edge half-planes \emph{overlap to cover every exterior point} ---
a point beyond a vertex lies in the outer half-plane of an incident
edge --- and the interior is filled by finite balls, so
$\Rx=\R^{2}\setminus\X$: reconstructible. Contrast
Example~\ref{ex:twopoints}, the degenerate ``triangle'' with one edge:
there the single edge's two outer half-planes leave the collinear rays
uncovered, and reconstructibility fails. The moral is that
reconstructibility is about whether the defective outer half-spaces
\emph{tile} the exterior, which a genuine polygon does and a segment
does not. (The same three points return in
Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak}, where complete reconstruction turns
out \emph{not} to imply that the axis carries the homotopy type of the
complement.)
\end{example}

\subsection{Compact manifolds, the sphere, and space forms}
\label{sec:compact}

Here the faithful layer is \emph{vacuous}: on a compact manifold
$\Mg$ is bounded, no sequence of medial centres escapes, there are no
ideal medial limit functions and no hull condition, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:compact} gives total reconstruction outright.

\begin{corollary}[Round sphere and spherical space forms]
\label{cor:sphere}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item For every closed $\varnothing\neq\X\neq\Sn$,
$\Rg=\Sn\setminus\X$; likewise $\Rg=M\setminus\X$ on every spherical
space form $M=\Sn/\Gamma$, for every closed $\X$ --- an instance of
Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}.
\item On the round sphere (constant curvature; radius normalised to
$1$), if moreover $|\X|\ge2$, then $\Mg=\Mx$, and the two-point
axis already reconstructs everything: $\Rx=\Sn\setminus\X$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(a) is Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}. (b) Let $a\in\Mg$ and suppose
two distinct minimising geodesics from $a$ reach the same foot
$x_{0}$. On the round sphere $\exp_{a}$ is injective on the open
ball of radius $\pi$, so two distinct geodesics from $a$ of equal
length $\le\pi$ with a common endpoint force that length to be
$\pi$ and $x_{0}$ to be the antipode of $a$. Then
$\dX(a)=\pi$ and the free ball
$B(a,\pi)=\Sn\setminus\{x_{0}\}$ avoids $\X$, so
$\X\subseteq\{x_{0}\}$ --- against $|\X|\ge2$. Hence the minimising
geodesics of every $a\in\Mg$ have at least two distinct feet:
$\Mg\subseteq\Mx$, so $\Mg=\Mx$, and (a) gives
$\Rx=\Rg=\Sn\setminus\X$.
\end{proof}

Statement (b) is a genuine constant-curvature rigidity --- two
minimising geodesics to a common foot force an antipode, so a cut
point always presents a second foot --- and it fails under variable
positive curvature (Example~\ref{ex:shield}); on a space form
$\Sn/\Gamma$ with $\Gamma\neq\{1\}$ cut points occur below the
diameter and only the two-geodesic statement (a) is claimed.

\begin{example}[A single point on $\Sn$: the antipodal centre]
\label{ex:point-sphere}
$\X=\{x_{0}\}\subseteq\Sn$. Then $\Mx=\varnothing$, so the two-point
theory reconstructs nothing. But the antipode $\bar x_{0}$ is a medial
centre in $\Mg$: it is joined to $x_{0}$ by an $(n-1)$-sphere of
minimising geodesics, all of length $\pi=\dX(\bar x_{0})$, and the free
ball $B(\bar x_{0},\pi)=\Sn\setminus\{x_{0}\}$ swallows everything.
Corollary~\ref{cor:sphere} thus reconstructs the whole complement from
a single point --- the extreme demonstration that $\Mg$, not $\Mx$, is
the reconstruction axis. The capture curve from any $p\ne x_{0},\bar
x_{0}$ ascends the meridian away from $x_{0}$ and reaches $\bar x_{0}$
in time $\pi-\dX(p)$: capture, class (1).
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Cut-shielding and the two-point axis]\label{ex:shield}
Under \emph{variable} positive curvature the two axes genuinely
separate: a capturing centre $a$ may have two minimising geodesics
to a \emph{single} foot (a cut point of $a$ occurring before any
second foot appears), so $a\in\Mg\setminus\Mx$, and a point captured
by $a$ is reconstructed by $\Rg$ but possibly by no two-point ball.
This is the \emph{cut-shielding} obstruction, and it is why the
two-point theorem needs a spread hypothesis
(Corollary~\ref{cor:spread}) or constant curvature
(Corollary~\ref{cor:sphere}(b)), while the two-geodesic theorem
needs nothing. The obstruction is quantitative: on prolate
ellipsoids of revolution, numerical experiment locates a threshold
aspect ratio (polar to equatorial) $c^{\ast}=2.6\pm0.1$ past which
shielding switches on
for a two-point contact set; shielded points are exactly class-(1)
points of $\Rg$ whose capturing centres lie in $\Mg\setminus\Mx$.
An analytic determination of the threshold is open
(\S\ref{sec:open}(3)).
\end{example}

\subsection{Hyperbolic space and Hadamard manifolds}
\label{sec:hadamard}

Let $M$ be Hadamard (complete, simply connected, variable
$\sect\le0$). The ideal medial limit functions are Busemann
functions at a level (Remarks~\ref{rem:horo}
and~\ref{rem:offset}), so Theorem~\ref{thm:char} reads: $\Rg$ is
the union of the finite medial balls and the shrunken horoballs
$\{b_{\xi}<-c(\xi)\}$ over the defective ideal points $\xi$. This is
the exact Hadamard analogue of Corollary~\ref{cor:flat}, with
``half-space'' replaced by ``horoball''.

The \emph{faithful} question --- which ideal points $\xi$ are
defective, and at what level --- is the curved analogue of
Bia\l{}o\.zyt's defective-hyperplane certificate. The natural answer
mirrors the flat one, with the \emph{horoconvex hull} in the role of
the convex hull --- for $K\subseteq\partial\Hb{}$, $\hconv(K)$ is
the trace on the horosphere of the intersection of the horoballs of
$M$ containing $K$, which on a flat horosphere reduces to the
ordinary convex hull: $\xi$ is defective iff the contact set
$\X\cap\partial\Hb{}$ on the maximal free horosphere at $\xi$ fails
to be horoconvex, $\X\cap\partial\Hb{}\neq
\hconv(\X\cap\partial\Hb{})$, with level $c(\xi)=0$. Establishing
this certificate is the horoconvex counterpart of Motzkin's theorem
and is beyond the flat certificate assumed here; we treat it as part
of the faithful-layer programme (\S\ref{sec:open}(1)--(2)) and use,
in the examples, only the unconditional statements proved above.

\begin{example}[Two ideal directions in $\HH^{2}$]\label{ex:hyp}
Let $\X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}$ in the hyperbolic plane. The bisector is a
complete geodesic with two ideal endpoints $\xi_{\pm}\in\bdry\HH^{2}$.
Escaping bisector centres $a_{k}\to\xi_{\pm}$ have reaches
$r_{k}=d(a_{k},x_{0})\to\infty$, and $\psi_{a_{k}}=d(\cdot,a_{k})-r_{k}
\to b_{\xi_{\pm}}$, a Busemann function at level $0$; by the swallow
lemma the two open horoballs $\{b_{\xi_{\pm}}<0\}$ are reconstructed.
Together with the finite bisector balls, which fill a lens-shaped
region around the geodesic segment $[x_{0},x_{1}]$, these exhaust
$\Rg$: every medial centre lies on the bisector, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:char} every reconstructed point lies in a finite
bisector ball or in one of the two horoballs. This is the hyperbolic
mirror of Example~\ref{ex:twopoints}, with ``open half-plane''
replaced by ``open horoball'' --- the escaping centres now run to the
two \emph{ideal points} of the bisector rather than off to Euclidean
infinity, and the swallowed regions are horoballs there. The contact
set on the maximal free horosphere at each $\xi_{\pm}$ is the single
pair $\{x_{0},x_{1}\}$, non-horoconvex --- the horoconvex analogue of
the defective supporting line. How much of the complement the lens
and the two horoballs leave uncovered --- the hyperbolic counterpart
of the two collinear outer rays --- depends on the horoball geometry;
horoballs in $\HH^{2}$ are far thinner than half-planes, and the
uncovered escape class \textup{(3)} is correspondingly larger than
its flat counterpart (see also Remark~\ref{rem:complexlimits}(b)).
The swallow and the characterisation above are, in any case,
unconditional.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[The ideal limit is genuinely needed]
\label{ex:idealneeded}
In both Example~\ref{ex:twopoints} and Example~\ref{ex:hyp} the
drifted region is reconstructed by no ball findable from within: a
point $p$ deep in the open half-plane / horoball, at an obtuse angle
to the bisector, has its capture curve escaping with a single foot
(Remark~\ref{rem:class2}), so it is class (2), reconstructed only by
remote bisector balls, with the ideal limit as the uniform
certificate for the whole region. This is why Theorem~\ref{thm:char}
must include the ideal members and why the pure capture theorem
(Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}) cannot extend to non-compact
manifolds.
\end{example}

\subsection{Non-compact positive curvature}\label{sec:poscurv}

Here reconstruction is genuinely partial and neither the flat nor the
Hadamard certificate applies; the trichotomy still governs.

\begin{example}[Paraboloid of revolution]\label{ex:paraboloid}
Let $M$ be a paraboloid of revolution ($\sect>0$, a single flattening
end) and $\X$ a small circle about the vertex. The vertex is a finite
medial centre reconstructing the inner disk: class (1). Every point on
the flaring side ascends its meridian forever with a single foot, and
no medial limit function reaches it: class (3). Hence
$\Rg\subsetneq M\setminus\X$ --- partial reconstruction, decided by
the non-compactness of $M$, not by the sign of the curvature.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Ideal centres in positive curvature]\label{ex:posideal}
Take $\X=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}$ on the paraboloid. The bisector meridian
is unbounded, and its escaping centres furnish genuinely \emph{ideal}
medial limit functions on a $\sect>0$ manifold. There is no
contradiction with the boundedness phenomena of positive curvature:
it is the maximal free \emph{convex} regions that are bounded under
$\sect>0$, and the sublevel sets $\{\psi<0\}$ are unbounded
\emph{non-convex} free regions. The example shows the ideal class is
not a nonpositive-curvature artefact.
\end{example}

\subsection{The flat cylinder: a faithful certificate beyond
Euclidean space}\label{sec:cylinder}

The simplest manifold outside every certificate above is the flat
cylinder $C=(\R/L\mathbb{Z})\times\R$: flat but not simply
connected, so Theorem~\ref{thm:bia} does not apply and neither does
the Hadamard picture, and non-compact, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:compact} is silent about its ends. Its
horofunction boundary consists of two points --- the compact circle
factor washes out of every limit $d(\cdot,a_{k})-d(o,a_{k})$ --- so
ideal medial limit functions are $\pm z+\mathrm{const}$, attached to
the two ends; write $z$ for the second coordinate and $\pi$ for the
projection to the circle factor.

\begin{proposition}[End certificate on the flat cylinder]
\label{prop:cylinder}
Let $\X\subseteq C$ be closed, $\varnothing\neq\X\neq C$, bounded
above: $z_{+}:=\sup_{\X}z<\infty$. The upper contact set
$\X_{+}:=\X\cap\{z=z_{+}\}$ is nonempty and compact, and exactly one
of the following holds.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item\emph{(Defective end.)} $\X_{+}$ omits a circle position:
$\pi(\X_{+})\neq\R/L\mathbb{Z}$. Then $\Mg$ meets every slice
$\{z=s\}$ for all large $s$, the free-ball functions of such centres
converge locally uniformly (no subsequence needed) to the medial
limit function $\psi=z_{+}-z$, and
\[
  \{z>z_{+}\}\ \subseteq\ \Rg :
\]
the maximal free end-region is reconstructed in full, at level $0$.
\item\emph{(Capped end.)} $\X\supseteq(\R/L\mathbb{Z})\times
\{z_{+}\}$. Then every point above the top circle has a unique
minimising geodesic to $\X$ (the vertical one), no medial ball meets
$\{z>z_{+}\}$, and $\Rg\cap\{z>z_{+}\}=\varnothing$: the free
end-region is not reconstructed at all (in particular no ideal
medial limit function $c-z$ exists).
\end{enumerate}
The lower end is symmetric; an end on whose side $\X$ is unbounded
admits no ideal medial limit function at all ($\psi\ge0$ on $\X$
would fail).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\X$ closed and $\{z_{+}-1\le z\le z_{+}\}$ compact make $\X_{+}$
nonempty and compact; and with
$\Theta_{\varepsilon}:=\pi(\X\cap\{z\ge z_{+}-\varepsilon\})$ the
sets $\Theta_{\varepsilon}$ are compact, nested, and
$\bigcap_{\varepsilon>0}\Theta_{\varepsilon}=\pi(\X_{+})=:
\Theta_{+}$ (a point $(\alpha,a_{\varepsilon})\in\X$ with
$a_{\varepsilon}\to z_{+}$ has limit $(\alpha,z_{+})\in\X$), so
$\Theta_{\varepsilon}\to\Theta_{+}$ in the Hausdorff sense as
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

Two elementary facts about $q=(\theta,s)$ with $s>z_{+}$. First, the
horizontal displacement of any minimising geodesic from $q$ to a
foot is at most $L/2$ in absolute value --- re-wrapping the lift by
$\mp L$ strictly shortens otherwise --- with equality forcing a
second minimising geodesic (both wraps). Hence, comparing with any
top point,
\[
  s-z_{+}\ \le\ \dX(q)\ \le\ \sqrt{(s-z_{+})^{2}+L^{2}/4}
  \ \le\ s-z_{+}+\frac{L^{2}}{8(s-z_{+})}.
\]
Second, every foot $(\alpha,a)$ of $q$ then has
$s-a\le\dX(q)$, i.e.\ $a\ge z_{+}-\varepsilon(s)$ with
$\varepsilon(s):=L^{2}/(8(s-z_{+}))\to0$: feet of high points are
near-top points, and their positions lie in
$\Theta_{\varepsilon(s)}$.

\emph{(i)} Fix $\alpha_{0}$ and $\rho>0$ with the arc of radius
$\rho$ about $\alpha_{0}$ disjoint from $\Theta_{+}$, and $s_{1}$
with $\Theta_{\varepsilon(s)}$ contained in the
$\rho/2$-neighbourhood of $\Theta_{+}$ for $s\ge s_{1}$. Suppose a
slice $\{z=s\}$ with $s\ge s_{1}$ contained no point of $\Mg$. Then
every $(\theta,s)$ has a unique minimising geodesic, whose
horizontal displacement $\delta(\theta)\in(-L/2,L/2)$ (strict, by
uniqueness) is continuous in $\theta$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:usc} with
single values), and the foot position is $\theta+\delta(\theta)$
mod $L$. As $\theta$ increases through one period,
$\theta+\delta(\theta)$ is continuous with total increment $L$, so
by the intermediate value theorem its values cover \emph{every}
circle position --- including the forbidden arc about $\alpha_{0}$:
a contradiction. So $\Mg$ meets every slice $\{z=s\}$, $s\ge s_{1}$;
pick $a_{s}$ there. For $(\theta,z)$ in a fixed compact set and $s$
large, the displacement bound gives
$s-z\le d((\theta,z),a_{s})\le s-z+L^{2}/(8(s-z))$, while
$s-z_{+}\le\dX(a_{s})\le s-z_{+}+L^{2}/(8(s-z_{+}))$; subtracting,
$\psi_{a_{s}}\to z_{+}-z$ locally uniformly. Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}
swallows the sublevel set: $\{z>z_{+}\}\subseteq\Rg$.

\emph{(ii)} If the full top circle lies in $\X$ then for
$q=(\theta,s)$, $s>z_{+}$, the vertical segment to
$(\theta,z_{+})\in\X$ has length $s-z_{+}$, while any geodesic to
any $x\in\X$ has length $\ge s-z(x)\ge s-z_{+}$, with equality
throughout only for that vertical segment: the minimising geodesic
is unique, so $\Mg\cap\{z>z_{+}\}=\varnothing$. And no medial ball
--- centred, therefore, at height $\le z_{+}$ --- contains a point
$q$ above the circle: the minimising geodesic from the centre to $q$
stays in the open ball and crosses
$\{z=z_{+}\}\subseteq\X$, contradicting freeness. So
$\Rg\cap\{z>z_{+}\}=\varnothing$; and an ideal limit $c-z$ would
swallow $\{z>c\}$, so none exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the cylinder teaches]\label{rem:cylinder}
The dichotomy is decided by $\X$ alone --- a faithful certificate,
on a manifold covered by neither Theorem~\ref{thm:bia} nor the
Hadamard formulation. Two degenerations
are forced by the \emph{compact} horosphere. First, Bia\l{}o\.zyt's
``non-convex contact set'' relaxes to ``proper subset of the
horosphere circle'': a \emph{single} contact point suffices for the
two-geodesic drift, the wrap-around geodesic acting as the second
contact. For $\X$ a single point this gives $\Rg=C\setminus\X$ in
full --- the non-compact counterpart of the $|\X|=1$ clause of
Corollary~\ref{cor:compact} --- while $\Mx=\varnothing$ and
$\Rx=\varnothing$ exactly as in $\R^{n}$: on non-simply-connected
manifolds the two axes separate already for singletons. Second, the
level is always $0$ (a drift centre's reach differs from its height
above $z_{+}$ by $O(1/s)$): compact horospheres leave no room for
the offset phenomenon, consistent with the expectation that
$c(\xi)>0$, if it occurs at all, requires inhomogeneous
infinite-volume horospheres (\S\ref{sec:open}(2)). The homotopy
content of the singleton example is recorded in
Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak}: the reconstruction is complete while
$C\setminus\X\not\simeq\Mg$ --- the two swallowed escape rays are
exactly the Aubry set of $\dX$, and they carry the fundamental
group, which the medial complex of \S\ref{sec:complex} recovers
from the two ideal cells.
\end{remark}

\subsection{How much curvature, where}\label{sec:table}

\begin{remark}[The hypotheses, layer by layer]\label{rem:curvhyp}
Since the geometries above range over constant, variable, and merely
sign-constrained curvature, we record exactly what each layer
assumes; ``$\sect\le0$'' etc.\ always means a \emph{sign} condition
on in-general-variable curvature, never constancy, unless said
otherwise.
\begin{itemize}
\item\emph{The dynamical layer} --- capture
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}), swallow (Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}),
characterisation (Theorem~\ref{thm:char}), trichotomy
(Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}), and total reconstruction on compact
$M$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}): \emph{no curvature hypothesis at
all}. Variable curvature of either or mixed sign is covered.
\item\emph{The Hadamard picture} (\S\ref{sec:hadamard}): the sign
condition $\sect\le0$, with variable curvature expressly allowed,
\emph{together with simple connectedness}. The latter is not
cosmetic: it is what identifies horofunctions with Busemann
functions of rays (Remark~\ref{rem:horo}), and on a
non-simply-connected $\sect\le0$ manifold even the horoconvex
formulation is unavailable --- the flat cylinder's end dichotomy
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder}) takes its place there. The
certificate itself is open in both cases (\S\ref{sec:open}(1)).
\item\emph{The knife-edge} (Theorem~\ref{thm:bia}): exact flatness
\emph{and} simple connectedness, i.e.\ $M=\R^{n}$ itself. A flat
cylinder has $\sect\equiv0$ but is not covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:bia} verbatim: its ideal certificates attach to
the two ends, not to a visual sphere of directions --- they are
instead the dichotomy of Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder}.
\item\emph{Constancy of curvature} is essential in exactly one
result above: the two-point theorem on the round sphere
(Corollary~\ref{cor:sphere}(b)). Its mechanism --- two minimising
geodesics to a common foot force an antipode, so a cut point always
presents a second foot --- is a constant-curvature rigidity, and
the conclusion genuinely fails under variable $\sect>0$ (the
prolate-ellipsoid shielding of Example~\ref{ex:shield}). The
two-\emph{geodesic} counterpart needs no curvature at all
(Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}).
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\section{The homotopy layer: capture, the Aubry set, and the limits
of swallowing}\label{sec:homotopy}

Remark~\ref{rem:lieutier} separated the capture theorem from the
canonical singular gradient flow of Lieutier and
Albano--Cannarsa--Nguyen--Sinestrari, whose purpose is homotopy
equivalence. This section runs the two together and settles the
homotopy question for the completed theory: when the axis carries
the homotopy type of the complement, what obstructs it when it does
not, and what either answer has to do with the reconstruction
$\Rg$. Throughout, $\Phi$ denotes the \emph{canonical} flow: the
unique generalised-gradient trajectory of $\dX$ through each point
of $M\setminus\X$ --- Lieutier's flow in $\R^{n}$ \cite{Lie};
existence and uniqueness \cite[Thm.~3.4, Cor.~3.6]{ACNS} in the
Euclidean case and \cite[\S4]{ACNS} on Riemannian domains. The
construction is local, its only input being the local semiconcavity
of $\dX$ on $M\setminus\X$ \cite{ACa,MM}, so it defines $\Phi$ for
every closed $\X$ in every complete $M$; uniqueness gives the
semigroup property. Canonical trajectories satisfy the inclusion
\eqref{eq:incl}, so Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} applies to them
verbatim; and they are global in time: $\dX$ is nondecreasing along
them, so they stay in $M\setminus\X$, and speed $\le1$ together with
completeness of $M$ rules out finite-time breakdown. We import two
further facts, both local, from that literature.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(F\arabic*)}]
\item\label{F1} \emph{Joint continuity:}
$(t,p)\mapsto\Phi_{t}(p)$ is continuous on
$[0,\infty)\times(M\setminus\X)$. (Local semiconcavity makes the
ascending field one-sidedly Lipschitz, and Gr\"onwall gives locally
Lipschitz dependence on the initial point; this is the continuity
that underlies the homotopy statements of \cite{Lie,ACNS}.)
\item\label{F2} \emph{Propagation:} $\Sigma$ is positively invariant
under $\Phi$: once a canonical trajectory is singular it stays
singular \cite[Thms.~3.7 and~4.5]{ACNS}. (Stated there for domains,
under curvature bounds; the proof --- semiconcavity plus a
differential inequality --- is local, and a finite time window of a
trajectory lives in a compact subset of $M\setminus\X$, where the
bounds hold. So it applies verbatim here.)
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}[Capture is permanent; the cut locus is invariant]
\label{lem:perm}
Let $p\in M\setminus\X$ and suppose the canonical trajectory of $p$
is captured, with first-approach time $T$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}\textup{(i)}. Then
\[
  \Phi_{t}(p)\ \in\ \Sigma\qquad\text{for every }t>T :
\]
the trajectory does not merely approach the axis; it enters it and
never leaves. Moreover the cut locus
\[
  \Cut(\X)\ :=\ \overline{\Sigma}\cap(M\setminus\X)
  \qquad(\text{closure in }M)
\]
is positively invariant as well.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\Phi_{T}\in\Sigma$, apply \ref{F2} from time $T$. If
$\Phi_{T}\notin\Sigma$, then by the definition of $T$ every interval
$(T,T+\delta]$ contains a time $s_{\delta}$ with
$\Phi_{s_{\delta}}\in\Sigma$ (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}); \ref{F2} from time $s_{\delta}$ gives
$\Phi_{t}\in\Sigma$ for all $t\ge s_{\delta}$, and
$\delta\downarrow0$ covers all of $(T,\infty)$. For the cut locus:
$q\in\Cut(\X)$ is a limit of $a_{j}\in\Sigma$, so by \ref{F1} and
\ref{F2}, $\Phi_{t}(q)=\lim_{j}\Phi_{t}(a_{j})\in\overline{\Sigma}$;
and $\Phi_{t}(q)\in M\setminus\X$ always.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform capture is a homotopy equivalence]
\label{thm:homotopy}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed, and let $U\subseteq M\setminus\X$
be open and positively invariant under the canonical flow, on which
capture is uniform: for some $D<\infty$ the trajectory of every
$p\in U$ meets $\Sigma$ by time $D$. Then
$\Phi_{D}(U)\subseteq\Sigma\cap U$, and the inclusions
\[
  \Sigma\cap U\ \hookrightarrow\ \Cut(\X)\cap U\ \hookrightarrow\ U
\]
are homotopy equivalences, with $\Phi_{D}$ as homotopy inverse and
the flow itself as the homotopy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi_{D}(p)\in\Sigma$ for $p\in U$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:perm} (the
trajectory meets $\Sigma$ at some $t^{*}\le D$ and stays), and
$\Phi_{D}(p)\in U$ by invariance. Set
$r:=\Phi_{D}\colon U\to\Sigma\cap U$, write
$i\colon\Sigma\cap U\hookrightarrow U$ for the inclusion, and let
$H(p,s):=\Phi_{sD}(p)$, jointly continuous by \ref{F1}. Then $H$ is
a homotopy in $U$ from $\mathrm{id}_{U}$ to $i\circ r$; and $H$
restricted to $(\Sigma\cap U)\times[0,1]$ takes values in
$\Sigma\cap U$ (\ref{F2} and invariance of $U$), so it is also a
homotopy from $\mathrm{id}_{\Sigma\cap U}$ to $r\circ i$. Hence $i$
is a homotopy equivalence. The same two sentences with
$\Cut(\X)\cap U$ in place of $\Sigma\cap U$ --- using the
invariance of the cut locus from Lemma~\ref{lem:perm} and
$\Phi_{D}(U)\subseteq\Sigma\cap U\subseteq\Cut(\X)\cap U$ --- show
that $\Cut(\X)\cap U\hookrightarrow U$ is a homotopy equivalence,
and the inner inclusion follows by two-out-of-three.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bounded distance: topological faithfulness]
\label{cor:homotopy}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed,
$\varnothing\neq\X\neq M$, and suppose
$D:=\sup_{M\setminus\X}\dX<\infty$. Then escape is impossible,
$\Rg=M\setminus\X$, every component $\Omega$ of $M\setminus\X$
meets $\Sigma$, and
\[
  \Sigma\cap\Omega\ \hookrightarrow\ \Cut(\X)\cap\Omega\
  \hookrightarrow\ \Omega
\]
are homotopy equivalences. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item on every compact $M$:
$M\setminus\X\ \simeq\ \Mg\ \simeq\ \Cut(\X)$, unconditionally ---
the closed-set counterpart of the classical retraction of
$M\setminus\{x_{0}\}$ onto $\Cut(x_{0})$;
\item for every coarsely dense closed set in any complete $M$ ---
e.g.\ a periodic $\X\subseteq\R^{n}$ --- the complement, an
\emph{unbounded} domain outside the scope of \cite{Lie,ACNS}, has
the homotopy type of its two-geodesic axis and of its cut locus.
\end{enumerate}
With Corollary~\ref{cor:spread} this completes a pairing:
$\sup\dX<\inj(M)$ makes the two-point reconstruction metrically
complete ($\Rx=M\setminus\X$), and $\sup\dX<\infty$ alone makes the
two-geodesic picture topologically faithful.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Escape forces $\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(p)+t\to\infty$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}(ii)), impossible under the bound; so
every $p\notin\Mg$ is captured, hence $p\in\Rg$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}(i)), and $\Mg\subseteq\Rg$ trivially:
$\Rg=M\setminus\X$. If a canonical trajectory avoided $\Sigma$ on
$[0,\tau]$, the rate-one identity \eqref{eq:rateone} would give
$\dX(p)+\tau\le D$; hence every trajectory meets $\Sigma$ by time
$D-\dX(p)\le D$, and the meeting point lies in the component of its
start. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy} with $U=\Omega$ (open;
invariant, since trajectories are continuous and stay in
$M\setminus\X$). Compact $M$ has $D\le\operatorname{diam}(M)$; a
$\delta$-dense $\X$ has $D\le\delta$.
\end{proof}

Beyond the bound, escape occurs and the statement genuinely changes.
The right language is the \emph{Aubry set} of weak KAM theory, and
the translation is exact.

\begin{lemma}[The Aubry set of $\dX$ is the escape class]
\label{lem:aubry}
Following \cite[Def.~1.4]{CCF}, let
\begin{multline*}
  \Aub(\X)\ :=\ \bigl\{q\in M\setminus\X:\ q=\gamma(t_{0})\
  \text{for some } t_{0}>0\\
  \text{and some arclength }
  \gamma:[0,\infty)\to M\ \text{with}\ \dX(\gamma(t))=t\ \ \forall
  t\ge0\bigr\},
\end{multline*}
the union of the infinite calibrated rays of $\X$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $\Aub(\X)\cap\Sigma=\varnothing$;
\item $\Aub(\X)$ is closed in $M\setminus\X$;
\item a point $p\in M\setminus\X$ lies in $\Aub(\X)$ if and only if
its capture curves escape --- that is, $\Aub(\X)$ is exactly classes
\textup{(2)}$\,\cup\,$\textup{(3)} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}, and
\[
  M\setminus(\X\cup\Aub(\X))\ =\ \{\text{captured points}\}\
  \supseteq\ \Sigma ,
\]
an open set.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $q=\gamma(t_{0})$ lie on a calibrated ray. If $q$ had a
second minimising geodesic $\sigma\neq\gamma|_{[0,t_{0}]}$, the
curve $\sigma$ followed by $\gamma|_{[t_{0},t]}$ would realise
$\dX(\gamma(t))=t$ for $t>t_{0}$ with a corner at $q$, hence could
be strictly shortened --- contradiction. (The same argument shows
that past a point of $\Sigma$ no minimising geodesic keeps
minimising, so calibrated rays avoid $\Sigma$ outright.)

(ii) Let $q_{j}=\gamma_{j}(t_{j})\to q\in M\setminus\X$ with
$\gamma_{j}$ calibrated. Then $t_{j}=\dX(q_{j})\to\dX(q)>0$, and
the feet $\gamma_{j}(0)$ eventually lie in the compact set
$\bar B(q,2\dX(q))\cap\X$. Calibrated rays are unit-speed
minimising geodesics on every initial segment
(\cite[Rem.~1.5]{CCF}); by properness of $M$ a subsequence
converges locally uniformly to an arclength ray $\gamma$, and
$\dX(\gamma(t))=t$ passes to the limit: $q=\gamma(\dX(q))\in
\Aub(\X)$.

(iii) All capture curves from $p\notin\Sigma$ agree until their
first approach to $\Sigma$: off $\Sigma$ the inclusion
\eqref{eq:incl} is the ODE of the single unit field, and the
trajectory is the arclength extension of the minimising geodesic of
$p$ (\S\ref{sec:prelim}). If that trajectory escapes, it never
meets $\Sigma$ and the rate-one identity holds forever; prepending
the minimising segment from the foot of $p$ produces a curve
$\gamma$ with $\dX(\gamma(t))=t$ for all $t\ge0$: an infinite
calibrated ray through $p$, so $p\in\Aub(\X)$. Conversely let
$p=\gamma(t_{0})\in\Aub(\X)$; by (i) applied at each $\gamma(s)$,
$s>0$, the ray avoids $\Sigma$, and since the trajectory off
$\Sigma$ is the extension of the unique minimising geodesic, the
trajectory of $p$ is $t\mapsto\gamma(t_{0}+t)$: it never meets
$\Sigma$, and $\dX\to\infty$ along it --- escape. Points of
$\Sigma$ are captured at time $0$ and excluded from $\Aub(\X)$ by
(i); openness of the captured set is (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[{Cannarsa--Cheng--Fathi \cite[Thm.~1.6]{CCF}, in
the vocabulary of the trichotomy}]\label{thm:ccf}
For every closed $\X\neq\varnothing$ in every complete $M$, the
inclusion
\[
  \Mg\ \hookrightarrow\ M\setminus(\X\cup\Aub(\X))\ =\
  \{\text{captured points}\}
\]
is a homotopy equivalence: \emph{the captured part of the complement
always has the homotopy type of the axis} --- no bound on $\dX$, no
compactness. (Under uniform capture this is
Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy} with $U=M\setminus\X$; in general the
uniform clock fails and the statement is exactly
\cite[Thm.~1.6]{CCF}, translated by Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}. Their
Theorem~1.1 adds that $\Mg$ is always locally contractible.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Homotopy follows capture, not swallowing]
\label{rem:homotopyleak}
The three classes of Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy} now have distinct
topological meanings. Class (1) carries the homotopy type of $\Mg$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}); the Aubry set
$\Aub(\X)=(2)\cup(3)$ is what the axis cannot see; and the swallow
splits $\Aub(\X)$ into its reconstructed part (2) and its free part
(3) --- a \emph{metric} split that the homotopy type ignores. Both
failure modes occur.
\begin{itemize}
\item\emph{Escape unswallowed (class (3) active).} $\X$ the round
circle of radius $R$ in $\R^{3}$: every axis point has the whole
circle as feet, and $\Mg$ is exactly the axis, contractible. The
captured set is the open solid cylinder $\{\rho<R\}$ (each foot-ray
there crosses the axis), which deformation-retracts to $\Mg$ in
accordance with Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}; the Aubry set is the closed
shell $\{\rho\ge R\}\setminus\X$, and its unswallowed part --- the
flange $\{z=0,\ \rho>R\}$, the plane of the circle beyond it --- is
exactly what $\Rg=\R^{3}\setminus(\X\cup\{z=0,\rho\ge R\})$
misses. The homotopy type $\R^{3}\setminus\X\simeq
S^{1}\vee S^{2}$ is created across that shell and is invisible to
the contractible axis.
\item\emph{Escape swallowed (class (2) only) --- complete
reconstruction without homotopy equivalence.} The flat cylinder
with $\X$ a single point (Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder},
Remark~\ref{rem:cylinder}): $\Rg=C\setminus\X$ is complete, yet
$C\setminus\X\simeq S^{1}\vee S^{1}$ while $\Mg$ is the opposite
generator, contractible. The dictionary locates the leak exactly:
$\Aub(\X)$ is the punctured generator through the point --- two
vertical calibrated rays, entirely of class (2), swallowed by the
wrap-around balls, whose non-contractibility (radius beyond the
injectivity radius) is what lets them reconstruct without
transporting homotopy type. The captured set is the open strip
$C\setminus\{\text{generator}\}$, contractible, in accordance with
Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}. Completeness of the reconstruction is a
metric statement; it does not imply topological faithfulness.
\end{itemize}
Under $\sup\dX<\infty$ the Aubry set is empty --- $\dX$ is
unbounded along a calibrated ray --- and the two completions
re-fuse: that is Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy}. Emptiness of
$\Aub(\X)$ is strictly weaker than bounded distance: inside a
parabola in $\R^{2}$ every normal crosses the symmetry axis before
it stops minimising, so $\Aub(\X)$ misses the interior component
entirely and the (unbounded, unboundedly deep) interior is homotopy
equivalent to its medial ray, both contractible. Section
\ref{sec:complex} closes the leak: what the axis cannot see, the
ideal medial limit functions can --- every swallowed-uncaptured
point lies in an ideal free horoball
(Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}), and gluing those cells onto the
axis restores the homotopy type of $\Rg$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}). The leak itself is not a curvature
phenomenon: it recurs for three points in the plane, where complete
reconstruction and a contractible axis coexist with a wedge of
three circles (Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Two classical instances, and the smooth dual side.} The
corollary and proposition that close the section read
Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy} back into classical geometry: uniform
capture \emph{is} the spine theory of cusped hyperbolic and
crystallographic geometry; and for smooth $\X$ the homotopy
equivalence is the boundary of a mapping-cylinder structure whose
inward dual is the Thom space.

\begin{corollary}[Spines: cusped hyperbolic manifolds and
crystallographic quotients]\label{cor:spines}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item Let $M$ be a connected complete hyperbolic manifold of
finite volume with cusps, and let $\X$ be the union of a system of
disjoint closed horoball neighbourhoods of the cusps. Then
$\sup\dX<\infty$ and
\[
  M\ \simeq\ M\setminus\X\ \simeq\ \Mg\ \simeq\ \Cut(\X) ,
\]
where $\Cut(\X)$ is the Ford--Voronoi spine of the system --- the
complex dual to the Epstein--Penner decomposition attached to the
same horoballs \cite{EP}. The spine theorem of cusped hyperbolic
geometry is uniform capture for the horoball contact set.
\item Let $M=\R^{n}/\Gamma$ be a compact flat manifold,
$x_{0}\in\R^{n}$, and $\X=\{\pi(x_{0})\}$ (or any finite set).
Then $\Cut(\X)$ is the projected wall complex of the Voronoi
diagram of the orbit $\Gamma x_{0}$, and
$M\setminus\X\simeq\Mg\simeq\Cut(\X)$. For the square torus and a
single point, $\Cut(\X)$ is the union of the two ``antipodal''
circles --- a figure-eight, matching
$T^{2}\setminus\{x_{0}\}\simeq S^{1}\vee S^{1}$ --- while
$\Mx=\varnothing$: the singleton has no two-point axis at all, and
the two-geodesic axis alone carries the type. The homotopy type
that the flat cylinder's complete reconstruction fails to transmit
(Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak}) is carried honestly the moment the
manifold is compact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(a) $M\setminus\X$ is the open thick-with-tubes part, of compact
closure (Margulis; finite volume), so $\sup\dX<\infty$ and
Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy} applies. For the identification of
the axis, lift to the universal cover $\HH^{n}$: the preimage of
$\X$ is a $\Gamma$-invariant family of disjoint closed horoballs.
A horoball is convex, so a point outside it has a unique nearest
point on it, joined by a unique minimising geodesic; hence two
minimising geodesics to $\X$ end on two \emph{distinct} horoballs
of the lifted family, and $\Sigma$ is the wall set of the Voronoi
decomposition of $\HH^{n}$ determined by the family --- whose
quotient closure is, by definition, the Ford--Voronoi spine. (The
two feet may well project to the \emph{same} point of $\X$ in $M$,
so it is $\Mg$, not $\Mx$, that carries the spine --- the
two-geodesic axis is again the right object.) Finally
$M\simeq M\setminus\X$: the closed cusp neighbourhoods retract
onto their horospherical boundaries, and the closed thick part
onto the open one along a collar. (b) One level down: the lift of
$\X$ is the orbit $\Gamma x_{0}$ (a Delone set; finitely many
orbits for a finite set), $\Sigma$ projects from the walls of its
classical Voronoi diagram, and $\dX$ is bounded by the covering
radius, so Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy} applies. On
$\R^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}$ with $x_{0}=0$: the Voronoi cell is the unit
square centred at $0$, its boundary projects to the two circles
$\{x=\tfrac12\}\cup\{y=\tfrac12\}$, meeting only at
$(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$: a wedge of two circles.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Smooth contact set: the captured part is a
mapping cylinder]\label{prop:thom}
Let $\X$ be a closed embedded $C^{\infty}$ submanifold of the
complete manifold $M$, $S(\nu)$ its unit normal bundle,
\[
  \rho(v)\ =\ \sup\{t>0:\ \dX(\exp(tv))=t\}\ \in\ (0,\infty]
\]
the cut time, $S_{\mathrm{fin}}=\{\rho<\infty\}$, and
$\sigma:S_{\mathrm{fin}}\to M\setminus\X$,
$\sigma(v)=\exp(\rho(v)v)$, the \emph{swallow map}, whose image
$\Cu(\X):=\sigma(S_{\mathrm{fin}})$ is the classical cut locus.
Then $\Aub(\X)=\{\exp(tv):\rho(v)=\infty,\ t>0\}$, and the normal
exponential descends to a homeomorphism
\begin{multline*}
  \mathrm{Cyl}(\sigma)\ :=\ \bigl(S_{\mathrm{fin}}\times(0,1]\
  \sqcup\ \Cu(\X)\bigr)\big/\bigl[(v,1)\sim\sigma(v)\bigr]
  \ \xrightarrow{\ \approx\ }\
  M\setminus\bigl(\X\cup\Aub(\X)\bigr),\\
  (v,t)\mapsto\exp\bigl(t\rho(v)\,v\bigr) :
\end{multline*}
for smooth $\X$, with no further hypothesis, \emph{the captured
part of the complement is the mapping cylinder of the swallow
map}, and it strong deformation retracts onto $\Cu(\X)$. In
particular $\Sigma\subseteq\Cut(\X)\subseteq\Cu(\X)$, the
inclusion $\Sigma\hookrightarrow\Cu(\X)$ is a homotopy equivalence
for every smooth closed $\X$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf} and
two-out-of-three) --- the equivalence of \cite{CCF} sharpened, on
the smooth case, to a retraction onto the full cut locus ---
and $\Cut(\X)=\Cu(\X)$ when $\dim\X=0$, by Bishop's theorem
\cite{Bis}. If $\Aub(\X)=\varnothing$ --- equivalently
$\rho<\infty$ everywhere, in particular whenever $\sup\dX<\infty$
--- then $M\setminus\X$ itself is the cylinder, and under uniform
capture the whole chain
$\Sigma\hookrightarrow\Cut(\X)\hookrightarrow\Cu(\X)
\hookrightarrow M\setminus\X$ consists of homotopy equivalences.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Minimising geodesics from a point to $\X$ arrive orthogonally
(first variation), so every $p\in M\setminus\X$ is
$\exp(\dX(p)v)$ for some $v\in S(\nu)$ with $\dX(p)\le\rho(v)$;
the calibration interval $\{t:\dX(\exp(tv))=t\}$ is $(0,\rho(v)]$
(closed at $\rho$ by continuity); and $\rho(v)=\infty$ makes
$t\mapsto\exp(tv)$ an infinite calibrated ray, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry} identifies $\Aub(\X)$ with the union of the
open rays $\{\exp(tv):t>0\}$, $\rho(v)=\infty$. Positivity of
$\rho$ is the tubular neighbourhood theorem; continuity of
$\rho:S(\nu)\to(0,\infty]$ is the classical continuity of the cut
time, proved for submanifolds as for points (see \cite{BP} and the
references there, and \cite[III.4]{Sak} for a point).

Write $F(v,t)=\exp(t\rho(v)v)$ on $S_{\mathrm{fin}}\times(0,1]$,
continuous since $\rho$ is finite and continuous on the open set
$S_{\mathrm{fin}}$. \emph{Image and surjectivity:} a captured $p$
outside $\Sigma$ has a unique foot direction $v$, and
$\rho(v)=\infty$ would put $p\in\Aub(\X)$; so
$p=F(v,\dX(p)/\rho(v))$. A point $p\in\Sigma$ has each of its
directions $v$ stopped at $p$ --- past a point of $\Sigma$ no
minimising geodesic keeps minimising
(Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}(i)) --- so $\rho(v)=\dX(p)$ and
$p=\sigma(v)$: thus $\Sigma\subseteq\Cu(\X)$ and $F$ maps onto the
captured part; and the image avoids $\Aub(\X)$, since a point of
$\Aub(\X)\cap\mathrm{im}\,F$ would carry two distinct minimising
directions and lie in $\Sigma$, while
$\Aub(\X)\cap\Sigma=\varnothing$. \emph{Injectivity off $t=1$:} if
$p=F(v,t)$ with $t<1$ then $\dX(p)<\rho(v)$; a second
representation would again put $p\in\Sigma$ and force
$\dX(p)=\rho$, a contradiction --- the same argument shows
$F(v,t)\notin\Cu(\X)$ for $t<1$. Hence $F$ descends to a
continuous bijection
$\bar F:\mathrm{Cyl}(\sigma)\to
M\setminus(\X\cup\Aub(\X))$. \emph{Properness:} let $K$ be a
compact subset of the captured part, $0<\varepsilon\le\dX\le C$ on
$K$. Feet of points of $K$ lie in the compact
$\X_{K}=\{x\in\X:\dist(x,K)\le C\}$; if $v_{k}\in
S_{\mathrm{fin}}|_{\X_{K}}$ contribute to $\bar F^{-1}(K)$ and
$v_{k}\to v$ with $\rho(v)=\infty$, then the limit point
$\lim F(v_{k},t_{k})=\exp(sv)$ with
$s=\lim t_{k}\rho(v_{k})\in[\varepsilon,C]$ would lie in
$\Aub(\X)\cap K$ --- impossible; so the directions stay in a
compact subset of $S_{\mathrm{fin}}$ on which $\rho\le R$, the
$F$-preimage of $K$ is a closed subset of that compactum
$\times\,[\varepsilon/R,1]$, and $\Cu(\X)\cap K$ is compact
because $\Cu(\X)$ is closed in the captured part (if
$\sigma(v_{k})\to q$ captured, feet confined give $v_{k}\to v$
after a subsequence, $\rho(v_{k})=\dX(\sigma(v_{k}))\to\dX(q)$
finite, so by continuity $\rho(v)=\dX(q)$ and $q=\sigma(v)$). A
proper continuous bijection onto a locally compact Hausdorff space
is a homeomorphism; the cylinder coordinate
$H_{s}(v,t)=(v,\,t+s(1-t))$, the identity on $\Cu(\X)$, is a
strong deformation retraction. Finally
$\Cut(\X)\subseteq\Cu(\X)$: if $a_{k}\in\Sigma\to q\in
M\setminus\X$, pick feet $x_{k}$ of $a_{k}$ with normal directions
$w_{k}\in S(\nu)$; points of $\Sigma$ sit at their own cut time
(the corner argument above), so
$\rho(w_{k})=\dX(a_{k})\to\dX(q)$; the feet are confined, so after
a subsequence $w_{k}\to w$, and continuity of $\rho$ gives
$\rho(w)=\dX(q)<\infty$ and $q=\sigma(w)\in\Cu(\X)$. (In
particular $\overline{\Sigma}$ avoids $\Aub(\X)$ for smooth $\X$
--- a statement we do not claim for general closed $\X$, where no
continuous cut time is available: the merging of directions that
keeps $\Mg$ from being closed is not, by itself, excluded from
happening along a calibrated ray.) For
$\dim\X=0$, Bishop's theorem \cite{Bis} applied at the strictly
nearest point gives $\Cu(\X)\subseteq\overline{\Sigma}$, whence
$\Cut=\Cu$. The final chain is Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy} plus the
retraction just built plus two-out-of-three.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The two flows, and the Thom space]\label{rem:bp}
Proposition~\ref{prop:thom} is the outward half of a picture whose
inward half is due to Basu and Prasad \cite{BP} (see also the
survey \cite{Pra}): for a closed embedded submanifold $\X$ of a
complete $M$, the square $\dX^{2}$ is Morse--Bott on
$M\setminus\Cu(\X)$, its gradient flow deformation-retracts
$M\setminus\Cu(\X)$ onto $\X$, and for closed $M$ the collapse
$M/\Cu(\X)$ is the Thom space of the normal bundle of $\X$. The
same normal geodesics thus carry three retractions: inward to
$\X$ below the cut time \cite{BP}; outward to $\Cu(\X)$ across it
(Proposition~\ref{prop:thom}); and --- the only one that survives
when $\X$ is an arbitrary closed set --- the canonical flow into
the axis, which turns at $\Sigma$ instead of stopping
(Theorem~\ref{thm:homotopy}). For smooth $\X$ the three compute
the homotopy type of every space in sight from medial data:
$M\setminus(\X\cup\Aub)\simeq\Cu(\X)$,
$M\setminus\Cu(\X)\simeq\X$, $M/\Cu(\X)\cong\mathrm{Th}(\nu)$. On
the flat cylinder with $|\X|=1$ (Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak})
the picture is visible whole: $\rho=\infty$ exactly at the two
vertical directions --- the Aubry rays --- and the captured strip
is the mapping cylinder of
$\sigma:S^{1}\setminus\{\pm\}\to\{\text{opposite generator}\}$.
Two further notes. \emph{(i) Stability:} $\Cu(\X)$ is
Hausdorff-stable under $C^{2}$ perturbations of the metric and of
the submanifold \cite{Bh}, so the cylinder structure is a robust
model, in the spirit of the $\lambda$-medial axis \cite{CL}.
\emph{(ii) Retraction versus equivalence:} for arbitrary closed
$\X$, even Lieutier's Euclidean equivalence \cite{Lie} is, to our
knowledge, not known to improve to a deformation retraction ---
the obstruction is the continuity of a canonical centre selection
--- so the smooth case, where the retraction is explicit and
strong, is genuinely special.
\end{remark}

\section{The medial complex: the ideal cells close the leak}
\label{sec:complex}

Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak} located the failure of topological
faithfulness precisely: the axis carries the homotopy type of the
captured class (1), and what it cannot see is the Aubry set
$(2)\cup(3)$, which the swallow splits by a metric criterion the
homotopy type ignores. The flat cylinder suggested the repair ---
glue the free horoballs of the ideal medial limit functions onto the
captured part --- and the theorem to hope for is that the resulting
\emph{medial complex} always carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$.
This section proves it, on every complete manifold, for every
closed $\X$, with no hypothesis. The
entire content is a covering theorem: \emph{the finite layer does
not leak}. Every point of class (2) --- swallowed, but invisible to
its own ascent --- lies in an \emph{ideal} free horoball, at depth
no smaller than its depth in any finite medial ball. Granting that,
the captured set and the ideal free horoballs form an open cover of
$\Rg$, the homotopy colimit of its diagram is $\Rg$ by the gluing
calculus of Dugger--Isaksen \cite{DI}, and the captured vertex is
identified with $\Mg$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}.

Throughout, $W:=M\setminus(\X\cup\Aub(\X))$ denotes the captured
set, open by Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}, and for
$p\in M\setminus\X$ and $a\in M$ we write
\[
  u_{p}(a)\ :=\ \dX(a)-d(a,p),
\]
the \emph{margin of $p$ at $a$}: $u_{p}(a)>0$ with $a\in\Mg$ says
exactly that the medial ball of $a$ swallows $p$ at depth
$u_{p}(a)$. As a difference of $1$-Lipschitz functions, $u_{p}$ is
$2$-Lipschitz in $a$ (and in $p$).

\begin{lemma}[Margin transport]\label{lem:transport}
Let $x\in M\setminus\X$, let $\Phi$ be a capture curve from $x$, and
let $T=\sup\{t:\Phi_{s}\notin\Sigma\ \forall s\le t\}$ be its first
approach to the axis as in Theorem~\ref{thm:capture}. Then for every
$p\in M$ the margin $t\mapsto u_{p}(\Phi_{t})$ is nondecreasing on
$[0,T]$ and $2$-Lipschitz on all of $[0,T_{\max})$:
\[
  u_{p}(\Phi_{t})\ \ge\ u_{p}(x)\quad(t\le T),
  \qquad
  |u_{p}(\Phi_{t})-u_{p}(\Phi_{s})|\ \le\ 2|t-s| .
\]
Moreover, on any interval $[0,t]$ on which $\Phi$ avoids $\Sigma$,
the curve is the arclength extension of the minimising geodesic of
$x$: capture curves are unique, and are calibrated geodesics, until
their first approach.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On $[0,T)$ the rate-one identity \eqref{eq:rateone} gives
$\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(x)+t$, while $d(\Phi_{t},p)\le d(x,p)+t$ since
$\Phi$ is $1$-Lipschitz; subtracting, $u_{p}(\Phi_{t})\ge u_{p}(x)$,
and continuity extends this to $t=T$. The Lipschitz bound is the
$2$-Lipschitz property of $u_{p}$ along a $1$-Lipschitz curve. For
uniqueness: on an avoidance interval,
$\dX(\Phi_{t})=\dX(x)+t$ and $d(x,\Phi_{t})\le t$ force
$d(x,\Phi_{t})=t$ (as $\dX(\Phi_{t})\le\dX(x)+d(x,\Phi_{t})$), so
$\Phi|_{[0,t]}$ has length $t=d(x,\Phi_{t})$ and is a minimising
geodesic; the concatenation [foot of $x$]$\to x\to\Phi_{t}$ has
length $\dX(x)+t=\dX(\Phi_{t})$, hence minimises, hence has no
corner at $x$: $\Phi|_{[0,t]}$ is the geodesic extension of the
minimising geodesic of $x$, as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cut rays: grazing trajectories ride the cut locus to
infinity]\label{lem:cutray}
Let $z\in\Aub(\X)\cap\Cut(\X)$. Then the (unique) capture curve
$\Phi$ from $z$ --- the shift of the calibrated ray of $z$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}(iii) --- satisfies
\[
  \Phi_{t}(z)\ \in\ \Aub(\X)\cap\Cut(\X),\qquad
  \dX(\Phi_{t}(z))=\dX(z)+t ,
  \qquad\text{for every }t\ge0 :
\]
an escaping ray that touches the closure of the axis stays in it
forever.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}(iii) and its proof, every capture curve
from $z$ is the arclength shift of the calibrated ray of $z$; it
never meets $\Sigma$, and the rate-one identity holds for all time.
In particular the \emph{canonical} trajectory of
\S\ref{sec:homotopy} is this ray shift. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:perm} the cut locus
$\Cut(\X)=\overline{\Sigma}\cap(M\setminus\X)$ is positively
invariant under the canonical flow, so $\Phi_{t}(z)\in\Cut(\X)$; and
forward points of a calibrated ray lie in $\Aub(\X)$ by definition.
\end{proof}

Whether $\Aub(\X)\cap\Cut(\X)$ can be nonempty for some closed $\X$
--- merging medial directions accumulating along an escaping
calibrated ray --- is the regularity question already flagged in
Proposition~\ref{prop:thom} (it is empty for smooth $\X$, by
continuity of the cut time; the structure theory of \cite{TS,MT} is
the natural tool for the general case). The point of
Lemma~\ref{lem:cutray} is that the theorem below does not need to
know: a grazing ray is not an obstruction but a certificate, since
it drags medial centres with it to infinity.

\begin{theorem}[Ideal swallow: the finite layer does not leak]
\label{thm:idealswallow}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed, and let $p\in\Aub(\X)\cap\Rg$ ---
a swallowed, uncaptured point, class \textup{(2)} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}. Then for \emph{every} finite witness
$a_{0}\in\Mg$ with $m_{0}:=u_{p}(a_{0})>0$ there is an \emph{ideal}
medial limit function $\psi$ with
\[
  \psi(p)\ \le\ -m_{0}\ <\ 0 :
\]
$p$ lies in an ideal free horoball, at depth at least its depth in
any finite medial ball.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\gamma$ be the calibrated ray of $p$, $p=\gamma(t_{0})$ with
$t_{0}=\dX(p)$, and let $\eta:[0,L]\to M$, $L=d(a_{0},p)$, be a
minimising geodesic from $a_{0}$ to $p$; it stays in the free ball
$B(a_{0},\dX(a_{0}))\subseteq M\setminus\X$. Concatenate:
\[
  \sigma(s):=\eta(s)\ (0\le s\le L),\qquad
  \sigma(L+t):=\gamma(t_{0}+t)\ (t\ge0).
\]
Along $\eta$, $d(\eta(s),p)=L-s$ and $\dX(\eta(s))\ge\dX(a_{0})-s$
give $u_{p}(\sigma(s))\ge m_{0}$; along the ray,
$\dX(\gamma(t))=t$ and $d(\gamma(t),p)=t-t_{0}$ give
$u_{p}=t_{0}\ge m_{0}$ (the second inequality because
$u_{p}(\sigma(\cdot))\ge m_{0}$ persists to $s=L$ by continuity,
where $u_{p}(p)=\dX(p)=t_{0}$). So
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigmamargin}
  u_{p}(\sigma(s))\ \ge\ m_{0}\qquad\text{for all }s\ge0 .
\end{equation}
The endpoint data are opposite: $\sigma(0)=a_{0}\in\Sigma$ is
captured (at time $0$), while $p=\sigma(L)\in\Aub(\X)$; the captured
set is open and $\Aub(\X)$ is closed in $M\setminus\X$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}), so, using \eqref{eq:sigmamargin},
\[
  s^{\ast}:=\inf\,\sigma^{-1}(\Aub(\X))\ \in\ (0,L],\qquad
  x_{\ast}:=\sigma(s^{\ast})\in\Aub(\X),\qquad
  u_{p}(x_{\ast})\ge m_{0},
\]
and $\sigma(s)$ is captured for every $s<s^{\ast}$.

\emph{Case A (the transition grazes the cut locus):} suppose there
are $s_{j}\uparrow s^{\ast}$ with
$\sigma(s_{j})\in\overline{\Sigma}$. Then
$x_{\ast}\in\Cut(\X)\cap\Aub(\X)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cutray} the
points $w_{t}:=\Phi_{t}(x_{\ast})$ lie in $\Cut(\X)$ with
$\dX(w_{t})=\dX(x_{\ast})+t\to\infty$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:transport} (the trajectory never meets $\Sigma$, so
$T=\infty$) $u_{p}(w_{t})\ge u_{p}(x_{\ast})\ge m_{0}$. Pick
$a_{t}\in\Sigma$ with $d(a_{t},w_{t})\le\min(1,1/t)$: then
$a_{t}\in\Mg$,
\[
  u_{p}(a_{t})\ \ge\ m_{0}-2/t,\qquad
  \dX(a_{t})\ \ge\ \dX(x_{\ast})+t-1/t\ \to\ \infty .
\]
Since $\dX$ is bounded on bounded sets, $(a_{t})$ has no bounded
subsequence. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cpt} a subsequence of
$(\psi_{a_{t}})$ converges locally uniformly to a medial limit
function $\psi$ with $\psi(p)=\lim(-u_{p}(a_{t}))\le-m_{0}$, and
$\psi$ is ideal. Done.

\emph{Case B (the transition is clean):} otherwise there is
$s_{1}<s^{\ast}$ with $\sigma(s)\notin\overline{\Sigma}$ for
$s\in[s_{1},s^{\ast})$. Take $s_{j}\uparrow s^{\ast}$ in
$[s_{1},s^{\ast})$ and set $x_{j}:=\sigma(s_{j})$: captured, not in
$\overline{\Sigma}$. Each $x_{j}$ has a capture curve $\Phi^{j}$
with finite first approach $T_{j}$, and by the dichotomy proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:capture} there are times
$t_{j}\in[T_{j},T_{j}+1/j]$ with
$b_{j}:=\Phi^{j}_{t_{j}}\in\Sigma=\Mg$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:transport},
\[
  u_{p}(b_{j})\ \ge\ u_{p}(x_{j})-2/j\ \ge\ m_{0}-2/j,
  \qquad
  \dX(b_{j})\ \ge\ \dX(x_{j})+T_{j}-1/j .
\]
If $(T_{j})$ is unbounded, pass to a subsequence with
$T_{j}\to\infty$: then $\dX(b_{j})\to\infty$, and exactly as in Case
A, Lemma~\ref{lem:cpt} produces an ideal $\psi$ with
$\psi(p)\le-m_{0}$. If $(T_{j})$ is bounded, pass to a subsequence
$T_{j}\to\bar T<\infty$. On $[0,T_{j})$ each $\Phi^{j}$ avoids
$\Sigma$, hence (Lemma~\ref{lem:transport}) is the geodesic
extension of the minimising geodesic of $x_{j}$. Since
$x_{\ast}\notin\Sigma$ ($\Aub(\X)\cap\Sigma=\varnothing$), the
direction field $U$ is single-valued and continuous at $x_{\ast}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:usc}), so the extensions converge locally uniformly
to the extension of the minimising geodesic of $x_{\ast}$ --- which
is the calibrated ray of $x_{\ast}$ --- and, as
$d(b_{j},\Phi^{j}_{T_{j}})\le1/j$,
\[
  b_{j}\ \longrightarrow\
  z:=\Phi_{\bar T}(x_{\ast})
  \ \in\ \Aub(\X)\cap\overline{\Sigma},
  \qquad
  u_{p}(z)=\lim u_{p}(b_{j})\ge m_{0} :
\]
a grazing point with full margin. Case A applied at $z$ (its
hypothesis is exactly $z\in\Aub(\X)\cap\Cut(\X)$) finishes the
proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ideal decomposition of the reconstruction]
\label{cor:idealdecomp}
For every complete $M$ and closed $\X$,
\[
  \boxed{\ \Rg\ =\ W\ \cup\ \bigcup\bigl\{\{\psi<0\}:\ \psi\
  \text{an \emph{ideal} medial limit function of }\X\bigr\}\ }
\]
--- the captured set plus the ideal layer; the finite medial balls
are needed only where capture already operates. On the Aubry set the
trichotomy is decided by the ideal functions alone:
\[
  \textup{class (2)}=\Aub(\X)\cap\bigcup_{\psi\,\mathrm{ideal}}
  \{\psi<0\},
  \qquad
  \textup{class (3)}=\Aub(\X)\setminus\bigcup_{\psi\,\mathrm{ideal}}
  \{\psi<0\} :
\]
a point escapes unswallowed if and only if it is Aubry and no ideal
medial limit function is negative on it. In $\R^{n}$ this sharpens
Theorem~6 of \cite{Bia}: the half-space layer alone covers the
entire drift-swallowed set.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$W\subseteq\Rg$ is Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy}(1), the horoballs
are swallowed by Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}, and conversely
$\Rg\setminus W=\Aub(\X)\cap\Rg$ is covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}. The class identities follow since
$\Aub(\X)\cap W=\varnothing$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The medial complex carries the reconstruction]
\label{thm:complex}
Let $M$ be complete, $\X$ closed, and let $\Psi$ be any family of
ideal medial limit functions whose free horoballs cover
$\Aub(\X)\cap\Rg$ (by Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow} the family of
\emph{all} of them qualifies). Then
\[
  \mathcal{U}\ :=\ \{W\}\ \cup\
  \bigl\{\{\psi<0\}:\ \psi\in\Psi\bigr\}
\]
is an open cover of $\Rg$, and the natural map from the homotopy
colimit of its \v{C}ech diagram (the members and their finite
intersections) to $\Rg$ is a weak equivalence \cite{DI} --- a
homotopy equivalence, since these spaces are ANRs. Since
$\Mg\hookrightarrow W$ is a homotopy equivalence
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}), the \emph{medial complex} --- $\Mg$
together with the ideal free horoballs, glued along the pattern of
their overlaps with the captured set and with one another ---
carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$; and of $M\setminus\X$ whenever
the reconstruction is complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Covering is Corollary~\ref{cor:idealdecomp} together with
$\{\psi<0\}\subseteq\Rg$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:swallow}) and
$W\subseteq\Rg$; all members are open. The homotopy-colimit
statement for an arbitrary open cover is \cite{DI}; open subsets of
manifolds and their homotopy colimits have CW type, so the weak
equivalence upgrades by Whitehead. The last sentence substitutes the
CCF equivalence at the captured vertex.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Faithfulness without an ideal layer]
\label{cor:noideal}
If $\X$ admits no ideal medial limit function --- e.g.\ whenever
$\Mg$ is bounded, so that no sequence of medial centres escapes ---
then class \textup{(2)} is empty, $\Rg=W$, and
\[
  \Rg\ \simeq\ \Mg :
\]
the two-geodesic axis carries the homotopy type of everything it
reconstructs. This extends the faithfulness regime of
Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy} beyond bounded distance: $\dX$ may be
unbounded and the Aubry set nonempty, as on the paraboloid of
Example~\ref{ex:paraboloid} ($\Mg$ the vertex, $\Rg$ the inner disk, both
contractible, the entire flaring end escaping), or for the round
circle in $\R^{2}$ ($\Mx$ the centre, $\Rx$ the open disk).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Class (2) $=\Aub(\X)\cap\bigcup_{\mathrm{ideal}}\{\psi<0\}
=\varnothing$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:idealdecomp}; so
$\Rg=W\simeq\Mg$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The examples, reassembled.} On the flat cylinder with
$\X$ a point (Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak}): $W$ is the open strip,
$\Mg$ the opposite generator, and the two ideal cells are the open
half-cylinders $\{\pm z>0\}$, each a homotopy circle, with
$\{z>0\}\cap\{z<0\}=\varnothing$ and each $W$-overlap an open square,
contractible. The complex is the double mapping cylinder
\[
  S^{1}\ \longleftarrow\ \ast\ \longrightarrow\
  \ast\ \longleftarrow\ \ast\ \longrightarrow\ S^{1}
  \ \ \simeq\ \ S^{1}\vee S^{1} ,
\]
the figure-eight: exactly $C\setminus\X$. The leak of
Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak} is not an anomaly of the theory but
the visible size of its ideal cells. For the round circle in
$\R^{3}$: $W=\{\rho<R\}$ is the captured solid cylinder, the two
ideal cells are the half-spaces $\{\pm z>0\}$, all three
contractible with contractible overlaps, so the complex is
contractible --- and indeed $\Rg=\R^{3}\setminus(\X\cup
\{z=0,\rho\ge R\})$ is star-shaped about the centre. Here
$M\setminus\X\simeq S^{1}\vee S^{2}$ differs from $\Rg$: the complex
is faithful to the \emph{reconstruction}, and the discrepancy is
exactly class (3), the unswallowed flange. For two points in
$\R^{2}$: strip plus two half-planes, all contractible over
contractible overlaps; $\Rg$, the plane minus two closed rays, is
contractible. In all three, finitely many maximal cells suffice as
$\Psi$; Theorem~\ref{thm:complex} is indifferent to the choice.

\begin{remark}[What the complex decides, and what it does not]
\label{rem:complexlimits}
(a) \emph{Levels.} Any covering family $\Psi$ computes the same
homotopy type, so the complex never needs the optimal level: the
level function $c(\xi)$ of Remark~\ref{rem:offset} (item (2) of
\S\ref{sec:open}) is untouched, as is the detection question ---
\emph{which} ideal functions exist is still read from $\Mg$, not
from $\X$ (items (1)--(2)).
(b) \emph{The flat no-leak conjecture, reduced --- and refuted.} In
$\R^{n}$ every ideal medial limit function is affine and its cell
$H=\{\psi<0\}$ is an open half-space avoiding $\X$; along a
calibrated ray $\gamma(t)=x_{\gamma}+tu$ the affine function
$t\mapsto\psi(\gamma(t))$ starts nonnegative
($\psi\ge0$ on $\X$) and is monotone, so a ray that enters $H$ never
leaves: $\Aub(\X)\cap H$ is a bundle of inward half-lines. The
conjecture that $\Rx=\R^{n}\setminus\X$ forces
$\R^{n}\setminus\X\simeq\Mx$ is therefore equivalent, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}, to the statement that these attachments
are trivial: $H\cap W\hookrightarrow H$ \emph{and its
finite-intersection versions} are weak equivalences. For a single
cell both spaces are contractible candidates --- $H$ is convex, and
$H\cap W$ is $H$ minus the half-line bundle --- and on the acute
triangle below the single-cell attachments are indeed trivial. The
conjecture fails anyway: Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak} exhibits
double overlaps $H'\cap H''$ that sink entirely into the Aubry set,
and three points in the plane already leak all of their homotopy.
On a Hadamard manifold the corner mechanism is throttled by the
thinness of horoballs --- a calibrated ray not asymptotic to the
cell's ideal point eventually exits it, so three points in
$\HH^{2}$ do not even reconstruct completely --- and which complete
Hadamard reconstructions leak is open.
(c) \emph{Regularity.} When the cover is good --- all members and
intersections contractible, as below the convexity radius, or for
all of $\R^{n}$ if (b) holds --- the nerve theorem \cite{Bor}
replaces the homotopy colimit by a simplicial complex, and Damon's
skeletal calculus \cite{Dam} realises exactly this
reconstruction-from-medial-data programme for compact generic
regions in $\R^{3}$; the local-tree and delta-convex structure of
\cite{TS,MT} is what a CW model of the complex would be built on.
(d) \emph{Where the classes live.} The escape flow
$E_{t}(x):=\Phi_{t}(x)$ restricts to $\Aub(\X)$ (unique rays, no
branching), is continuous there by Lemma~\ref{lem:usc}, and
$E_{R}$ is a homotopy inverse to
$\Aub(\X)\cap\{\dX\ge R\}\hookrightarrow\Aub(\X)$ for every $R$:
classes (2)$\,\cup\,$(3) are carried entirely at infinity, which is
why only ideal objects can see them --- the geometric content of
Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The flat leak: three points refute the no-leak
conjecture]\label{prop:flatleak}
Let $\X\subset\R^{2}$ be three affinely independent points. Then
the reconstruction is complete, and
\[
  \Rx\ =\ \R^{2}\setminus\X\ \simeq\ S^{1}\vee S^{1}\vee S^{1},
  \qquad\text{while $\Mx$ is the Voronoi tripod, contractible.}
\]
Complete reconstruction does not transport homotopy type even in
$\R^{2}$, even for $|\X|=3$: the flat no-leak conjecture of
Remark~\ref{rem:complexlimits}(b) is false.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $e$ for a generic edge of the triangle, $\ell_{e}$ for its
line, $n_{e}$ for its outward unit normal and $m_{e}$ for its
midpoint. $\Mx$ is the Voronoi skeleton of three points --- the
circumcentre with its three outward bisector rays --- a tripod.

\emph{Ideal cells.} Along the outward bisector ray the centres
$a_{t}=m_{e}+tn_{e}$ have, for all large $t$, exactly the endpoints
of $e$ as feet and $\dX(a_{t})=\sqrt{t^{2}+h^{2}}$, $2h=|e|$ (for an
obtuse triangle the opposite vertex is the nearer foot for small
$t$ on one edge, which changes nothing in the limit); hence
$d(x,a_{t})-\dX(a_{t})\to\psi_{e}(x):=\sca{m_{e}-x}{n_{e}}$ locally
uniformly, an \emph{ideal} medial limit function whose cell
$H_{e}=\{\psi_{e}<0\}$ is the open half-plane beyond $\ell_{e}$.

\emph{The Aubry set.} In $\R^{n}$ the unit-speed ray $t\mapsto p+tv$
from $p\in\X$ is calibrated iff
$\bigcup_{t>0}B(p+tv,t)=\{x:\sca{x-p}{v}>0\}$ avoids $\X$, i.e.\ iff
$v$ lies in the normal cone $N_{p}$ of $\conv\X$ at $p$; and every
Aubry point lies on such a ray through its foot
(Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}). Hence
$\Aub(\X)=\bigcup_{i}\bigl(p_{i}+N_{p_{i}}\bigr)$, three closed
corner cones.

\emph{Complete reconstruction.} Fix a vertex $p$, let $n',n''$ be
the normals of its two edges, so
$N_{p}=\operatorname{cone}(n',n'')$ and $p\in\ell'\cap\ell''$. If
$v=an'+bn''$ with $a,b\ge0$ had $\sca{v}{n'}\le0$ and
$\sca{v}{n''}\le0$, then
$|v|^{2}=a\sca{n'}{v}+b\sca{n''}{v}\le0$, so $v=0$. Thus every
$v\in N_{p}\setminus\{0\}$ has, say, $\sca{v}{n'}>0$, and since
$p\in\ell'$,
\[
  \psi'(p+tv)\ =\ -t\,\sca{v}{n'}\ <\ 0\qquad(t>0):
\]
the ray lies in the adjacent cell from the instant it leaves $p$.
So $\Aub(\X)\subseteq H_{1}\cup H_{2}\cup H_{3}$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:idealdecomp} gives
$\Rg\supseteq W\cup\Aub(\X)=\R^{2}\setminus\X$, i.e.\
$\Rx=\Rg=\R^{2}\setminus\X$ ($\Mx=\Mg$ in $\R^{n}$).

Finally $\R^{2}$ minus three points is a wedge of three circles,
and the tripod is contractible.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Anatomy of the flat leak]\label{rem:flatleak}
(a) \emph{The corner mechanism.} For an acute triangle the failure
is isolated exactly where Remark~\ref{rem:complexlimits}(b) did not
look. Each single-cell attachment is trivial: $W\cap H_{e}$ is the
strip between the two corner cones flanking $e$, contractible, as
is $H_{e}$. But two cells adjacent at a vertex $p$ overlap in the
nonempty open cone
$H'\cap H''=p+\{v:\sca{v}{n'}>0,\ \sca{v}{n''}>0\}\subseteq
p+N_{p}\subseteq\Aub(\X)$ (the containment because the normals
subtend an angle $\ge90^{\circ}$): the double overlap sinks
\emph{entirely} into the Aubry set, $W\cap H'\cap H''=\varnothing$,
and the finite-intersection attachment fails as badly as possible.
The cover $\{W,H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}$ is good (all nonempty pieces
convex or strips; $H_{1}\cap H_{2}\cap H_{3}=\varnothing$ since the
weighted outward normals sum to zero), so the nerve theorem
applies: the nerve is the complete graph $K_{4}$ --- four
contractible vertices, six edges, every triangle missing --- which
is a wedge of three circles. Theorem~\ref{thm:complex} thus
computes $\Rx$ exactly; it is $W\hookrightarrow\Rx$ that fails.
The three leaked classes are localised by winding: a loop winding
once about $p$ must cross the unbounded cone
$p+N_{p}\subseteq\Aub(\X)$, so no captured loop winds about any
vertex, while the cycle $W\to H'\to H''\to W$ around $p$ winds
once. For an \emph{obtuse} triangle the cover is not even good: the
obtuse corner's overlap cone protrudes past its normal cone, the
protrusion is captured near the apex and Aubry far out (inside the
opposite vertex's cone), and both $W\cap H'\cap H''$ and one
$W\cap H_{e}$ become disconnected --- the goodness threshold of
item~(5b) of \S\ref{sec:open} is visible already for triangles:
the ideal cover of three points is good precisely when no angle is
obtuse.

(b) \emph{The leak count for finite sets.} Let $\X\subset\R^{2}$ be
finite and affinely spanning, $k=|\X|$, with no point of $\X$ in
the relative interior of a hull edge, and let $V$ be the number of
hull vertices. Consecutive hull vertices bound an unbounded Voronoi
edge, so every hull edge is defective as above; the corner
computation of Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak} covers every normal
cone, and $\Rx=\R^{2}\setminus\X\simeq\bigvee_{k}S^{1}$. On the
other side $\Mx$ is the Voronoi skeleton, whose first Betti number
is the number of \emph{bounded} Voronoi cells, i.e.\ of interior
points of $\X$. The leak therefore has rank exactly $V\ge3$: one
class per hull corner. Complete reconstruction is generic for
finite planar sets; what fails, by exactly the number of Aubry
corners, is transport.

(c) \emph{Corners are the mechanism.} The notched half-plane
$\X=\{y\le0,\ |x|\ge1\}$ has $\Aub(\X)=\{y>0,\ |x|\ge1\}$, the
vertical rays over the shelf, and no hull corner; the single ideal
cell $\{y>0\}$ (centres $(0,t)$, feet $(\pm1,0)$) covers the Aubry
set at level $0$, the reconstruction is complete, and there is no
leak: $\R^{2}\setminus\X$ and $\Mx=\{x=0\}$ are both contractible.
Separately, the \emph{single-cell} attachment can itself fail
further afield: for $\X=$ unit circle $\cup$ the plane $\{z=-10\}$
in $\R^{3}$, the cell $H=\{z>0\}$ is defective (centres on the
circle--plane bisector bowl escape with
$\psi\to-z$), its Aubry trace is the cylinder shell
$\{\rho=1,z>0\}$, and $H\cap W$ = (open tube) $\sqcup$ (outside) is
disconnected --- though there the reconstruction is incomplete
below the plane.

(d) \emph{The moral, inverted.} The leak of
Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak} is not a curvature phenomenon: it is
affine, generic, and quantified by the corners of the convex hull.
What the cylinder adds is only that the cells themselves can be
homotopy circles; the flat leak builds its loops from contractible
cells glued around a corner. Lieutier's theorem is genuinely a
theorem about bounded domains --- completeness of the
reconstruction does not restore it in the unbounded setting --- but
the medial complex does, everywhere (Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}).
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion: the layers of the theory}\label{sec:discussion}

It is worth stating plainly what has been achieved and what the
shape of the theory is, because the metric and the topological
statements are easy to conflate.

\emph{The unconditional layer is complete.} On every complete
Riemannian manifold and for every nonempty closed proper $\X$, the
reconstruction $\Rg$ is exactly the union of the sublevel sets of the
medial limit functions (Theorem~\ref{thm:char}); every point is
captured, drift-swallowed, or escaping (Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy});
and reconstruction is total on every compact manifold
(Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}). No curvature bound, genericity,
analyticity, spread, or cardinality hypothesis appears anywhere in
this layer. The two ideas that make it work are worth isolating:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Stop at the axis, do not flow through it.} The margin one
would like to accumulate is not monotone through multi-geodesic
points (Remark~\ref{rem:notmonotone}); but the axis is the
\emph{goal}, so stopping at its first approach both avoids the
non-monotonicity and delivers capture with the margin intact. This
is what frees the compact theorem from any swallow property past cut
points, and from every regularity question at focal and conjugate
degenerations.
\item \emph{Normalise limits by the reach.} Measuring a free ball by
its depth $\dX(a)$ rather than by the distance to a fixed basepoint
makes the limit objects basepoint-free and absorbs the historical
``offset'' into the function itself (Remarks~\ref{rem:horo}
and~\ref{rem:offset}). The swallow lemma then costs three lines on
\emph{any} complete manifold.
\end{itemize}

\emph{The faithful layer is curvature-specific and only partly
known.} Replacing ``$\psi$ is a medial limit function'' by a
certificate readable from $\X$ and the geometry of $M$ is exactly the
content of Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem in the flat case
(Corollary~\ref{cor:flat}) and of the end dichotomy on the flat
cylinder (Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder}); it is vacuous on compact
manifolds, has a natural horoconvex formulation on Hadamard
manifolds (\S\ref{sec:hadamard}), and is open in mixed curvature
(\S\ref{sec:open}). The honest summary is: the \emph{dynamics} of
reconstruction are understood in full generality; the
\emph{combinatorics} of which asymptotic directions carry a defect
are understood exactly where a Motzkin-type theorem is available,
and Bia\l{}o\.zyt's theorem is the flat instance of such a theorem.

\emph{The topological layer cuts across both.} Homotopy type is
transported by capture and by nothing else: the captured part of the
complement always has the homotopy type of the axis
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}), the equivalence is total when
$\sup\dX<\infty$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy}), and swallowing ---
metrically complete as it may be --- transmits nothing
(Remark~\ref{rem:homotopyleak}: the flat cylinder; three points in
the plane). The medial complex is the exact repair: the finite layer
does not leak (Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}), so the axis with the
ideal free horoballs glued on carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$
unconditionally (Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}). Note the asymmetry of
the two failure classes: class (3) is invisible to $\Rg$ but
perfectly visible to the ideal layer, being exactly the Aubry points
on which every ideal medial limit function is nonnegative
(Corollary~\ref{cor:idealdecomp}); class (2) is reconstructed by
$\Rg$ but its homotopy is carried only by the complex. And the leak
is affine, not Riemannian --- the corner cones of a convex hull
already produce it (Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak}) --- so the
complex is not a tax paid for curvature: it is the correct object
even in $\R^{n}$, where Lieutier's bounded-domain theorem does not
extend to unbounded complements.

\section{What remains}\label{sec:open}

The theory is now unconditional in its dynamical form on every
complete Riemannian manifold, and, with \S\ref{sec:complex}, in its
homotopy form as well. What remains open is exactly the
\emph{faithful} layer --- certificates decidable from $\X$ and the
geometry of $M$, with $\Mg$ absent --- together with the regularity
of the medial complex; items (1)--(2) and (5) live on the
non-compact (escape) side, and (3)--(4) are their compact-frontier
companions.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item\emph{Certificates in mixed curvature.} For which ideal points
(horofunctions) $h$ of a general non-compact $M$ is $h+c$ a medial
limit function, read from the contact structure of $\X$ on
$\{h=0\}$? Known: $\conv$/Motzkin in $\R^{n}$ \cite{Bia,Mot}, and
the flat cylinder, whose end certificate is the sharp dichotomy of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder}; vacuous on compact $M$; formulated,
via the horoconvex hull, on Hadamard manifolds
(\S\ref{sec:hadamard}), where proving the horoconvex counterpart of
Motzkin's theorem is the first item of business. Unknown already
for a complete surface with both signs of curvature; and unwritten
for the cusped hyperbolic surface (horocyclic cross-sections
shrinking up the cusp) and the general flat quotient
$\R^{n}/\Gamma$ (toral and mixed ends), which should follow the
cylinder's two steps --- horofunction ends, then a wrap-around
forcing argument --- but are outside the theorems as stated.
\item\emph{The level function.} On an inhomogeneous Hadamard
manifold, characterise
$c(\xi)=\inf\{c:\ b_{\xi}+c\ \text{medial limit}\}$ from $\X$ alone
(Remark~\ref{rem:offset}); the level vanishes in every instance
computed here, and no example with $c(\xi)>0$ is known --- deciding
whether the deep-part phenomenon of Remark~\ref{rem:offset}
actually occurs is the sharpest open question on the Hadamard
side.
\item\emph{The two-point axis in variable curvature.} Quantify
cut-shielding (Example~\ref{ex:shield}): the prolate threshold
$c^{\ast}=2.6\pm0.1$ awaits an analytic determination, and more
generally the class of compact $M$ with $\Rx=M\setminus\X$ for
every closed $\X$ with $|\X|\ge2$ (the round sphere qualifies,
Corollary~\ref{cor:sphere}(b); bounded aspect ratio conjecturally).
\item\emph{Chebyshev problems on the frontier.} The detection
questions the method isolates at its boundary: Motzkin's theorem in
Carnot groups ($\mathbb{CH}^{n}$ horospheres) and in Alexandrov
spaces of indefinite curvature (pinched horospheres); and the
domain (Lieutier) problem on $\Sn$, where the boundary's positively
curved intrinsic geometry becomes the detection object.
\item\emph{Regularity of the medial complex.} The homotopy question
across the Aubry set is settled by \S\ref{sec:complex}: the medial
complex --- $\Mg$ with the ideal free horoballs glued on --- always
carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}),
because the finite layer does not leak
(Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}). What remains is its structure.
\emph{(a) The flat leak, localised:} the flat no-leak conjecture is
settled, negatively --- three points in the plane reconstruct
completely ($\Rx=\R^{2}\setminus\X$, the sets characterised by
\cite[Thm.~6]{Bia}) and leak all three of their loops past a
contractible axis (Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak}); for finite
planar $\X$ the leak has rank exactly the number of hull corners,
and it dies without corners (Remark~\ref{rem:flatleak}). What
survives is localisation: is the leak in $\R^{n}$ likewise carried
by the non-smooth strata of $\partial\conv\X$, and which pairs of
homotopy types $(\R^{n}\setminus\X,\ \Mx)$ are realised by
completely reconstructing sets? \emph{(b) A CW or nerve model:}
when is the ideal cover good, so that the nerve theorem \cite{Bor}
computes the complex (for three points:\ iff the triangle has no
obtuse angle, Remark~\ref{rem:flatleak}(a)) --- and can the
local-tree and delta-convex structure theory of \cite{TS,MT}, or
Damon's skeletal calculus \cite{Dam}, produce a finite model in the
generic compact-contact case? \emph{(c) Grazing rays:} can
$\Aub(\X)\cap\Cut(\X)\neq\varnothing$ actually occur for a closed
(necessarily non-smooth, Proposition~\ref{prop:thom}) contact set?
Lemma~\ref{lem:cutray} shows the theory is indifferent, but the
example, or its impossibility, would calibrate how wild the
transition between capture and escape can be.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Conclusion.} Bia\l{}o\.zyt's Theorem~6 generalises to
every complete Riemannian manifold, in two layers. The
\emph{unconditional} layer is proved here in full: reconstruction by
the two-geodesic axis is exactly the union of the sublevel sets of
the medial limit functions (Theorem~\ref{thm:char}); every point is
captured, swallowed, or escaping (Theorem~\ref{thm:trichotomy});
capture is total on compact manifolds
(Corollary~\ref{cor:compact}), with no hypotheses whatsoever beyond
completeness and closedness. The \emph{faithful} layer --- replacing
``is a medial limit'' by a certificate on contact sets --- is exact
on the flat knife-edge ($\conv$, Theorem~\ref{thm:bia}), proved on
the flat cylinder (the end dichotomy of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cylinder}), formulated on Hadamard manifolds
($\hconv$, \S\ref{sec:hadamard}), and trivial on compact manifolds;
its extension into mixed curvature, together with the Chebyshev-set
problems it generates, is the genuine residue. The two analytic
obstructions that block the passage from $\R^{n}$ --- the
containment step past cut points and the radial offset --- are
removed, the first by stopping the ascent at its first approach to
the axis instead of flowing through it, the second by normalising
the limit objects by their own reach. A topological layer sits on
top: the captured part of the complement always has the homotopy
type of the axis (Theorem~\ref{thm:ccf}, via the Aubry dictionary of
Lemma~\ref{lem:aubry}), totally so when $\sup\dX<\infty$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:homotopy}); the flat cylinder separates the two
completions --- swallowing finishes the reconstruction across the
Aubry set, but only capture transports homotopy type --- and the
medial complex re-fuses them: the finite layer does not leak
(Theorem~\ref{thm:idealswallow}), so the axis with its ideal free
horoballs glued on carries the homotopy type of $\Rg$ on every
complete manifold, for every closed contact set
(Theorem~\ref{thm:complex}); the leak the complex repairs is already
flat --- three points in the plane leak all of their homotopy past a
contractible axis, one loop per hull corner
(Proposition~\ref{prop:flatleak}) --- and the residue of the
homotopy programme is regularity, not existence
(\S\ref{sec:open}(5)). In the smooth case the layer is classical
geometry made medial: the captured part is the mapping cylinder of
the swallow map onto the cut locus, its collapse is the Thom space
\cite{BP}, and its uniform-capture instances are the Ford--Voronoi
and crystallographic spines (Corollary~\ref{cor:spines},
Proposition~\ref{prop:thom}).
